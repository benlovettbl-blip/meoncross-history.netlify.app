1066 – 1381

Medieval England

The Struggle for Power: Monarchy, Church & Popular Rebellion

Volume Overview & Curriculum Scope From the catastrophic clash of Saxon shield wall and Norman cavalry on Senlac Ridge in 1066 to the armed peasant revolt marching on London in 1381, this reading anthology charts three turbulent centuries in which monarchical authority, spiritual hegemony, and popular resistance forged the medieval English realm.

Curriculum Enquiries in This Volume

Chronological Syllabus Overview
01
1066: The Succession Crisis & The Battle of Hastings pp. 2–3 • Ref [1.1]–[1.5]
Key Question: Why did three men claim one throne in 1066, and how did William secure victory at Hastings?
The Witan's dilemma, Harold Godwinson's coronation, Viking invasion at Stamford Bridge, and the tactical turning point on Senlac Ridge.
02
Castles, Terror & Domesday: How Did the Normans Subjugate England? pp. 4–5 • Ref [2.1]–[2.5]
Key Question: How did William the Conqueror crush English rebellion and consolidate total monarchical control by 1086?
Motte-and-bailey blitz, the Harrying of the North, Portchester Castle keep, the Domesday survey, and Saxon land dispossession.
03
Crown vs Church: Why Did Henry II Clash With Thomas Becket? pp. 6–7 • Ref [3.1]–[3.5]
Key Question: Was the murder of Thomas Becket a defense of royal law or a catastrophic abuse of monarchical tyranny?
Criminous clerks, Constitutions of Clarendon, six years of papal exile, the fatal Christmas rage, and Canterbury martyrdom.
04
Magna Carta (1215) & Medieval Kingship: Liberty or Barons’ Power Grab? pp. 8–9 • Ref [4.1]–[4.5]
Key Question: Did Magna Carta establish universal rule of law or was it a self-interested treaty protecting aristocratic privilege?
King John 'Softsword', fiscal extortion, the Papal Interdict, catastrophe at Bouvines, Runnymede, and Clause 39 liberties.
05
The Black Death (1348) & The Peasants’ Revolt (1381) pp. 10–11 • Ref [5.1]–[5.5]
Key Question: Did demographic catastrophe and popular rebellion permanently shatter feudal serfdom in England?
Demographic collapse, Statute of Labourers wage freeze, radical hedge-priests, the sack of London, and the Smithfield climax.
Core Concepts Monarchical Sovereignty • Feudal Land Tenure • Secular vs Canon Law • Constitutional Rule of Law • Demographic Catastrophe & Labour Mobility
