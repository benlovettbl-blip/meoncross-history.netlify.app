### Overarching Curricular Enquiry:
> **"How did oceanic trade, religious fragmentation, and constitutional revolution forge the Early Modern World (1453–1750)?"**

This Year 8 enquiry investigates the profound transformations that linked England with the wider world between the Ottoman capture of Constantinople in 1453 and the mid-eighteenth century. Grounded in vertical curriculum coherence, pupils explore the collapse of medieval certainties, the emergence of oceanic trade networks, the brutality of transatlantic enslavement, indigenous resistance, and the violent domestic struggle over sovereignty that executed an anointed king and created a fiscal-military parliamentary state.

### Scheme of Work Document Key & Historical Figure Taxonomy

To ensure balanced classroom parity between top-down political state actors and bottom-up collective social movements, our Key Stage 3 curriculum enforces a rigorous bipartite taxonomy:

* **`KEY HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`**: Specific named historical figures, monarchs, statesmen, chroniclers, and radical leaders who exercised executive authority, recorded firsthand witness testimony, or led decisive political actions. Rendered in textbook spreads with authenticated primary portrait miniatures, lifespan markers, and decisive actions.
* **`REPRESENTATIVE HISTORICAL VOICE`**: Composite historical archetypes representing broader social classes, labour forces, and collective resistance movements (e.g. *The New Model Army Agitator*, *The Enclosure Freeholder*). Rendered in textbook spreads as clean, full-width typographical feature cards without artificial stock imagery, giving equal academic dignity and analytical focus to ordinary working people.

## Detailed Enquiry Breakdown

### Enquiry 1: Constantinople 1453 & The Spice Routes: Why Did Europeans Sail West?
* **Enquiry Question:** *Why did the Ottoman capture of Constantinople in 1453 force European monarchs to look across the Atlantic?*
* **Historical Core:** The Byzantine bastion of Constantinople falls to Sultan Mehmed II’s siege artillery on 29 May 1453; closure and taxing of traditional Silk Road and Levant spice routes; Venetian and Genoese trading monopolies; Portuguese exploration down the West African coast under Prince Henry the Navigator; Mansa Musa and the myth of African gold; Columbus’s 1492 transatlantic voyage seeking an alternative westerly route to the East Indies.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Causation & Consequence / Geopolitics* — Understanding that European oceanic exploration was born out of weakness and geopolitical encirclement, not strength.
* **Key Historical Actor:** Sultan Mehmed II (`KEY HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`).

---

### Enquiry 2: The Age of Discovery & Spanish Silver: How Did Silver Transform Global Power?
* **Enquiry Question:** *How did the discovery of American silver reshape European warfare and provoke English privateering?*
* **Historical Core:** The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) divides the globe between Spain and Portugal; Spanish conquest of the Aztec and Inca empires; the Cerro Rico silver mountain of Potosí; massive bullion flows into Habsburg Spain; inflation and economic destabilization across Europe; English Protestant privateers ("Sea Dogs" like Sir Francis Drake and John Hawkins) plundering Spanish treasure galleons with Queen Elizabeth’s covert blessing; the showdown of the 1588 Spanish Armada.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Change & Continuity / Economic Causation* — Examining how American precious metals turned Spain into a military superpower while financing European religious wars.
* **Key Historical Actor:** Sir Francis Drake (`KEY HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`).

---

### Enquiry 3: The Kingdom of Benin & West African Trade: What Was Africa Like Before the Slave Trade?
* **Enquiry Question:** *What does the Kingdom of Benin reveal about the sophistication of West African states before European colonization?*
* **Historical Core:** The flourishing Edo civilisation of the Kingdom of Benin (modern Nigeria); the divine kingship of the Oba; the magnificent moats and defensive earthworks of Benin City; metallurgy excellence: the lost-wax casting of the world-renowned Benin Bronzes; Portuguese and Dutch trade dispatches describing a clean, orderly city with zero beggars; early trade based on mutual diplomatic respect: textiles, ivory, and pepper exchanged for European brass and copper manillas.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Historical Diversity & Pre-Colonial Sovereignty* — Challenging Eurocentric myths that pre-colonial Africa was primitive or uncivilized.
* **Key Historical Actor:** Oba Ewuare II of Benin (`KEY HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`).

---

### Enquiry 4: The Gunpowder Plot (1605): Why Did Catholic Conspirators Seek to Blow Up Parliament?
* **Enquiry Question:** *Was the Gunpowder Plot a desperate act of religious resistance or an inevitable reaction to state persecution?*
* **Historical Core:** The Elizabethan religious settlement and anti-Catholic penal laws (fines for recusancy, hanging and drawing of Jesuit priests); the succession of James VI of Scotland as James I (1603); dashed Catholic hopes for toleration; Robert Catesby gathers a cell of radical gentlemen (including Guy Fawkes); thirty-six barrels of gunpowder hidden under the House of Lords; the anonymous Monteagle warning letter; the arrest of Fawkes on the night of 4–5 November 1605; torture in the Tower; execution of conspirators and subsequent anti-Catholic hysteria.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Evidence & Forensic Interrogation* — Interrogating state surveillance and confessions extracted under torture.
* **Key Historical Actor:** Robert Catesby (`KEY HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`).

---

### Enquiry 5: The Civil War & The Trial of Charles I: Why Did England Execute Its King?
* **Enquiry Question:** *Why did the struggle between King and Parliament lead to the unprecedented trial and public execution of Charles I in 1649?*
* **Historical Core:** Charles I and the Divine Right of Kings; eleven years of Personal Rule (1629–1640) and arbitrary Ship Money taxes; the Bishop’s Wars and the Long Parliament; outbreak of civil war in 1642; Oliver Cromwell and the creation of the disciplined New Model Army; the radicalization of ordinary soldiers: the 1647 Putney Debates (*"The poorest he that is in England hath a life to live as the greatest he"*); Pride’s Purge and the Rump Parliament; the trial of Charles I in Westminster Hall; the historic regicide on 30 January 1649.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Significance & Constitutional Transformation* — Tracing the radical ideological shift from Divine Right to Parliamentary sovereignty.
* **Key Historical Voice:** The New Model Army Agitator (`REPRESENTATIVE HISTORICAL VOICE`).

---

### Enquiry 6: The Commonwealth & Navigation Acts: How Did Cromwell Build an Imperial Navy?
* **Enquiry Question:** *How did the English Republic lay the commercial and naval foundations of the British Empire?*
* **Historical Core:** The establishment of the Commonwealth under Oliver Cromwell; the 1651 Navigation Act requiring all goods entering England to be carried on English ships, targeting Dutch merchant dominance; Anglo-Dutch naval wars; the "Western Design" (1655): naval expedition seizing Jamaica from Spain as a permanent Caribbean sugar base; state militarization: expanding the Royal Navy from 30 to over 150 warships; the emergence of mercantilism as official state doctrine.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Causation & Imperial Commercial Strategy* — Analysing how state naval power was deployed to monopolize global commercial routes.
* **Key Historical Actor:** Oliver Cromwell (`KEY HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`).

---

### Enquiry 7: The Transatlantic Slave Trade: How Did Chattel Slavery Power British Prosperity?
* **Enquiry Question:** *How did the triangular slave trade operate, and why was it described as a machine for turning human lives into capital?*
* **Historical Core:** The triangular trade network (manufactured goods from Britain to West Africa, enslaved Africans across the Middle Passage to the Americas, raw sugar/tobacco/cotton back to Britain); the dehumanizing horrors of the slave ship hold (the Brookes cross-section); physical brutality of plantation enslavement in the Caribbean and American colonies; immense profits flowing to British ports (Bristol, Liverpool, London) and financing domestic banks, canals, and early factories; eyewitness exposure by ship surgeon Alexander Falconbridge.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Historical Evidence & Forensic Extraction* — Interrogating financial ledgers and architectural diagrams to understand commodification.
* **Key Historical Actor:** Alexander Falconbridge (`KEY HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`).

---

### Enquiry 8: Resistance, Maroons & Abolition: How Was Enslavement Contested and Overturned?
* **Enquiry Question:** *To what extent was the transatlantic slave trade ended by enslaved resistance rather than British parliamentary reformers?*
* **Historical Core:** Daily resistance on plantations (sabotage, tool breaking, preserving African cultural traditions); armed revolts across the Caribbean; Queen Nanny and the Jamaican Maroons: guerrilla mountain warfare forcing the British army to sign a peace treaty recognizing Maroon sovereignty in 1739; the emergence of the British abolitionist movement; Olaudah Equiano’s bestselling autobiography; Thomas Clarkson and William Wilberforce; the 1807 Abolition of the Slave Trade Act.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Historical Agency & Interpretations* — Contrasting top-down parliamentary legislation with bottom-up African armed and cultural resistance.
* **Key Historical Actor:** Queen Nanny of the Maroons (`KEY HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`).

---

### Capstone Enquiry (Page 20 Synthesis): Enclosure, The Bank of England & The Fiscal-Military State
* **Enquiry Question:** *“Britain became a global superpower because of its financial and parliamentary institutions, not merely its military bravery.” How far do you agree?*
* **Historical Core:** The Glorious Revolution of 1688 and the 1689 Bill of Rights; the foundation of the Bank of England (1694) and the creation of the National Debt; institutional borrowing at low interest rates enabling continuous naval expansion; the parliamentary Enclosure Acts: dispossessing independent yeomen and commoners, commercializing agriculture, and generating an urban wage-labour force; Daniel Defoe’s social survey of burgeoning British commerce.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Synoptic Evaluation & Socioeconomic Analysis* — Synthesizing agrarian transformation, institutional finance, and imperial expansion.
* **Key Historical Voice:** The Enclosure Freeholder (`REPRESENTATIVE HISTORICAL VOICE`).
