Curriculum Roadmap & Chronological Spine

1450 – 1750

Trace the 300-year transformation of early modern power: from the fall of Constantinople in 1453 to global oceanic rivalries, civil war regicide, financial revolution, and resistance to Atlantic slavery.

1453
Fall of Constantinople & Rise of Ottoman Hegemony
Sultan Mehmed II breaches Byzantine walls; Ottoman Empire takes control of Silk Road spice trade, forcing European crowns to search for oceanic routes.
1494
Treaty of Tordesillas & Atlantic Oceanic Rivalry
Pope Alexander VI divides the non-European world between Catholic Spain and Portugal, sparking fierce Protestant pushback from English and Dutch privateers.
1588
Defeat of the Spanish Armada & English Expansion
Elizabeth I’s navy repels King Philip II’s invasion fleet; England establishes global privateering, chartered trading companies, and early American colonies.
1605
Gunpowder Plot & Jacobean Religious Volatility
Catholic conspirators lead by Robert Catesby attempt to blow up King James I and Parliament; state responds with intense anti-popery legislation and surveillance.
1649
Execution of King Charles I & The Commonwealth
English Civil War concludes in unprecedented regicide; monarchy and House of Lords abolished as Oliver Cromwell establishes the Puritan Commonwealth.
1688
Glorious Revolution & The 1689 Bill of Rights
James II deposed; William III and Mary II sign the Bill of Rights, establishing parliamentary supremacy and religious limits on the British Crown.
1694
Founding of the Bank of England & National Debt
Financial Revolution creates modern public credit; British fiscal-military state funds global naval supremacy, colonial garrisons, and overseas empire.
1739
Jamaican Maroon Treaty: Sovereign African Resistance
Queen Nanny and Jamaican Maroons defeat British forces in the Blue Mountains, forcing the Crown to sign a treaty recognizing Maroon land sovereignty.

Edexcel Paper 1 & 3 Prep
Source A: Papal Bull Inter Caetera & Treaty of Tordesillas (1493–1494)

“We assign to you, Catholic Kings of Castile and Leon, all islands and mainlands discovered westward of the meridian line. We strictly command you to lead the inhabitants to embrace the Christian faith, so that the Holy Roman Catholic Church may be exalted and barbarous nations brought to the salvation of Christ.”

Record: ARCHIVO GENERAL DE INDIAS • SEVILLE • PATRONATO 1
Provenance Clue: Official papal decree by Pope Alexander VI; reveals how Spanish imperial claims were justified by divine mandate and religious zeal.
Source B: Richard Hakluyt, Discourse on Western Planting (1584)

“This western enterprise will yield great profit unto our realm: providing naval timber and pitch, venting our woollen cloth, and employing our idle poor. Crucially, it will check the pride of King Philip of Spain, whose gold mines in the West Indies feed his war against Protestant England.”

Record: BRITISH LIBRARY • LONDON • COTTON MS TITUS B.XIII
Provenance Clue: Secret strategic briefing for Queen Elizabeth I; emphasizes commercial profit, domestic unemployment, and geopolitical rivalry with Catholic Spain.

2. PROVENANCE & MOTIVE Evaluate how the authorship and purpose of each document (papal decree vs secret royal briefing) affects its historical reliability.
3. HISTORICAL JUDGEMENT Reach a reasoned conclusion: which source is more useful for understanding why European nations took to the Atlantic Ocean?

Task 4: Historical Skill: Historical Interpretations • Historiographical Debate

Enquiry: How far do you agree with Interpretation 1 that the English Civil War was an inevitable constitutional battle for parliamentary liberty?

Edexcel Paper 3 Prep
Interpretation 1: Lord Macaulay & G.M. Trevelyan, The Whig Orthodoxy (1848 / 1926)
The Whig View: Inevitable March of Parliamentary Liberty

“The clash between King and Parliament was the grand heroic struggle of English history. Charles I was an unbending tyrant who sought to extinguish ancient liberties through illegal taxes like Ship Money and eleven years of Personal Rule. The Civil War was the inevitable, necessary triumph of parliamentary freedom, rule of law, and Protestant democracy over royal absolutism.”

Scholar: Lord Macaulay (Whig Historian & Statesman)
Interpretation 2: Professor Conrad Russell & John Morrill, The Revisionist View (1990 / 1993)
The Revisionist View: An Unforced Accident & War of Religion

“The Civil War was neither inevitable nor primarily about modern constitutional liberty. It was a tragic, unforced breakdown caused by the personal untrustworthiness of Charles I and explosive religious fear. In 1640, nobody wanted war; England stumbled into disaster because the King failed to govern three distinct kingdoms—England, Scotland, and Ireland—simultaneously.”

Scholar: Conrad Russell (Professor of Commonwealth History, King's College London)
1. INTERPRETATION 1 CLAIMS Analyse Macaulay’s Whig argument that tyranny and Ship Money made parliamentary conflict inevitable.
2. INTERPRETATION 2 CRITIQUE Evaluate Russell’s Revisionist counter-argument that war was an accidental crisis of religion and personal mistrust.
3. HISTORIOGRAPHICAL VERDICT Weigh both interpretations against factual evidence (1642 Militia Ordinance, Bishops' Wars) to reach an independent verdict.

The 4 Golden Rules of Historical Extended Writing:
  1. Direct Answer: Open every paragraph with a clear thematic point that directly answers the enquiry question.
  2. Specific Evidence: Ground every argument in precise historical facts, figures, names, and contemporary legislation.
  3. Causal Connectives: Never just describe events; explain the mechanism of how and why one event caused or accelerated another.
  4. Evaluative Judgement: Weigh competing arguments against explicit historical criteria to reach a nuanced, independent conclusion.
