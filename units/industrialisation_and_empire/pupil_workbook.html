Departmental PEEL Disciplinary Writing Standards:

• [P] Point (Clear Enquiry Claim): An authoritative, precise opening thesis statement directly answering the historical enquiry question.
• [E] Evidence (Archival Data & Corroboration): Specific, named historical evidence (figures, dates, statistics, legislation) drawn directly from the facing evidence page.
• [E] Explanation (Causal & Analytical Reasoning): In-depth historical reasoning using disciplinary connectives to explain how and why this factor was significant, transformative, or consequential.
• [L] Link (Evaluative Verdict): A substantiated conclusion directly linking arguments back to the enquiry question and reaching a justified historical judgement.

Curriculum Roadmap & Chronological Spine

1750 – 1900

Follow Britain's dual transformation: the domestic industrial revolution at home and the expansion of imperial power abroad.

1769–1784
The Energy & Materials Revolution Watt patents separate steam condenser (1769); Cort patents puddling & rolling at Funtley (1783-84).
1780–1830
The Rise of the Factory System Water frames and steam looms concentrate workers into textile cities; domestic system collapses.
1815–1819
Post-Napoleonic Distress & Peterloo Corn Laws spark starvation; 60,000 peaceful reformers charged by cavalry at St Peter’s Field (1819).
1832–1848
The Struggle for Parliamentary Voice Great Reform Act enfranchises middle class (1832); Chartists present million-signature petitions (1838–48).
1842–1858
Urban Crisis & Public Health Reform Chadwick exposes slum death rates (1842); King Cholera epidemics lead to the Great Stink & Bazalgette (1858).
1857–1858
The Indian Rebellion & Crown Rule Uprising in Meerut shatters East India Company control; Queen Victoria assumes direct Crown sovereignty (1858).
1870–1900
High Imperialism & The Global Balance Sheet Scramble for Africa; Victorian cultural propaganda contrasts against domestic working-class inequality.
Hampshire Local History Connection:

Our curriculum directly anchors national events into our local Hampshire landscape: from Henry Cort’s surviving puddling weir and slag wall at Funtley, to Fareham Red brick clay pits, and the Portsmouth Royal Navy Dockyard industrial complex.

  • James Watt (1736–1819): Patented the separate condenser steam engine (1769).
  • Henry Cort (1741–1800): Fareham ironmaster who invented puddling & rolling at Funtley (1783–84).
  • Isambard Kingdom Brunel (1806–1859): Engineer of Great Western Railway & SS Great Britain.
  • Edwin Chadwick (1800–1890): Author of landmark 1842 Sanitary Report.
  • Lord Shaftesbury (1801–1885): Parliamentary champion of Ten Hours Act & Coal Mines Act.
  • Henry Hunt (1773–1835): Radical orator at the 1819 St Peter’s Field rally (Peterloo).
  • Earl Grey (1764–1845): Whig Prime Minister who steered 1832 Great Reform Act.

The 6 GCSE Disciplinary Command Words

1. Causal Weighting: Prioritising multiple causes into triggers vs underlying pre-conditions.

2. Significance: Judging historical importance using Scale, Duration, and Transformation.

3. Change & Continuity: Distinguishing between radical turning points and persistent continuities.

4. Historiographical Debate: Weighing competing academic interpretations with evidence.

5. Analytical Narrative: Tracing causal links across an evolving sequence of events without story-telling.

6. Source Utility: Interrogating Content, Context, and Provenance (NOP: Nature, Origin, Purpose).
