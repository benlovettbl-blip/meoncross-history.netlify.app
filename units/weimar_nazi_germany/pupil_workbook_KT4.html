JUN 1933 • Law for the Encouragement of Marriage Key Topic 4.1

The Nazi state offers marriage loans of up to 1,000 marks to newlywed Aryan couples, provided the bride gives up her job. For each child born, 25% of the loan is permanently cancelled, boosting the national birth rate.

15 SEP 1935 • Enactment of the Nuremberg Racial Laws Key Topic 4.4

Announced at the Nuremberg Rally, the Reich Citizenship Law strips German Jews of citizenship and voting rights. The Law for the Protection of German Blood and Honour makes marriage and sexual relations between Jews and Aryans illegal.

MAR 1935 • Introduction of Military Conscription & Rearmament Key Topic 4.3

Hitler openly renounces the Versailles disarmament clauses, announcing a peacetime army of 550,000 men and the creation of the Luftwaffe. Massive military rearmament absorbs hundreds of thousands of unemployed German workers.

DEC 1936 • First Hitler Youth Law Passed Key Topic 4.2

All non-Nazi youth organisations (Catholic and sporting clubs) are banned or absorbed. Membership of the Hitlerjugend becomes effectively expected for all children aged 10–18, and is made strictly compulsory in March 1939.

9–10 NOV 1938 • Kristallnacht (The Night of Broken Glass) Key Topic 4.4

Following the assassination of diplomat Ernst vom Rath in Paris, Goebbels unleashes nationwide anti-Jewish pogroms. SA and SS mobs burn over 1,000 synagogues, destroy 7,500 Jewish businesses, murder 91 Jews, and send 30,000 Jewish men to concentration camps.

1 SEP 1939 • Invasion of Poland & Outbreak of War Key Topic 4.3

Germany invades Poland, triggering World War Two in Europe. The four-year drive for economic autarky (self-sufficiency) shifts into total war mobilization, and persecution of minorities escalates toward mass deportation and murder.

Archival & Conceptual Blueprint • The Nazi Social Machine: Youth, Women & Racial Persecution

1. The Nazi Social Matrix: Indoctrination of Youth and Domesticity of Women
👦 The Hitlerjugend (Ages 10–18): Military training, ideological drills, physical fitness, map-reading, rifle shooting. Made compulsory in 1939 to produce hardened future soldiers for the Wehrmacht.
👧 The League of German Girls (BDM): Domestic preparation, childcare, eugenics, physical gymnastics. Trained young girls to become obedient Aryan mothers to reverse the birth rate decline.
👩 Women & Motherhood (Kinder, Küche, Kirche): Law for Encouragement of Marriage gave 1,000-mark loans. Mother’s Cross awarded on Hitler’s mother’s birthday. 1937 labour shortage forced millions back into rearmament factories.
2. The Escalating Ladder of Anti-Semitic Persecution (1933–1938)
1933: ECONOMIC BOYCOTT 1 April 1933: Nationwide SA boycott of Jewish shops, lawyers, and doctors. Law for Restoration of the Professional Civil Service sacks all Jewish civil servants and university professors.
1935: THE NUREMBERG LAWS Reich Citizenship Law strips German Jews of civil and voting rights, reducing them to state "subjects". Law for Protection of German Blood and Honour bans intermarriage and sexual relations with Aryans.
1938: KRISTALLNACHT POGROM 9–10 Nov 1938: Goebbels coordinates nationwide SA/SS violence. Over 1,000 synagogues burned, 7,500 shops looted, 91 Jews killed, 30,000 men sent to camps, and a 1 billion mark fine imposed.

Master Knowledge Organiser • Key Topic 4: Life in Nazi Germany, 1933–39

1. Core Disciplinary Vocabulary & Conceptual Definitions
• Kinder, Küche, Kirche: Children, Kitchen, Church: the traditionalist Nazi slogan dictating that German women’s lives belong in the home.
• The Mother’s Cross: Medal awarded to honour prolific Aryan mothers (Bronze for 4, Silver for 6, Gold for 8 or more children).
• The Hitler Youth (Hitlerjugend): Compulsory youth movement for boys aged 10–18, providing military training and ideological indoctrination.
• League of German Girls (BDM): Female youth organisation training girls in domesticity, fitness, and racial health to become future mothers.
• National Labour Service (RAD): Compulsory 6-month manual labour service for young men aged 18–25, used for public works and Autobahns.
• Strength Through Joy (KdF): DAF division providing subsidized holidays, theatre tickets, and cruises to keep workers happy and productive.
• The Nuremberg Laws (1935): Racial laws stripping German Jews of citizenship and outlawing marriage and sexual relations with Aryans.
• Kristallnacht (Nov 1938): Night of Broken Glass: violent nationwide pogrom destroying 1,000 synagogues and sending 30,000 Jews to camps.
2. Essential Chronology
• 1 Apr 1933: One-day nationwide SA boycott of Jewish shops
• Jun 1933: Law for Encouragement of Marriage introduces loans
• Mar 1935: Military conscription reintroduced, boosting employment
• 15 Sep 1935: Nuremberg Laws strip Jews of German citizenship
• Dec 1936: First Hitler Youth Law absorbs rival organisations
• 1937: Labour shortages force regime to recruit women into factories
• Aug 1938: Jewish men forced to add "Israel", women "Sara" to names
• 9–10 Nov 1938: Kristallnacht pogrom unleashes nationwide terror
• 15 Nov 1938: Jewish children expelled from all German state schools
• Mar 1939: Hitler Youth membership made strictly compulsory for all boys
3. Key Historical Figures Matrix
• Gertrud Scholtz-Klink: Reich Women’s Leader; headed the Nazi Women’s League to enforce domestic ideology and motherhood.
• Baldur von Schirach: Reich Youth Leader; oversaw the expansion and compulsory militarisation of the Hitler Youth.
• Bernhard Rust: Minister of Education; Nazified school curricula, history books, and purged Jewish teachers.
• Dr Robert Ley: Head of the German Labour Front (DAF); controlled workers, banned strikes, and ran Strength Through Joy (KdF).
• Hermann Goering: Plenipotentiary for the Four-Year Plan (1936); directed economic autarky and rearmament for war.
• Hjalmar Schacht: President of Reichsbank and Economics Minister; devised the New Plan and MEFO bills to finance early recovery.

Grade 9 Exam Technique Masterclass • Pearson Edexcel Paper 3 Rubrics

1. The 4/4 Mark Formula: Inference from Source A [4 Marks • 5 Mins] TARGET: 4 / 4
Mark Scheme Law: Two separate inferences required. Each inference must state: (1) what you can deduce beyond surface description + (2) a verbatim quote or precise detail from Source A supporting your deduction. Never quote without explaining what it means!
2. The 12/12 Mark Formula: Explain Why [12 Marks • 18 Mins] TARGET: 11–12 / 12 (LEVEL 4)
Three Linked PEEL Paragraphs: Use both provided stimulus bullet points + bring in ONE distinct self-selected knowledge point. Every paragraph must feature causal links: "Consequently, As a direct consequence, This meant that..." Connect your factors: explain how economic crisis enabled political radicalism!
3. The 8/8 Mark Formula: How Useful are Sources B & C [8 Marks • 12 Mins] TARGET: 7–8 / 8 (LEVEL 3)
COP Formula (Content, Origin, Purpose): Evaluate Source B then Source C. Interrogate content using own knowledge; evaluate provenance (who wrote it, when, why, and who was the intended audience). Never dismiss a source as "biased"; explain how bias makes it useful as evidence of propaganda or fear!
4. The 16/16 Mark Formula: Evaluative Essay [16 Marks + 4 SPaG • 25 Mins] TARGET: 15–16 / 16 (LEVEL 4)
Criteria-Led Judgment Architecture:
• Opening: State your sustained thesis and establish your evaluative criteria (e.g. legal illusion vs daily social reality).
• Section 1: Support the targeted Interpretation using its quotes and your own historical evidence.
• Section 2: Evaluate the competing Interpretation using counter-evidence from your own knowledge.
• Conclusion: Deliver a justified verdict explaining why one historian’s emphasis is more persuasive.
