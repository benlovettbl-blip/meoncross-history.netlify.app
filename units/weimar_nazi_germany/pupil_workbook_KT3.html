27 FEB 1933 • The Reichstag Fire & Emergency Powers Decree Key Topic 3.1

The Reichstag building in Berlin is destroyed by arson; Dutch Communist Marinus van der Lubbe is arrested. Hitler uses the fire to persuade Hindenburg to sign the Reichstag Fire Decree, suspending freedom of speech, assembly, and press.

23 MAR 1933 • Passage of the Enabling Act (Dictatorship Established) Key Topic 3.1

Surrounded by armed SS and SA stormtroopers chanting intimidation at the Kroll Opera House, the Reichstag votes 444 to 94 to pass the Enabling Act. Hitler gains total legal power to enact laws without parliament or president for four years.

30 JUN 1934 • The Night of the Long Knives (SA Purge) Key Topic 3.1

Fearing the 2-million-strong SA under Ernst Röhm wanted a "second revolution", Hitler unleashes SS execution squads. Over 400 political rivals, including Röhm, Gregor Strasser, and former Chancellor Schleicher, are murdered without trial.

2/20 "27 Feb 1933: Reichstag Fire gave Hitler the pretext to crush civil liberties overnight."

2 AUG 1934 • Death of Hindenburg & The Armed Forces Oath Key Topic 3.1

President Hindenburg dies aged 86. Hitler merges the offices of Chancellor and President, assuming the supreme title of Führer und Reichskanzler. Every officer and soldier in the German armed forces swears an unconditional oath of loyalty to Hitler personally.

JUL 1933 • The Reich Concordat with the Vatican Key Topic 3.2

Hitler signs an agreement with Pope Pius XI: the Catholic Church promises to stay out of German politics, while the Nazi regime guarantees religious freedom and Catholic schools. Within months, the Nazis begin systematically harassing Catholic youth groups and priests.

AUG 1936 • The Berlin Olympic Games Propaganda Showcase Key Topic 3.3

Germany hosts the 11th Olympic Games in Berlin, showcasing Nazi modern efficiency to 49 nations. Anti-Semitic signs are temporarily removed, but African-American sprinter Jesse Owens demolishes Aryan racial theories by winning four gold medals.

SOURCE B
Speech by Propaganda Minister Joseph Goebbels to Radio Directors, Berlin, March 1933
From an official address given by Dr. Joseph Goebbels to regional broadcasting directors in Berlin, March 1933, shortly after his appointment as Minister of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda.
“We make no bones about it: the radio belongs to us and to no one else! We will place radio in the service of our National Socialist idea, and no other idea shall be expressed here. Radio must be purged of all un-German elements. We intend to mobilize the minds of the entire nation so thoroughly that no foreign or hostile thought can find an audience.”
SOURCE C
Contemporary photograph of the People’s Radio (Volksempfänger VE301), Berlin, 1933
From an official photograph published in Germany, 1933. It shows the newly introduced Volksempfänger (People’s Radio), commissioned by Joseph Goebbels.
The Volksempfänger was mass-produced to sell for only 76 Reichsmarks, roughly one week’s wage for an average industrial worker. By 1939, over seventy percent of German households owned a radio set—the highest proportion in Europe. The sets were engineered with a limited shortwave receiver so that ordinary citizens could not easily listen to foreign broadcasts, ensuring that families heard only Nazi propaganda and Hitler’s speeches.

HISTORICAL INTERPRETATIONS 1 AND 2 Target: How and why historians differ (AO4)
INTERPRETATION 1
From an account by historian David Welch, The Third Reich: Politics and Propaganda (2002)
“Nazi propaganda was remarkably successful in winning over the hearts and minds of the German people. Joseph Goebbels understood that propaganda could not rely solely on crude slogans; it had to appeal to existing national traditions and pride. Through the distribution of cheap ‘People’s Radios’, the cinematic spectacles of Leni Riefenstahl, and the euphoric pageantry of the Nuremberg rallies, the regime created a genuine sense of national community (Volksgemeinschaft). For the majority of Germans between 1933 and 1939, conformity was based on genuine enthusiasm and admiration for Hitler rather than state coercion.”
INTERPRETATION 2
From an account by historian Richard J. Evans, The Third Reich in Power (2005)
“The apparent unity of the German people was fundamentally an illusion created by the omnipresence of the terror state. While Goebbels’ propaganda was loud and relentless, ordinary Germans were far more cynical and detached than official rallies suggested. What truly controlled attitudes was the constant threat of denunciation to the Gestapo, the pervasive network of SS surveillance, and the dread of protective custody in concentration camps like Dachau. Germans did not conform because they believed every word broadcast on the radio; they conformed because speaking out meant arrest, torture, and ruin.”
