### Overarching Curricular Enquiry:
> **"How was power won, maintained, contested, and transformed in Medieval England (1066–1485)?"**

This Year 7 enquiry investigates the delicate and volatile balance of medieval power between the **Crown**, the **Catholic Church**, the **Barons**, and the **Peasant Commons**. Grounded in Christine Counsell’s principles of vertical curriculum coherence, this unit introduces Year 7 pupils to rigorous historical thinking: interrogating medieval monastic chronicles, analyzing visual material culture (Bayeux Tapestry, parish Doom frescoes), understanding feudal socioeconomic structures, and evaluating the long-term institutional shifts that ultimately birthed the early modern state.

## Detailed Enquiry Breakdown

### Enquiry 1: 1066 & The Battle of Hastings: Why Did William Win?
* **Enquiry Question:** *Why did William of Normandy win the Battle of Hastings in 1066?*
* **Historical Core:** Edward the Confessor’s death without an heir; the four claimants (Harold Godwinson, William of Normandy, Harald Hardrada, Edgar the Atheling); Stamford Bridge and the 200-mile forced march; the collision on Senlac Hill (14 October 1066); Norman cavalry and archers vs Anglo-Saxon housecarl shield wall; the tactical turning point of the feigned retreat.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Change & Continuity / Causal Interaction* — Evaluating whether Norman military superiority, Saxon exhaustion, or battlefield chance was the decisive factor.
* **Task 1 Vocabulary Practice:** *Dual-Term Analytical Distinction* (Housecarl vs Fyrd).
* **Task 2 Archival Interrogation:** William of Poitiers vs Bayeux Tapestry excerpt.
* **Extended Writing Focus:** Sustained causal balance weighing Norman tactics against Saxon battlefield vulnerabilities.

---

### Enquiry 2: Castles, Terror & Domesday: How Did William Control England?
* **Enquiry Question:** *How did William the Conqueror establish and maintain control over a hostile Anglo-Saxon population?*
* **Historical Core:** The defensive challenge of a minority Norman garrison (c. 7,000 Normans ruling 2 million Saxons); rapid motte-and-bailey timber castle construction; regional rebellions (Hereward in Ely, Edgar in York); the brutal Harrying of the North (1069–1070) and resultant famine; the Domesday Book (1086) as an unprecedented fiscal and military inventory of national wealth; local Hampshire examples (Portchester Castle keep and Fareham hundred entries).
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Dual-Source Utility* — Cross-referencing monastic chronicles of northern destruction with the cold bureaucratic audit of Domesday.
* **Task 1 Vocabulary Practice:** *Contextual Cloze* (Motte-and-bailey, castellan, Harrying, subjection, Domesday).
* **Task 2 Archival Interrogation:** Orderic Vitalis (*Ecclesiastical History*) on the scorched-earth devastation.
* **Extended Writing Focus:** Edexcel-aligned Dual-Source Utility evaluating content, provenance, and historical context.

---

### Enquiry 3: Crown vs Church: Why Did Henry II Clash with Thomas Becket?
* **Enquiry Question:** *Why did the dispute between Henry II and Thomas Becket escalate into cathedral murder?*
* **Historical Core:** The dual legal system of medieval England (King’s courts vs Ecclesiastical courts); "benefit of clergy" and lenient sentences for criminous clerks; Henry II appoints his loyal Chancellor Thomas Becket as Archbishop of Canterbury (1162); Becket’s dramatic spiritual transformation into champion of Church autonomy; the 1164 Constitutions of Clarendon; Becket’s six-year exile; coronation of the Young King; Becket’s excommunications; the fateful royal outburst and the four knights’ murder in Canterbury Cathedral (29 December 1170); Henry’s 1174 public whipping penance.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Causation & Transformation* — Unpicking structural institutional friction (secular vs canon law) from personal clash of loyalties.
* **Task 1 Vocabulary Practice:** *Vocabulary Mapping* (Benefit of Clergy, Excommunication, Martyrdom).
* **Task 2 Archival Interrogation:** Clause 3 of Constitutions of Clarendon vs Edward Grim eyewitness testimony.
* **Extended Writing Focus:** 3-tier causal narrative evaluating why compromise became impossible.

---

### Enquiry 4: Magna Carta (1215): Liberty or Barons’ Power Grab?
* **Enquiry Question:** *To what extent was Magna Carta (1215) a selfish baronial power grab rather than a charter of liberty?*
* **Historical Core:** King John’s disastrous military failures in Normandy (Bouvines, 1214); exorbitant taxation (scutage, wardship fines, arbitrary exactions); dispute with Pope Innocent III and the Interdict (1208–1213); the baronial rebellion and capture of London; the negotiations at Runnymede (June 1215); key clauses: Clause 12 (no scutage without common counsel), Clause 39 (trial by peers and law of the land), Clause 40 (justice not sold or delayed), Clause 61 (security clause allowing 25 barons to seize royal property); papal annulment and the First Barons’ War; the reissues under Henry III.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Historical Significance & Anachronism* — Distinguishing 13th-century baronial self-interest from modern constitutional mythology.
* **Task 1 Vocabulary Practice:** *Dual-Term Analytical Distinction* (Scutage vs Feudal Relief).
* **Task 2 Archival Interrogation:** Magna Carta Clause 39 vs Pope Innocent III’s Bull of Annulment.
* **Extended Writing Focus:** Historiographical debate weighing baronial oligopoly against the emergence of the Rule of Law.

---

### Enquiry 5: Doom Paintings, Tithes & Village Life: What Was Life Like for Medieval Peasants?
* **Enquiry Question:** *How far did the Catholic Church and the manor court maintain total control over medieval peasants?*
* **Historical Core:** The Three Estates of medieval society (*bellatores*, *oratores*, *laboratores*); the manorial system: demesne land, strip farming, open-field rotation; the onerous burdens of the serf/villein: week-work, boon-work, merchet, heriot, and the parish tithe (one-tenth of all grain and livestock); the parish church as the physical and psychological centre of village existence; visual theology: church Doom wall paintings illustrating the Last Judgment, weighing of souls by St Michael, and eternal torment in the Hellmouth; local Hampshire case: Bishop’s Waltham Palace and the episcopal estates.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Historiographical Debate & Social Structure* — Contrasting the "feudal oppression" model with modern social history revealing peasant communal resilience and court negotiation.
* **Task 1 Vocabulary Practice:** *Contextual Cloze* (Villein, Tithe, Demesne, Hellmouth, Manorialism).
* **Task 2 Archival Interrogation:** Luttrell Psalter agrarian illustration vs Bishop’s Waltham Pipe Roll extraction.
* **Extended Writing Focus:** Evaluating the psychological, spiritual, and physical constraints governing peasant life.

---

### Enquiry 6: 1348: How Did the Black Death Shatter the Medieval Social Order?
* **Enquiry Question:** *How significantly did the Black Death of 1348 transform English society and weaken the feudal system?*
* **Historical Core:** Origins of *Yersinia pestis* in Central Asia and arrival via merchant galleys at Melcombe Regis (Weymouth) in June 1348; clinical differences between bubonic and pneumonic forms; medieval etiologies: divine retribution for sin, planetary conjunctions, corrupt air (miasma); devastating demographic collapse (30% to 50% of England’s population dies in 18 months); social terror and breakdown of religious comfort (priests fleeing or dying); economic revolution: acute labour scarcity, abandoned lands, plummeting grain prices, soaring wages; peasant wage bargaining; the landlord backlash: 1351 Statute of Labourers attempting to freeze wages at 1346 levels.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Turning Point Analysis* — Evaluating whether 1348 marked the fatal death blow for serfdom or an acceleration of existing economic trends.
* **Task 1 Vocabulary Practice:** *Dual-Term Analytical Distinction* (Bubonic Plague vs Pneumonic Plague).
* **Task 2 Archival Interrogation:** Henry Knighton chronicle on peasant insolence vs parliamentary text of the 1351 Statute of Labourers.
* **Extended Writing Focus:** Analytical balance sheet on short-term disaster vs long-term socioeconomic liberation for survivors.

---

### Enquiry 7: 1381: The Peasants' Revolt: Why Did They Rebel & What Did They Achieve?
* **Enquiry Question:** *Why did the English peasantry rebel in 1381, and to what extent was their rebellion successful?*
* **Historical Core:** Underlying post-plague tensions and resentment over the Statute of Labourers; the trigger: the regressive Third Poll Tax of 1380 to fund the Hundred Years' War in France; resistance starts in Essex and Kent; charismatic leadership: Wat Tyler (military organizer) and radical priest John Ball (*"When Adam delved and Eve span, who was then the gentleman?"*); the disciplined march on London; sacking the Savoy Palace and storming the Tower of London; the execution of Archbishop Simon Sudbury; King Richard II’s meetings with rebels at Mile End and Smithfield; Tyler’s death at the hands of Mayor William Walworth; Richard II’s false promise to be their captain; the subsequent brutal royal suppression (*"Rustics you were, and rustics you are..."*).
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Historical Evidence & Forensic Extraction* — Analysing chronicler bias (aristocratic terror vs rebel rhetoric).
* **Task 1 Vocabulary Practice:** *Vocabulary Mapping* (Poll Tax, Egalitarianism, Charter Revocation).
* **Task 2 Archival Interrogation:** Froissart’s record of John Ball’s sermon vs Anonimalle Chronicle account of the Mile End charter demands.
* **Extended Writing Focus:** Balanced judgement: immediate military failure and execution of leaders vs long-term economic collapse of serfdom by 1450.

---

### Enquiry 8: The Wars of the Roses (1455–1485): Dynastic Bloodbath & Tudor Settlement
* **Enquiry Question:** *Why did the English nobility tear England apart in the Wars of the Roses, and how did Bosworth Field end the medieval era?*
* **Historical Core:** The structural weakness of the English monarchy under Henry VI (mental illness, loss of French territories in Hundred Years' War); "Bastard Feudalism" (nobles retaining private armies of liveried retainers); the rivalry between the House of Lancaster (Red Rose) and the House of York (White Rose); Richard Duke of York and the Earl of Warwick ("The Kingmaker"); the slaughter at Towton (1461), fought in a blizzard; Edward IV’s reign and the succession crisis upon his death; the mystery of the Princes in the Tower and Richard III’s usurpation (1483); the climax at Bosworth Field (22 August 1485): Richard III’s desperate cavalry charge, the Stanley family betrayal, and Richard’s death; Henry Tudor crowned Henry VII; marriage to Elizabeth of York uniting the roses into the Tudor rose.
* **Disciplinary Skill:** *Agency, Causation & Historical Significance* — Evaluating whether personal noble ambition or structural failure of the crown caused the 30-year civil war.
* **Task 1 Vocabulary Practice:** *Contextual Cloze* (Bastard Feudalism, Retainer, Usurpation, Towton, Bosworth, Dynastic Settlement).
* **Task 2 Archival Interrogation:** Chronicle of the Brut on the slaughter at Towton vs Polydore Vergil on Richard III’s heroic but doomed stand at Bosworth.
* **Extended Writing Focus:** Sustained explanation of why the Wars of the Roses demonstrated the fatal vulnerability of medieval kingship to overmighty subjects.

---

### Capstone Enquiry (Page 20 Synthesis): How Powerful Was a Medieval Monarch?
* **Enquiry Question:** *“Medieval English kings exercised absolute, unchecked power over their kingdom.” How far do you agree with this statement?*
* **Synoptic Scope:** Drawing evidence across all eight enquiries (William I’s autocracy vs the Domesday balance; Henry II’s retreat after Becket’s murder; John’s humiliation at Runnymede; the breakdown of control during the Black Death and 1381 revolt; the deposition and murder of monarchs during the Wars of the Roses).
* **Extended Writing Structure:** 
  1. *Introduction:* Defining royal power; thesis acknowledging that while monarchs held theoretical absolute authority, in practice their power was strictly constrained by financial reality, military reliance on the nobility, and the moral authority of the Church.
  2. *Theme 1 (Instruments of Royal Power):* Norman castles, forest laws, royal justice, taxation, and feudal tenure.
  3. *Theme 2 (The Restraints on Power):* The Catholic Church (papal excommunication, Becket), the Barons (Magna Carta, civil wars), and popular unrest (1381).
  4. *Conclusion:* Balanced historical verdict evaluating the fragile reality of medieval kingship.

## Pedagogical Priming & Quality Gates
1. **Strict School Anonymity:** 0 instances of prohibited school identifiers; use "The History Revision Hub • Student Curriculum Edition" or "The History Department".
2. **Pedagogical Recall Isolation:** All 5 Do Now questions on every verso page strictly test recall from *prior* lessons or prior Key Stage 2/3 knowledge, never from the current lesson.
3. **Dynamic Ruled Lines:** All extended writing pages enforce client-side `data-auto-lines="true"` measurement in Puppeteer to guarantee 0px overflow and zero underflow dead space.
4. **Authentic Primary Evidence:** 100% authentic archival records and primary paintings/frescoes; zero AI imagery.
