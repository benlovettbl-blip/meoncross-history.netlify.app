Living Unit Timeline: 18th & 19th Century Medicine (Part I: 1700–1854)

Chronological Framework • Prevention & Early Reform
1796 Jenner Develops Smallpox Vaccine
Edward Jenner inoculates James Phipps with cowpox pus from Sarah Nelmes, successfully protecting him from smallpox and founding immunology.
1847 Simpson Discovers Chloroform
James Young Simpson discovers the potent anaesthetic properties of chloroform in Edinburgh, eliminating operative pain shock.
1848 First Public Health Act Passed
Prompted by Edwin Chadwick’s 1842 report on sanitary conditions, Parliament establishes a General Board of Health (permissive and optional).
1854 John Snow & Broad Street Pump
Dr John Snow uses his Soho cholera spot map to identify contaminated water from the Broad Street pump, disproving the miasma theory.
• Living Timeline Sketchpad & Causal Synthesis (1700–1854) Sketch Jenner’s cowpox experiment, Simpson’s chloroform trials, or Snow’s Broad Street pump map.
2/14 The 18th century relied on empirical observation; the 19th century unlocked the laboratory.

Living Unit Timeline: 18th & 19th Century Medicine (Part II: 1854–1900)

Chronological Framework • Germ Theory, Antiseptics & Sanitation
1854–56 Nightingale at Scutari (Crimea)
Florence Nightingale enforces hospital sanitation, clean water, and fresh bedding, reducing British soldier mortality from 40% to 2%.
1861 Pasteur Publishes Germ Theory
Louis Pasteur disproves spontaneous generation using swan-neck flasks, demonstrating that airborne microorganisms cause decay and disease.
1865 Lister Pioneers Antiseptic Surgery
Inspired by Pasteur’s Germ Theory, Joseph Lister uses carbolic acid spray during operations, reducing amputee mortality from 46% to 15%.
1875 The Second Public Health Act
Disraeli’s government legally compels all municipal councils to provide clean water, sewers, and refuse collection, ending laissez-faire.
• Living Timeline Sketchpad & Causal Synthesis (1854–1900) Connect Pasteur’s swan-neck flask → Lister’s carbolic spray → Bazalgette’s brick sewer tunnels.
Pasteur opened the door; Lister, Koch, and Bazalgette marched through it. 3/14
