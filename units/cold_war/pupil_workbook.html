HISTORICAL NARRATIVE OVERVIEW • 1945–1991 SUPERPOWER CONFLICT SYNOPSIS

For forty-six years following the defeat of Nazi Germany, the United States and the Soviet Union stood locked in an apocalyptic confrontation between capitalist democracy and Marxist communism. Armed with thousands of thermonuclear warheads capable of extinguishing human civilisation, the superpowers competed through proxy warfare, espionage, space exploration, and ideological brinkmanship. Across seven rigorous enquiries, pupils investigate the origin of the Iron Curtain, the Berlin Airlift, the Cuban Missile Crisis, the proxy bloodshed in Korea and Vietnam, the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989, and the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991.

EVALUATIVE ESSAY Question 4: Evaluative Statement Essay [12 marks • 15 mins]
Key Stage 3 • GCSE Bridging Standard
‘President Kennedy’s decisive leadership and naval quarantine were the main reasons the Cuban Missile Crisis was resolved peacefully.’ How far do you agree? [12 marks]
Stimulus Points: (1) Kennedy’s decision to impose a naval quarantine instead of launching airstrikes (October 1962); (2) Khrushchev’s willingness to compromise and the secret agreement to remove US Jupiter missiles from Turkey.
1. KENNEDY’S QUARANTINE Explain how JFK resisted military pressure for immediate bombing, buying critical time and offering Khrushchev a non-lethal off-ramp.
2. KHRUSHCHEV & SECRET BARGAIN Explain the counter-arguments: Khrushchev’s pragmatic letters, backchannel diplomacy (Robert Kennedy), and the vital secret concession over Jupiter missiles in Turkey.
3. COMPARATIVE VERDICT Weigh whether peaceful resolution was achieved primarily through American resolve or mutual dread of nuclear apocalypse (MAD) and shared restraint.
Academic Connectives: To a large extent, it can be argued that... • On the one hand, Kennedy’s leadership was pivotal because... • In particular, by implementing a quarantine rather than airstrikes... • On the other hand, an alternative view is that Khrushchev... • In conclusion, the crisis was resolved primarily because... rather than...
Historical Word Bank: Fidel Castro • Bay of Pigs • Operation Anadyr • U-2 Spy Plane • ExComm • Hawks vs Doves • Naval Quarantine • DEFCON 2 • Vasili Arkhipov • Turkey Jupiter Missiles • MAD • Moscow-Washington Hotline

EVALUATIVE ESSAY Question 4: Evaluative Statement Essay [12 marks • 15 mins]
Key Stage 3 • GCSE Bridging Standard
‘The reform policies of Mikhail Gorbachev were the main reason for the collapse of Soviet power in Eastern Europe between 1985 and 1989.’ How far do you agree? [12 marks]
Stimulus Points: (1) Gorbachev’s introduction of Glasnost and Perestroika and his renunciation of the Brezhnev Doctrine; (2) Economic bankruptcy in the Soviet bloc and the growth of grassroots protest movements (e.g. Solidarity in Poland).
1. GORBACHEV’S REFORMS Explain how abandoning the Brezhnev Doctrine told Eastern European satellite dictators that Soviet tanks would no longer crush domestic rebellions.
2. ECONOMIC RUIN & POPULAR PROTEST Explain the counter-arguments: chronic consumer shortages, the Afghan quagmire, Western defense pressure (SDI), and organized resistance like Poland’s Solidarity.
3. COMPARATIVE VERDICT Weigh whether Gorbachev’s conscious choices or systemic economic and political rot made the collapse of the Soviet empire inevitable.
Academic Connectives: To a large extent, it can be argued that... • On the one hand, Gorbachev’s policy shift was decisive because... • In particular, by signaling that the Red Army would not intervene... • On the other hand, deep underlying structural factors included... • In conclusion, while economic weakness set the stage, Gorbachev’s actions were the primary catalyst because...
