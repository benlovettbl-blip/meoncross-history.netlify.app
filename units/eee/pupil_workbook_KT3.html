1572 • The Vagabonds Act Key Topic 3.2

Parliament passes the 1572 Vagabonds Act, creating a legal distinction between the 'deserving poor' (elderly and sick) and 'idle beggars'. Vagrants over 14 are whipped and burned through the ear; repeat offenders face execution.

1576 • Act for Relief of the Poor & Burbage Builds 'The Theatre' Key Topic 3.1

The 1576 Poor Act orders town councils to provide wool and raw materials to put the unemployed to work, creating 'Houses of Correction' (Bridewells). In the same year, actor James Burbage constructs 'The Theatre' in Shoreditch, launching Elizabethan public drama.

DEC 1577 • Drake Departs on Global Circumnavigation Key Topic 3.3

Sir Francis Drake departs Plymouth with five ships on the *Pelican* (*Golden Hind*). Backed secretly by Elizabeth and investors, his mission is to disrupt Spanish Pacific trade, discover trade routes to the Moluccas, and claim land for the English Crown.

2/16 "A Golden Age: But if you were a beggar in 1572, you were bored through the ear with a hot iron."

Living Timeline • Part 2: Chronological Anchors (Key Topic 3)

Instructions: Complete the dual-coding sketches to permanently anchor these chronological turning points in long-term memory.
SEPT 1580 • Drake Returns to Deptford; Knighted by Elizabeth Key Topic 3.3

Drake sails the *Golden Hind* into Plymouth Harbour after 33 months, becoming the first Englishman to circumnavigate the globe. His cargo of Spanish bullion yields a 4,700% return for royal investors, and Elizabeth knights him on board in April 1581.

1584–1585 • Raleigh’s Patent & The First Roanoke Colony Key Topic 3.4

Queen Elizabeth grants Sir Walter Raleigh a royal patent to explore and colonise Virginia. In 1585, 107 male colonists sail under Sir Richard Grenville and Ralph Lane to Roanoke Island, seeking gold, privateering bases, and fertile agricultural land.

1587–1590 • The Second Roanoke Colony ("The Lost Colony") Key Topic 3.4

John White leads 118 settlers, including women and children, to establish a permanent plantation. When White returns in 1590 after being delayed by the Armada, the entire colony has vanished without a trace, leaving only the word "CROATOAN" carved on a post.

Cultural & Global Blueprint • The Elizabethan Playhouse & Voyages of Discovery

1. Anatomy of an Elizabethan Playhouse: The Globe & The Swan (Bankside)
① The Heavens & Roof: Painted canopy ceiling over the stage supported by wooden pillars; housed winches, ropes, and cannons for special effects.
② The Yard / Pit: Unroofed earthen standing area surrounding the thrust stage; held up to 1,000 "groundlings" who paid 1 penny to watch in all weathers.
③ Tiered Galleries & Lords: Three tiers of covered wooden seating (2–3 pence); exclusive Lords’ Rooms above the stage cost 6 pence for aristocratic display.
2. Elizabethan Global Exploration: Drake’s Circumnavigation & The Virginia Colonies
🧭 Drake’s Global Track (1577–1580) • Route: Departed Plymouth → Cape Verde → Brazil → Strait of Magellan (Cape Horn) → Valparaíso → Lima → California (Nova Albion) → Pacific Crossing → Moluccas (Ternate) → Cape of Good Hope → Plymouth.
• Achievements: First English circumnavigation, claimed California for Elizabeth, broke Spanish Pacific exclusivity, secured clove treaty with Sultan of Ternate, returned with £140,000 in silver bullion.
🌲 Raleigh’s Virginia Colonisation (1585–1587) • 1585 First Colony: Ralph Lane and 107 soldiers settle Roanoke Island. Flagship *Tiger* floods seeds; settlers fail to farm, rely on Secotan handouts, alienate Chief Wingina, and evacuate with Drake in 1586.
• 1587 Second Colony ("Lost Colony"): John White leads 118 civilian men, women, and children. White returns in 1590 after Armada delays; colony has vanished, leaving only "CROATOAN".

Master Knowledge Organiser • Key Topic 3: Elizabethan Society in the Age of Exploration, 1558–88

1. Core Disciplinary Vocabulary & Conceptual Definitions
• Humanist Education: Renaissance curriculum focusing on classical Latin, Greek, rhetoric, and moral philosophy.
• Grammar School: Fee-paying secondary school for boys aged 7–14; over 70 established under Elizabeth.
• Agricultural Enclosure: Fencing common arable land into private pastures for sheep farming, dispossessing farm labourers.
• Impotent Poor: The elderly, sick, and disabled unable to work; categorized as "deserving" of parish relief.
• Sturdy Beggars: Able-bodied vagrants categorized as "idle"; punished by whipping and forced labour.
• Houses of Correction: Bridewell workhouses created by the 1576 Poor Act to punish idle vagrants with hard labour.
• Astrolabe: Navigation tool measuring the altitude of sun and stars to calculate latitude at sea.
• Royal Patent: Royal charter granting exclusive rights to explore, settle, and exploit foreign territories.
2. Essential Chronology
• 1572: Vagabonds Act passed
• 1576: Act for Relief of the Poor
• 1576: Burbage builds 'The Theatre'
• 1577: Drake sails on *Golden Hind*
• 1580: Drake returns to Deptford
• 1584: Raleigh receives Virginia patent
• 1585: First Roanoke colony founded
• 1586: Drake rescues Roanoke settlers
• 1587: Second Roanoke settlement
• 1590: White finds "CROATOAN" post
3. Key Historical Figures Matrix
• Sir Walter Raleigh: Courtier, poet, and explorer; organized and financed the Virginia colonization expeditions.
• James Burbage: Actor and builder; constructed 'The Theatre' in 1576, London’s first public playhouse.
• William Shakespeare: Playwright and actor; joined the Lord Chamberlain’s Men; shaped Elizabethan culture.
• Ralph Lane: Governor of the 1585 Roanoke colony; military officer whose brutality alienated native tribes.
• John White: Artist and governor of the 1587 "Lost Colony"; painted watercolors of Native American life.
• Chief Wingina: Secotan tribal chief on Roanoke Island; initially aided English, killed by Lane in 1586.

2. The 12/12 Mark Formula: Explain Why [12 Marks • 18 Mins] EDEXCEL JUNE 2019 • TARGET: 11–12 / 12 (LEVEL 4)
Three PEEL Paragraphs: Use both provided stimulus points + introduce at least ONE distinct self-selected knowledge point. Every paragraph must feature explicit causal reasoning words (Consequently, As a direct result, This meant that).
Level 4 Linking Formula: Do not just list reasons! Connect them causally: "This economic grievance did not operate in a vacuum; rather, it directly exacerbated religious fear because..."
3. The 16/16 Mark Formula: Evaluative Essay [16 Marks + 4 SPaG • 25 Mins] EDEXCEL JUNE 2022 • TARGET: 15–16 / 16 (LEVEL 4)
Criteria-Led Judgment Architecture:
• Introduction: Define the key terms, state your overarching thesis, and establish your criteria for judgment (e.g. short-term threat vs long-term structural instability).
• Paragraph 1 (Agree with Statement): Rigorously analyze the stated factor with precise dates, statistics, and historical names.
• Paragraphs 2 & 3 (Counter-Arguments): Introduce other critical factors (including required own-knowledge beyond the stimulus points).
• Conclusion: Weigh the factors comparatively! Never write "both were important". Explain why one factor was the foundational catalyst while another was merely a symptom.
★ Examiner Secret: High-scoring Level 4 candidates establish explicit criteria in the first two sentences: "In evaluating this statement, success must be judged by whether the policy achieved long-term institutional stability or merely temporary crisis containment..."
