17 NOV 1558 • Accession of Elizabeth I & Council Appointments Key Topic 1.1

Mary I dies; 25-year-old Elizabeth succeeds to the throne. She immediately appoints trusted Protestant statesman Sir William Cecil as her Principal Secretary, confronting £300,000 in Crown debts and French military forces stationed in Scotland.

MAY 1559 • The Acts of Supremacy & Uniformity (The Settlement) Key Topic 1.2

Parliament passes the Religious Settlement. The Act of Supremacy establishes Elizabeth as 'Supreme Governor' of the Church of England; the Act of Uniformity mandates the 1559 Book of Common Prayer and imposes a 1 shilling fine for recusancy.

SUMMER 1559 • The Royal Injunctions & National Visitations Key Topic 1.2

The Crown issues 57 Royal Injunctions enforcing Protestant worship. Clergy must preach royal supremacy, condemn papal authority, report recusants, and use English bibles. National visitations by 125 commissioners inspect parish compliance.

Instructions: Complete the dual-coding sketches to permanently anchor these chronological turning points in long-term memory.
JULY 1560 • The Treaty of Edinburgh Key Topic 1.1 / 1.4

French troops withdraw from Scotland after a rebellion by Protestant Scottish lords. The Treaty establishes peace and Mary, Queen of Scots gives up her claim to the English throne, though Mary refuses to formally ratify the agreement.

1566 • The Vestments Controversy & Puritan Dissent Key Topic 1.3

Archbishop Matthew Parker issues the 'Book of Advertisements' requiring all clergy to wear the surplice and tippet. In London, 37 Puritan vicars refuse to wear Catholic-style vestments and are deprived of their livings, revealing deep Protestant divisions.

MAY 1568 • Mary Queen of Scots Flees to England Key Topic 1.4

Following the murder of Lord Darnley and defeat at Carberry Hill, Catholic Mary Stuart flees Scotland in a fishing boat to Workington, Cumberland. Elizabeth places her under armed house arrest, triggering twenty years of Catholic conspiracy.

Archival & Conceptual Blueprint • Structure of Government & Religious Spectrum

1. Anatomy of Elizabethan Government: Power, Patronage & Prerogative
👑 The Monarch (Queen Elizabeth I): Ruled by Divine Right. Controlled Royal Prerogative: deciding foreign policy, declaring war, marriage, and religion. Could summon and dismiss Parliament at will.
🏛️ The Privy Council (Led by William Cecil): Approx. 19 trusted senior advisors who met daily. Managed government expenditure, state security, military logistics, and drafted royal legislation.
⚖️ Parliament (Lords & Commons): Met only 10 times in 44 years. Crucial power: approving extraordinary taxation (subsidies) and passing statute laws. Free speech was strictly limited by the Queen.
🛡️ Lords Lieutenant & Justices of the Peace (JPs): Lords Lieutenant trained county militias. Unpaid JPs (gentry) enforced laws locally: collected taxes, regulated wages, punished beggars, and tracked recusants.
2. The Elizabethan Religious Spectrum: Radical Puritanism vs The Middle Way vs Roman Catholicism
PURITAN REFORMERS • Leader: John Calvin (Geneva)
• Church Government: Committees of presbyters (no bishops)
• Services: Plain English, long sermons
• Ornaments: No vestments (surplices), no crucifixes, plain communion tables
• Sacraments: 2 (Baptism & Communion)
• Attitude to Queen: Wanted further radical purification
ELIZABETH’S VIA MEDIA (1559) • Supreme Governor: Queen Elizabeth I
• Hierarchy: Kept Archbishops and Bishops
• Services: English Book of Common Prayer
• Ornaments: Clergy wear white surplice; candles and music permitted
• Communion: Deliberately ambiguous wording accepting spiritual presence
• Recusancy: 1 shilling fine for non-attendance
ROMAN CATHOLIC CHURCH • Supreme Head: The Pope in Rome
• Hierarchy: Cardinals, Bishops, Priests
• Services: Traditional Latin Mass
• Ornaments: Rich embroidered vestments, crucifixes, statues of saints, altars
• Sacraments: 7 Sacraments
• Transubstantiation: Bread & wine become the literal body of Christ

Master Knowledge Organiser • Key Topic 1: Queen, Government and Religion, 1558–69

1. Core Disciplinary Vocabulary & Conceptual Definitions
• Royal Prerogative: The monarch’s exclusive customary powers over foreign policy, declaring war, marriage, and religion.
• Privy Council: A committee of around 19 trusted noble advisers who met daily to run government, finance, and security.
• Via Media: The "Middle Way": Elizabeth’s religious settlement balancing Protestant doctrine with traditional ritual.
• Act of Supremacy (1559): Established Elizabeth as "Supreme Governor" of the Church of England, requiring all clergy to take an oath.
• Act of Uniformity (1559): Mandated the Book of Common Prayer, church ornaments, vestments, and imposed a 1s recusancy fine.
• Recusancy: Refusing to attend compulsory Church of England Sunday services; practiced by devout Roman Catholics.
• Vestments Controversy: 1566 crisis where 37 Puritan vicars were suspended for refusing Archbishop Parker’s order to wear surplices.
• Succession: The legal right to inherit the crown; Elizabeth refused to name a successor throughout her reign.
2. Essential Chronology
• 1558: Elizabeth succeeds Mary I
• 1559: Act of Supremacy passed
• 1559: Act of Uniformity passed
• 1559: Royal Injunctions issued
• 1560: Treaty of Edinburgh signed
• 1566: Vestments Controversy (Parker)
• 1567: Murder of Lord Darnley
• 1568: Mary Stuart flees to England
• 1568–69: York & Westminster inquiry
• 1569: Northern Earls revolt breaks out
3. Key Historical Figures Matrix
• Queen Elizabeth I: Tudor monarch (1558–1603); established Protestant settlement and resisted marriage.
• Sir William Cecil: Principal Secretary & Lord Burghley; Elizabeth’s most trusted Protestant statesman.
• Matthew Parker: Archbishop of Canterbury (1559–75); drafted the 1566 Book of Advertisements.
• Mary, Queen of Scots: Catholic cousin of Elizabeth; claimed English throne; fled Scotland in 1568.
• Philip II of Spain: Catholic king of Spain and Netherlands; former husband of Mary I; rival to Elizabeth.
• Robert Dudley: Earl of Leicester; Master of the Horse, royal favourite, and leading Puritan patron at court.
