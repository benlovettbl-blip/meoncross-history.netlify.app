Executive Summary: The Departmental Stance on Historical Text Complexity

The History Department explicitly rejects the pervasive secondary practice of "simplifying" or "dumbing down" historical enquiry narratives to match a pupil's independent chronological reading age. In history, vocabulary and sentence syntax are the historical concepts. Dumbing down text strips away historical contingency, causal nuance, and academic dignity. Instead, our curriculum deploys High-Challenge Disciplinary Texts (calibrated at reading ages 16–18+) paired with The Decoded Classroom Model: whole-class teacher-modelled reading, synchronized digital speech tracking, and structured two-lesson enquiry pacing.

1.0 The Theoretical Foundation: Why Simplification Fails in History

In secondary education, an insidious trap occurs when teachers confuse a pupil's independent silent decoding ability with their conceptual historical capacity. When a Year 8 pupil with an independent reading age of 11.5 is given a simplified worksheet containing short, disjointed sentences (e.g. "Cort lived in Hampshire. He made iron. The Navy needed iron. He died poor."), their capacity for historical causal analysis is permanently truncated.

"If we want pupils to think like historians, we must immerse them in the language of historians. Simplifying the text does not close the reading gap; it widens it by permanently locking struggling readers out of the academic discourse required for higher-order causal reasoning." — Alex Quigley, Closing the Reading Gap (Routledge, 2020)

Text Complexity Flattened to Chronological Reading Age (11–12 yrs). Sentences severed to 8–12 words. Tier 3 terms replaced with colloquialisms. Uncompromised Academic Rigour (16–18+ yrs). Authentic syntactic complexity (22–30 words/sentence) rich in subordination.

3.0 The Two-Lesson Enquiry Choreography (The Timed Lesson Arc)

To ensure high challenge never translates into cognitive overload or teacher pacing anxiety, every major enquiry is calibrated across a standard Two-Lesson Sequence (2x 50 minutes):

Lesson 1: The Outpost & The Breakthrough 50 Minutes

00–10m
Hook & Spaced Retrieval: Display Source A (Coalbrookdale by Night). Link thermal energy of coal to prior knowledge of the domestic system and waterwheels.
10–30m
Modelled Shared Reading (Acts 1 & 2): Teacher reads aloud with expressive prosody (or projects web app natural audio at 1.0x with karaoke tracking). Pause at Act 1 to interrogate Baltic naval crisis.
30–45m
Forensic Fieldwork Analysis: Deconstruct Source B and the Funtley Dossier. Contrast 1784 painting with modern photos of the River Meon weir and Hampshire’s only iron slag wall.
45–50m
Formative Hinge Plenary: Execute Hinge Question 2: "Why does an iron slag dry-stone wall prove Cort’s chemical puddling process succeeded?"

Lesson 2: The Trial, Historiography & Mastery 50 Minutes

00–10m
Spaced Retrieval & Vocab Anchor: Complete retrieval check in printed workbook. Review crucial distinction: Brittle Pig Iron vs. Tough Naval Wrought Iron.
10–25m
Shared Reading & Academic Debate (Acts 3 & 4): Read Acts 3 & 4. Teacher leads debate: Was Cort a solitary genius, or did geographic proximity to Portsmouth Dockyard decide his triumph?
25–45m
Workbook Mastery (Pages 4–5): Pupils work independently in booklets: annotate furnace cross-section, analyze Admiralty trial minutes, and write 18-line PEEL extended answer using the Gold Criteria Strip.
45–50m
Live Exemplar Critique: Visualizer display of pupil work evaluated against the department's Gold Evaluation Criteria (Scale, Permanence, Catalyst).

5.0 Guidance for Head of Department & Teacher Induction (ECTs)

1. Never abandon pupils to silent, unassisted reading of Acts 1–3: Model reading with expressive voice inflection, stop at paragraph references, and unpack complex clauses as a collective scholar community.
2. Protect the Two-Lesson Rhythm: Never attempt to cram reading, source analysis, and an 18-line essay into a single 50-minute lesson. Lesson 1 is for deep historical immersion; Lesson 2 is for mastery output.
3. Leverage Digital Audio for SEND Differentiation: Pupils with dyslexia, processing delays, or EAL should be invited to plug in headphones during Lesson 2 and use the web app’s paragraph audio bar at 0.85x speed to re-listen to specific evidence dispatches while writing their workbook paragraphs.
