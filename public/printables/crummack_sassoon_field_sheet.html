Holograph
Sassoon's trench notebook f. 38r with live ink corrections
Elegy: for M.G. (Marcus Goodall) Was it for English morning, spilled & glowing, Across gray hummock'd fields, dim cattle showing, Was it for this I longed? — the glittering brass Of rays low on brown roofs & steaming grass, — A garden spiked with blue & splashed with white, Yellow and red and all the eye's delight; — Was it for these I longed, while you were dead, Your mirth destroyed and from your lolling head The racing thoughts gone out like smoke on air, Thinning & whirling & subsiding, — where? Sad victim, could you see your body thrown Into a shallow pit along that wood Thronged by the dead? O, there you lie not lone, Under the splinter'd trees; for the brotherhood Of discontented slain, with eyes that scowl, And bristly cheeks & chins all bloody-smears, Will hug their rank red wounds & limp & prowl, Squatting around your grave with moans & tears. But soon, I hope a monster shell will burst, And all such filth be blotted & dispersed: You'll no more need to cling to the dead clay, Dancing through fields of heaven to meet the day...
Forensic Paleography: Sassoon's Revisions Under Combat Stress
• Line 6: Struck out Orange → inserted Yellow (intensifies luminous English morning vs trench darkness).
• Line 10: Crossed out Following & → replaced with Thinning & (evokes dispersing smoke and fading brain).
• Line 11: Struck out Poor victim → replaced with Sad victim (rejects helpless pity for tragic dignity).
• Line 21: Struck out wet clay → substituted dead clay (personifies the Somme mud with mortal rot).

2nd Lt Ernest Crummack MC, DCM

1/5th & 2/4th York & Lancaster Regt · Ypres, Somme, Cambrai
Combat Record
Top: Sgt Crummack DCM (1916). Bottom: Mill Road & Pope's Nose.
The Working-Class Warrior: Born in Barnsley (1888), Ernest worked as an underground coalminer ("ripper") at Dinnington Main Colliery. Enlisting as a Private in Sept 1914, his toughness and instinctive leadership propelled him to Sergeant.
The Somme Rescue (3 July 1916 · Mill Road, Thiepval Wood):
Hearing that 2nd Lt Marcus Goodall lay mortally wounded in the open, Sgt Crummack climbed out of the British trenches under continuous machine-gun and shell fire. He crawled to Goodall, bandaged his wounds in the dirt, and carried him back to British care. Awarded: Distinguished Conduct Medal (DCM) & Russian Medal of St George
Canal du Nord Assault & Military Cross (27–29 Sept 1918):
Commissioned as 2nd Lieutenant, Crummack led his platoon across the dry Canal du Nord. When machine guns pinned down the advance east of the canal, he led a daring frontal rush, silenced the gun, and killed the crew. Awarded: Military Cross (MC) · London Gazette Citation
The 6 Decorations: MC • DCM • 1914–15 Mons Star • British War Medal • Victory Medal • Russian Order of St George
Field Stop 1: Boesinghe & Essex Farm (Day 1)

Stand on the western levee of the Yser Canal at Essex Farm. Gaze north towards The Northern Nip at Boesinghe where Ernest Crummack and the 1/5th York & Lancs endured the deadly first German Phosgene gas attack on 19 December 1915, donning crude flannel P-helmets to hold the line.

Field Stop 2: Thiepval Wood & Mill Road (Day 2)

Stand at the edge of Thiepval Wood looking across Mill Road towards the Pope's Nose redoubt. Contrast Sassoon's poetic grief with Crummack's physical valor. Note how Crummack's crawl into No Man's Land proves that British soldiers were not passive victims, but men bonded by extraordinary love and courage.
