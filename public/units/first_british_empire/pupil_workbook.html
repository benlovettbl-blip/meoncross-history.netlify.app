Year 8 Enquiry • 1600–1783

The First British Empire (1600–1783)

Chartered Trade, Transatlantic Slavery, Maroon Resistance & The American Revolution
Primary Visual Evidence • Corporate Imperial Statecraft Nathaniel Dance-Holland (c. 1773)

Following the 1757 Battle of Plassey and the 1765 Treaty of Allahabad, the East India Company transitioned from a maritime trading outfit into a military ruler collecting taxes from 20 million Bengalis. Nathaniel Dance portrayed Robert Clive not as an administrator, but as an imperial general:

1. Stance of Imperial Authority:
Clive stands resolute in scarlet regimental uniform, sword belt fastened, projecting military command over Indian sovereign territories.
2. The Bengal Battlefield:
In the background, sepoy infantry drill beneath smoke and battle pennants, symbolising the privatised corporate army that conquered Bengal.
3. Royal & Corporate Honours:
Embroidered badges of the Order of the Bath honour Clive’s elevation to the peerage funded by astronomical private plunder ('jagirs').
4. The Obscured Reality:
The painting glorifies military prestige while obscuring the horrific 1770 Bengal famine where corporate grain speculation devastated millions.
Scarlet Coat
Crown recognition of Company military conquest.
Drawn Sword
Armed force as foundation of trade monopoly.
Bengal Plain
Vast territorial wealth seized from Mughals.
Disciplinary Significance: In 18th-century imperial expansion, portraiture transformed private corporate commanders into national martial heroes to justify unprecedented chartered monopolies to Parliament.
Disciplinary Hinge Question: Historical Evaluation
Does this portrait portray Clive as an honourable servant of the British nation, or does it disguise a reckless corporate plunderer whose greed destabilised an entire subcontinent?

Question 3(b) & 3(c): Contrasting Interpretations [8 marks • 10 mins]

HISTORIOGRAPHICAL ANALYSIS
Interpretation 1 • Planter Edward Long, History of Jamaica (1774)
“The plantation negroes are naturally indolent and require the perpetual surveillance and discipline of the driver’s whip to extract labour. Without European mastery, order would instantly dissolve into ruin.”
Interpretation 2 • Historian Hilary Beckles, Caribbean Freedom (2004)
“Enslaved Africans waged an unrelenting war of resistance through daily sabotage, spiritual preservation, and armed guerrilla struggle. Queen Nanny proved that African military strategy could humble the British imperial army.”
Explain how Interpretations 1 and 2 differ on the nature of enslaved African resistance. [4 marks]
PART B
Sentence Stems: Interpretations 1 and 2 differ on... • Interpretation 1 argues that... • In contrast, Interpretation 2 suggests that... • This is a difference because...
Suggest one reason why Interpretations 1 and 2 differ on the reality of plantation control. [4 marks]
PART C
Scaffolded Sentence Stems: One reason why Interpretations 1 and 2 differ is because they have a different focus... • Specifically, Interpretation 1 focuses primarily on... • In contrast, Interpretation 2 focuses more on... • As a result, the authors reach different conclusions because...
