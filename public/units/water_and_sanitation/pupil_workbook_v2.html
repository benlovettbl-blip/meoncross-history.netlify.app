Curriculum Synopsis • The 2,000-Year Clean Water Quest

From the Roman lead pipes at Fishbourne Palace to the stench of medieval cesspits and the deadly cholera water pumps of Victorian London, clean water has been Britain’s greatest battle. In this Year 7 pupil workbook, students investigate how doctors, brave engineers, and government laws transformed Britain from a country plagued by water-borne death into a modern society where safe, clean drinking water flows from every tap.

Water & Sanitation (c.43 AD–Present) • Master Review

Unit Revision Hub
The Four Key Factors in Public Health Progress
1. SCIENTIFIC KNOWLEDGE Moving from false Miasma theory to Dr. John Snow’s cholera map (1854), Louis Pasteur’s Germ Theory (1861), and Robert Koch’s bacterial discoveries.
2. ENGINEERING & TECHNOLOGY Roman gravity aqueducts and bathhouses; Harington’s flushing toilet (1596); Bazalgette’s 82 miles of Victorian underground intercepting sewers.
3. GOVERNMENT POWER & LAW Abandoning laissez-faire after the Great Stink of 1858; the compulsory 1875 Public Health Act; establishing clean municipal water supplies for all.
4. INDIVIDUAL BRAVERY & REFORM Campaigners like Edwin Chadwick using statistics to shame Parliament, and Dr. John Snow risking his life to remove the Broad Street pump handle.
The 2,000-Year Historical Balance Sheet
Periods of Squalor & Disease: After Roman withdrawal (410 AD), piped water collapsed. Medieval towns and overcrowded 19th-century industrial cellar slums were plagued by cholera, typhoid, and infant mortality.
The Clean Water Triumph: Victorian sewer construction, reservoir networks, sand filtration, and chlorination virtually eliminated water-borne deaths, raising British life expectancy from 40 to over 80 years.
