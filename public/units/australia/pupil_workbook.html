Year 8 Enquiry • 1770–1901

The History of Australia (1770–1901)

First Nations Custodianship, Convict Colonisation, Frontier Wars & The Gold Rush
Primary Visual Evidence • The Founding of Colonial Australia Francis Fowkes (1788)

In January 1788, eleven British sailing ships commanded by Governor Arthur Phillip landed roughly 1,400 convicts, marines, and sailors at Sydney Cove. Fowkes’s contemporary survey records the vulnerable foothold of the penal colony on the edge of the Pacific:

1. Sheltered Deep-Water Anchorage:
Transports ride at anchor in Sydney Cove, chosen after Phillip abandoned Botany Bay due to dangerous ocean surf and poor fresh water.
2. The Convict Tent City:
Rough canvas tents and wattle-and-daub huts house 750 transported prisoners battling scurvy, dysentery, and near-starvation.
3. The Freshwater Tank Stream:
The vital freshwater creek that determined the location of modern Sydney, carved through sandstone by convicts to store drinking water.
4. The Obscured Indigenous Reality:
The imperial map depicts the land as an empty canvas, systematically erasing the sovereign presence of the Gadigal and Eora clans.
Canvas Tents
Fragile foothold on an isolated continent.
Naval Transports
15,000-mile exile under the Bloody Code.
Eora Country
65,000 years of custodianship erased.
Disciplinary Significance: In colonial history, surveying and cartography were weapons of imperial conquest that turned living Indigenous homelands into European property through the myth of terra nullius.
Disciplinary Hinge Question: Historical Evaluation
Does this contemporary survey depict the triumphant founding of a new nation, or the unauthorized military invasion of sovereign First Nations lands?

Question 3(b) & 3(c): Contrasting Interpretations [8 marks • 10 mins]

HISTORIOGRAPHICAL ANALYSIS
Interpretation 1 • Historian Henry Reynolds, The Other Side of the Frontier (1981)
“The European occupation of Australia was resisted from the beginning by Indigenous peoples using guerrilla tactics. It was not a peaceful settlement, but a protracted, bloody war that cost the lives of at least 20,000 Aboriginal people defending their lands.”
Interpretation 2 • Historian Keith Windschuttle, The Fabrication of Aboriginal History (2002)
“There was no systematic warfare or frontier genocide in colonial Australia. The conflicts that occurred were uncoordinated, small-scale criminal skirmishes over stolen goods and sheep, and the death tolls cited by orthodox historians have been drastically fabricated without documentary proof.”
Explain how Interpretations 1 and 2 differ on the nature and scale of frontier conflict in Australia. [4 marks]
PART B
Sentence Stems: Interpretations 1 and 2 differ on... • Interpretation 1 argues that... • In contrast, Interpretation 2 suggests that... • This is a difference because...
Suggest one reason why Interpretations 1 and 2 differ on the reality of the Frontier Wars. [4 marks]
PART C
