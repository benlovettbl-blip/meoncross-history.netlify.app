7. ►
The American Revolution ended convict transportation to North America, overcrowding British gaols — Britain had banished 50,000 convicts to America; losing the colonies forced Lord Sydney to choose Botany Bay to relieve crowded prison hulks.

11 ships carrying roughly 1,480 people, including over 750 convicts — The armada included two warships, six convict transports, and three store ships, sailing 15,000 miles over 252 days with low mortality.

Botany Bay was shallow, exposed to open gales, and lacked an adequate fresh water supply — Phillip scouted north to Port Jackson, finding "the finest natural harbour in the world" and fresh water at Sydney Cove on 26 January 1788.

10. ►►

11. ►►►
By distributing convict labourers to free settlers, allowing pardoned "emancipists" to acquire land — Unlike enslaved people, convicts had fixed terms; upon release, emancipists became prosperous farmers and merchants, challenging elites.

Enquiry 5: Gold Rush, Eureka & Federation
25. ►
The 1851 Australian Gold Rush in New South Wales and Victoria — Discoveries tripled the population in a decade as fortune-seekers ("diggers") arrived from Britain, Ireland, America, and China.
[✓] [✗]
26. ►
Expensive monthly mining licences enforced by police, with no voting rights — Miners burned licences and built a wooden stockade at Ballarat under the Southern Cross flag to defend their democratic liberties.
[✓] [✗]
27. ►►
European diggers resented their collective work success, sparking anti-Chinese riots — Mobs attacked Chinese camps at Lambing Flat, driving discriminatory taxes and laws that led to the 1901 White Australia Policy.
[✓] [✗]
28. ►►
By forcing Victoria to abolish licences, introduce cheap miners’ rights, and grant the vote — Rebel leader Peter Lalor was elected to parliament, leading Victoria to adopt the secret ballot, later copied worldwide.
[✓] [✗]
29. ►►►
Free settlers demanded self-government and refused to accept convict labour — Exiling convicts to a prosperous gold-rush colony destroyed transportation’s deterrent effect, prompting successful boycotts.
[✓] [✗]
30. ►►►
While uniting the colonies into a Commonwealth, the constitution excluded First Nations — The 1901 Constitution excluded Aboriginal people from the census, while immigration laws institutionalised a "White Australia".
[✓] [✗]
Unit Conceptual Foundations • 4 Core Anchors
Terra Nullius: ‘Nobody’s land’; the legal fiction used by the British Crown to claim Australia without treaties or recognising native ownership.
First Nations: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples holding continuous sovereignty and sacred Songlines for over 65,000 years.
Transportation: Judicial exile of British convicts to overseas penal colonies for 7 years, 14 years, or life for forced infrastructure labour.
Frontier Resistance: Protracted armed resistance of Indigenous clans against pastoral expansion, stockmen violence, and martial law.
