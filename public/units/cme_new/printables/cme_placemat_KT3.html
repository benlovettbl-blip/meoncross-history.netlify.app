🔑 Core Specification Terminology (Non-Negotiable Facts)
• Shuttle Diplomacy: Henry Kissinger flying between Middle Eastern capitals to broker disengagement agreements after the 1973 war.
• Camp David Accords (1978): 13-day summit hosted by Jimmy Carter producing frameworks for Sinai peace and Palestinian autonomy.
• UNLU: Unified National Leadership of the Uprising: clandestine committees directing strikes and protests during the First Intifada.
• Oslo II (1995): Interim agreement dividing the West Bank into Area A (Palestinian), Area B (joint), and Area C (full Israeli control, 73%).
📖 The 3-Stage Narrative Framework
Stage 1: The Spark (Dec 1987)
An Israeli military vehicle killed 4 labourers at the Erez checkpoint; funerals in Jabalia erupted into spontaneous demonstrations, stone-throwing, and strikes.
Stage 2: Civil Disobedience & Iron Fist
The underground UNLU directed boycotts and tax strikes; Defense Minister Yitzhak Rabin ordered ‘force, might, and beatings’, drawing global media condemnation.
Stage 3: Political Realization & Oslo
King Hussein severed West Bank ties in 1988; Israeli leaders realized direct military rule was unsustainable, prompting secret backchannel talks in Norway leading to the 1993 Oslo Accords.

One consequence was that it shattered British domestic and political resolve. Irgun militants disguised as milkmen detonated explosives in the basement, killing 91 people. This directly forced Attlee to refer Palestine to the UN in February 1947.

8-Mark Narrative (T-P-C)

The conflict began when 5 Arab armies invaded in May 1948. A crucial turning point occurred when the June truce allowed Israel to acquire Czech Avia fighters. Consequently, the IDF broke the siege of Jerusalem and secured 79% of Palestine.

8-Mark Importance (F-I-L)

Resolution 181 was vital because it allocated 55% of land to a Jewish state. Without this UN legal mandate, David Ben-Gurion could not have declared sovereign statehood on 14 May 1948.
