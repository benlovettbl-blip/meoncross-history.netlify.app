🧠 Section A: Rapid-Fire Knowledge Check (10 Facts) Cover, Recall, Self-Check
1. What crucial man-made waterway connects the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea, serving as a vital strategic chokepoint for international trade and oil?
Ans: The Suez Canal
2. Which strategic maritime passage at the mouth of the Gulf of Aqaba was blockaded by Egypt in 1956 and 1967, acts that Israel considered causes of war?
Ans: The Straits of Tiran
3. Which triangular desert peninsula connecting Africa to Asia was captured by Israel from Egypt in 1956 and 1967, before being returned under the Camp David Accords?
Ans: The Sinai Peninsula
4. Which strategic river forms the natural eastern boundary of the West Bank, flowing south from the Sea of Galilee into the Dead Sea?
Ans: The River Jordan
5. Which ancient and holy city, claimed as a capital by both Israelis and Palestinians, was divided between 1948 and 1967 before Israeli forces captured the Old City?
Ans: Jerusalem
6. Which elevated volcanic plateau bordering south-western Syria was captured by Israel during the 1967 Six Day War due to its dominant military vantage point over Galilee?
Ans: The Golan Heights
7. Which narrow coastal strip bordering Egypt's Sinai Peninsula came under Egyptian military control in 1948, was captured by Israel in 1967, and became an epicenter of the 1987 Intifada?
Ans: The Gaza Strip
8. Which territory west of the River Jordan was annexed by Jordan in 1950, occupied by Israel in 1967, and became the central focus of Jewish settlement expansion?
Ans: The West Bank
9. What was the de facto border established by the 1949 Armistice Agreements separating Israel from the West Bank, Gaza Strip, and Golan Heights until 1967?
Ans: The Green Line
10. Which arid southern desert region accounted for over 55% of the total land area allocated to the Jewish state under the 1947 UN Partition Plan?
Ans: The Negev Desert

🔑 Section C: Key Specification Terms (Non-Negotiable)
• Conflicting Demands: Zionist pressure for 250,000 Holocaust survivors vs. Arab demands for independence based on majority rule.
• Plan Dalet (Plan D): Haganah military plan of April 1948 to secure supply corridors and clear hostile villages before British departure.
• Law of Return (1950): Landmark Knesset law granting every Jewish person worldwide automatic citizenship in Israel.
• Protocol of Sèvres (1956): Secret collusion between Britain, France, and Israel to invade Sinai and seize the Suez Canal.
⚠️ THE EXAMINER'S TRAP DOOR: Don't confuse the 1947 UN Partition Plan (Res 181, which allocated 55% to a Jewish state) with the 1949 Armistice Green Line (which enclosed 79% of Mandatory Palestine). And remember: the decisive turning point of the 1948–49 War was the 4-week UN truce in June 1948 when Israel imported Avia S-199 fighters and rifles from Czechoslovakia!

2. Write a narrative account analysing the key events of the Arab-Israeli war (1948–49). (8 Marks)
Stage 1: Outbreak (15 May 1948)
Armies from Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon, and Iraq invaded simultaneously following the Declaration of Independence; Arab advance threatened Tel Aviv and besieged 100,000 Jews in Jerusalem.
Stage 2: Turning Point (June 1948)
A 4-week UN truce gave Israel critical breathing space to secretly import Avia S-199 fighters and rifles from Czechoslovakia, while David Ben-Gurion unified all militias into the IDF.
Stage 3: Outcome & Green Line (1949)
IDF launched sweeping autumn counter-offensives, opened the Burma Road into Jerusalem, and secured 1949 armistices enclosing 79% of Palestine, creating 700,000 Palestinian refugees (the Nakba).

3. Explain the importance of UN Resolution 181 for the creation of the State of Israel. (8 Marks)
Detailed Historical Facts (AO1) Causal Link to Outcome (AO2)
Passed by a 2/3 UN majority on 29 November 1947, allocating 55% of Mandatory Palestine to a Jewish state, 45% to an Arab state, and designating Jerusalem an international corpus separatum. It provided the essential international legal legitimacy for David Ben-Gurion to proclaim the sovereign State of Israel on 14 May 1948, ending two millennia of Jewish statelessness.
