Key Topic 2: The Escalating Conflict, 1964–73

Official Mark Scheme & Knowledge Reference · Answers 1 to 30 (Double-Sided Flip Reference)

Mark Scheme 1
1. What major political entity was established at the January 1964 Arab League Summit in Cairo to represent the Palestinian national movement?
Ans: The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO)
2. Which faction within the Palestinian movement, led by Yasser Arafat, advocated independent armed guerrilla struggle rather than reliance on regular Arab armies?
Ans: Fatah
3. What was the 'Water War' fought between Israel and Syria in the mid-1960s?
Ans: A series of military skirmishes over Syrian attempts to divert the headwaters of the River Jordan away from Israel's National Water Carrier
4. What major cross-border raid on 13 November 1966 saw the IDF attack a West Bank village in Jordanian territory following a Fatah landmine attack?
Ans: The Samu Raid
5. What dramatic air engagement on 7 April 1967 escalated border tensions between Israel and Syria to a fever pitch?
Ans: Israeli Mirage fighters shot down six Syrian MiG-21s and buzzed the skies over Damascus
6. What false intelligence report did the Soviet Union deliver to Egyptian officials in Cairo on 13 May 1967?
Ans: That Israel was massing 11 to 13 military brigades on its northern border to launch an invasion of Syria
7. What provocative step did President Nasser take on 16 May 1967 to demonstrate Egyptian solidarity with Syria?
Ans: He demanded that UN Secretary-General U Thant withdraw all UNEF peacekeepers from Sinai
8. What action by President Nasser on 22 May 1967 was treated by Israel as an unambiguous act of war (casus belli)?
Ans: The closure of the Straits of Tiran to all Israeli shipping and strategic goods
9. What pact signed in Cairo on 30 May 1967 convinced the Israeli cabinet that they were facing an imminent, coordinated three-front invasion?
Ans: A joint mutual defense pact signed between President Nasser and King Hussein of Jordan
10. Who was appointed Minister of Defense in Israel's emergency national unity government on 1 June 1967, electrifying public confidence?
Ans: General Moshe Dayan
11. What was Operation Focus (Moked) launched by the Israeli Air Force at 7:45 AM on Monday, 5 June 1967?
Ans: A devastating pre-emptive airstrike that destroyed over 300 Egyptian combat aircraft on the ground in under three hours
12. Why did Jordan enter the Six Day War on 5 June 1967 despite Israeli Prime Minister Eshkol sending a message urging King Hussein to stay out?
Ans: Nasser misled King Hussein by falsely claiming that Egyptian air strikes had destroyed Israeli airfields and that his army was marching on Tel Aviv
13. On what historic date did Israeli paratroopers capture the Old City of Jerusalem and reach the Western Wall for the first time in 19 years?
Ans: 7 June 1967
14. What geographical feature marked the final line of Israeli advance in the south after routing the Egyptian army in the Sinai Peninsula?
Ans: The east bank of the Suez Canal
15. What steep, heavily fortified plateau did the IDF storm on 9–10 June 1967 to silence Syrian artillery bombardments of northern kibbutzim?
Ans: The Golan Heights
16. What controversial maritime tragedy occurred on 8 June 1967 when Israeli jets and torpedo boats mistakenly attacked a neutral American vessel?
Ans: The USS Liberty incident
17. How much territory did Israel control following its stunning victory in the Six Day War compared to its pre-war boundaries?
Ans: It controlled over three times more territory than it had before the war
18. Approximately how many Arab casualties were suffered in the Six Day War compared to Israeli losses?
Ans: Around 20,000 Arab soldiers were killed, compared to fewer than 1,000 Israeli soldiers
19. What psychological impact did the Six Day War have on the Israeli public and religious communities?
Ans: A wave of national euphoria, messianic fervor, and the conviction that Israel's military might guaranteed permanent security
20. What term is used in the Arab world to describe the catastrophe and humiliation of the 1967 Six Day War defeat?
Ans: An-Naksah (The Setback)
21. What famous formula did the Arab League adopt at the Khartoum Summit on 1 September 1967?
Ans: The 'Three Noes': No peace, No recognition, No negotiations with Israel
22. What fundamental principle for resolving the Arab-Israeli conflict was established by UN Security Council Resolution 242 in November 1967?
Ans: 'Land for Peace'—Israeli withdrawal from occupied territories in exchange for recognized, secure borders
23. What crucial semantic ambiguity in the English text of UN Resolution 242 led to decades of competing legal interpretations?
Ans: It called for Israeli withdrawal from 'territories occupied' rather than 'THE territories occupied'
24. What major waterway was completely shut down from June 1967 until 1975 because Egypt scuttled ships and laid mines to block traffic?
Ans: The Suez Canal
25. Approximately how many additional Palestinian Arabs were displaced as refugees during the Six Day War, many for the second time?
Ans: Around 300,000
26. What was the 'Allon Plan', formulated by Israeli Deputy Prime Minister Yigal Allon in 1967?
Ans: A strategic proposal to annex a defensible security perimeter in the Jordan Valley while returning populated Arab areas to Jordanian civil control
27. What battle on 21 March 1968 between the IDF and Palestinian fedayeen in Jordan became a massive symbolic victory for Yasser Arafat's Fatah movement?
Ans: The Battle of Karameh
28. Who was elected Chairman of the PLO Executive Committee in February 1969, fundamentally shifting the organization away from Arab state control?
Ans: Yasser Arafat
29. Which radical Marxist-Leninist Palestinian faction, founded by George Habash, pioneered international aircraft hijackings to publicize the Palestinian cause?
Ans: The Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine (PFLP)
30. What dramatic event in September 1970 saw the PFLP hijack four Western airliners and blow up three of them on an abandoned airstrip in the Jordanian desert?
Ans: The Dawson's Field hijackings

Official Mark Scheme & Knowledge Reference · Answers 31 to 60

Mark Scheme 2
31. What was 'Black September' in 1970?
Ans: A brutal civil war in Jordan where King Hussein deployed the army to crush Palestinian militias and expel the PLO from the country
32. Where did Yasser Arafat and the PLO relocate their main headquarters and military bases after their expulsion from Jordan in 1970–71?
Ans: Lebanon
33. What terrorist atrocity was carried out by the Palestinian 'Black September' faction at the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich?
Ans: The kidnapping and murder of 11 Israeli Olympic athletes and coaches
34. What was Operation Wrath of God authorized by Israeli Prime Minister Golda Meir in the wake of the Munich massacre?
Ans: A covert Mossad assassination campaign across Europe and the Middle East targeting individuals involved in the Munich attack
35. What was the 'War of Attrition' fought between Egypt and Israel from 1967 to 1970 along the Suez Canal?
Ans: A grinding static war of heavy artillery duels, commando raids, and aerial dogfights designed to exhaust Israel's military resolve
36. Which superpower directly intervened to protect Egypt during the War of Attrition, deploying advanced anti-aircraft missiles and flying combat missions?
Ans: The Soviet Union
37. Who succeeded Gamal Abdel Nasser as President of Egypt following his sudden death from a heart attack in September 1970?
Ans: Anwar Sadat
38. What religious nationalist Israeli movement was founded in 1974 to spearhead Jewish settlement across the West Bank (Judea and Samaria)?
Ans: Gush Emunim (Bloc of the Faithful)
39. How did the 1967 conquests transform Israel's domestic economy in the 1970s?
Ans: It created a construction boom and brought over 100,000 Palestinian day-labourers from the occupied territories into low-wage jobs in Israel
40. What did the Palestinian National Charter, amended by the PLO in 1968, explicitly declare regarding the State of Israel?
Ans: That the establishment of Israel was entirely illegal and that armed struggle was the only way to liberate all of Palestine
41. Why was Anwar Sadat determined to launch a military offensive against Israel in 1973?
Ans: To break the diplomatic stalemate ('no war, no peace') and force the superpowers to pressure Israel into returning the Sinai
42. What bold geopolitical gamble did Sadat take in July 1972 that astonished the world and misled Israeli intelligence?
Ans: He expelled 15,000 Soviet military advisers and technicians from Egypt
43. Which Arab leader did Sadat ally with to plan a synchronized two-front surprise offensive against Israel in October 1973?
Ans: President Hafez al-Assad of Syria
44. What was the 'Conceptzia' (The Concept)—the fatal flaw in Israeli military intelligence prior to the 1973 War?
Ans: The unshakeable conviction that Egypt would never attack without long-range strike aircraft, and Syria would never attack without Egypt
45. On what date and holy occasion did Egypt and Syria launch their simultaneous surprise assault against Israel?
Ans: 6 October 1973, on Yom Kippur (the Day of Atonement) and during Muslim Ramadan
46. What formidable defensive barrier along the Suez Canal, built by Israel at a cost of $300 million, was breached by Egyptian engineers in October 1973?
Ans: The Bar-Lev Line
47. How did Egyptian engineering units ingeniously blast through the massive sand ramparts of the Bar-Lev Line within hours?
Ans: By using high-pressure water cannons adapted from British and German fire-fighting pumps
48. What Soviet-supplied weapon devastated Israeli tank counter-attacks in the opening days of the 1973 war?
Ans: The AT-3 'Sagger' wire-guided anti-tank missile
49. Why was the Israeli Air Force initially unable to provide close air support to troops on the Suez Canal and Golan Heights in 1973?
Ans: Because of Egypt and Syria's dense Soviet-built mobile surface-to-air missile (SAM) umbrella (SAM-6 and Shilka anti-aircraft guns)
50. What desperate armored engagement on the Golan Heights saw fewer than 100 Israeli Centurion tanks hold off 500 Syrian T-55 and T-62 tanks for three days?
Ans: The Battle of the Valley of Tears
51. What crucial strategic error did Anwar Sadat make on 14 October 1973, against the urgent advice of his Chief of Staff General Saad El Shazly?
Ans: He ordered Egyptian armored divisions to advance into the open desert beyond the protective cover of their SAM missile umbrella
52. Who led the audacious Israeli counter-crossing of the Suez Canal on 15–16 October 1973 (Operation Stouthearted Men)?
Ans: Major General Ariel Sharon
53. What dramatic military position had Israeli forces achieved on the western side of the Suez Canal by the end of the war?
Ans: They had completely encircled the Egyptian Third Army (20,000 men) and cut the road to Cairo
54. What was Operation Nickel Grass ordered by US President Richard Nixon on 12 October 1973?
Ans: A massive emergency military airlift flying 22,000 tons of tanks, ammunition, and precision missiles to Israel
55. What devastating economic weapon did the Organization of Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries (OAPEC) unleash during the 1973 War?
Ans: An oil production cut and full embargo against the US and Netherlands, causing global oil prices to quadruple
56. What tense Cold War standoff occurred on 24–25 October 1973 as the war drew to a close?
Ans: Soviet leader Brezhnev threatened unilateral military intervention to save the Egyptian Third Army, prompting the US to place its nuclear forces on DEFCON 3
57. What landmark United Nations Security Council Resolution, passed on 22 October 1973, called for an immediate ceasefire and the direct implementation of Resolution 242?
Ans: Resolution 338
58. What judicial inquiry in Israel investigated the government and military's catastrophic lack of preparedness for the 1973 War?
Ans: The Agranat Commission
59. What political fallout occurred in Israel in the months following the release of the Agranat Commission report in 1974?
Ans: Prime Minister Golda Meir and Defense Minister Moshe Dayan were forced to resign amid massive public outrage
60. What profound psychological and diplomatic breakthrough did the 1973 Yom Kippur War achieve for the Middle East?
Ans: It shattered Israel's assumption that Arab armies would never fight effectively, and restored Egyptian national honor, making peace talks as equals possible

One consequence was that it wrecked secret cooperative security relations between King Hussein of Jordan and Israel. On 13 November 1966, following a Fatah landmine, the IDF launched a massive punitive assault on the West Bank village of Samu, destroying 125 houses and killing 15 Jordanian soldiers. Riots erupted across Jordan demanding King Hussein’s overthrow, forcing Hussein to sign a mutual defence pact with Egypt in May 1967, ensuring Jordan would enter the Six Day War.
2. 8-Mark Importance Planning Grid: Explain the importance of the PFLP airplane hijacks of 1970 for international attitudes towards the Palestine issue.
Core Factor Factual Information (Names, Dates, Treaties) Direct Link to Named Outcome
The Dawson’s Field hijacks forced the Palestinian national cause onto the front pages of world media, but alienated Western public opinion. In September 1970, George Habash’s PFLP hijacked four airliners, landing three in Dawson’s Field in Jordan, holding over 300 Western hostages, and blowing up empty aircraft on global TV. While it pressured Western powers to acknowledge that Middle East peace required addressing Palestinian identity, it triggered Black September and led Western governments to brand Palestinian guerrilla groups as terrorist organizations.
3. 8-Mark Narrative Storyboard Grid: Write a narrative account analysing the actions of Nasser, the USSR, and the USA leading to the Six Day War (1967).

On 13 May, the USSR falsely warned Nasser that Israel was massing brigades on the Syrian border; to maintain his leadership, Nasser mobilized 100,000 troops in Sinai and expelled UN peacekeepers. On 22 May, Nasser closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping (an explicit Israeli casus belli) and signed a military pact with King Hussein of Jordan on 30 May, completing Israel’s encirclement. The USA refused to intervene militarily to open the Straits, signaling to Israeli Prime Minister Levi Eshkol and Moshe Dayan that Washington would not oppose an immediate pre-emptive strike.

• The 1967 vs 1973 Territorial Shift Israel conquered the Occupied Territories (Sinai, Gaza Strip, West Bank, East Jerusalem, Golan Heights) in the 1967 Six Day War, NOT in 1973. In 1973, borders remained essentially unchanged following the UN ceasefire.
• The Wording of UN Resolution 242 In UN Resolution 242 (1967), the English text intentionally called for Israeli withdrawal from "territories occupied" rather than "THE territories occupied". This deliberate ambiguity allowed Israel to claim it was not required to surrender all land.
• Israeli Intelligence and "The Conception" The catastrophic surprise of the 1973 Yom Kippur War was rooted in "The Conception" (Aman hubris) — the false Israeli assumption that Egypt would never attack without long-range strike aircraft capable of neutralizing Israeli airfields.
