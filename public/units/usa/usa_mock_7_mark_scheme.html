Question 2: Causal Explanation (12 Marks)
Target: AO1/AO2 (12 marks). Level 4 (10–12 marks): An analytical explanation showing a line of reasoning with clear links to the outcome, supported by accurate, detailed knowledge.
“One reason why the Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955–56) was successful was the outstanding local organisation by the Montgomery Improvement Association (MIA).” Instead of using buses, the MIA organised a complex and highly effective carpool system involving over 300 vehicles, alongside reduced-fare taxi rides from Black drivers, to ensure that Black workers could still travel to their jobs. This was kickstarted by Jo Ann Robinson distributing 35,000 leaflets. Therefore, the boycott was successful because this logistical network allowed the Black community to sustain the boycott for 381 days without being starved into submission by the city's authorities. “Another reason why it was successful was the severe economic pressure the boycott placed on local authorities and businesses.” Because Black Americans made up roughly 70% of bus riders in Montgomery, their year-long refusal to pay fares threatened the local bus company with bankruptcy and damaged downtown merchants. This solidarity was solidified by the respect for Rosa Parks, whose arrest united the community. Therefore, this led to success because the city authorities simply could not afford to maintain segregation if it meant the total financial ruin of their transport system. “A final reason for its success was the parallel legal strategy that reached the highest courts.” While the boycott applied economic pressure on the streets, the NAACP successfully took the case to the federal courts. In 1956, the US Supreme Court upheld the ruling in Browder v. Gayle, definitively declaring that segregation on Montgomery buses was unconstitutional based on the precedent set by the Brown v. Topeka case. Therefore, the boycott ultimately succeeded because the Supreme Court forced the state of Alabama to legally change its discriminatory laws, overriding local resistance. In conclusion, the boycott was successful because the sheer economic endurance and organisation of the grassroots movement gave civil rights lawyers the time and leverage needed to win the ultimate legal victory in the Supreme Court.

Question 3(d): Evaluation of Interpretation (16 Marks + 4 SPaG)
Target: AO4 (16 marks) + SPaG (4 marks). Level 4 (13–16 marks): Balanced discussion analyzing both interpretations with sustained contextual knowledge, leading to a justified judgment.
I agree to a large extent with Interpretation 2 that President Johnson was the president most responsible for the combat escalation of the war. After the 1964 Gulf of Tonkin incident, Johnson secured the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution, which gave him a blank check to wage war. As broadcast in Source C, Johnson ordered additional units to Vietnam, raising fighting strength to 125,000 troops, and increased the number of men called up by the draft system. This transformed Vietnam from an advisory mission into an active combat war, supporting Interpretation 2's view that Johnson was "primarily responsible for converting" the conflict into an "active combat war". However, Interpretation 1 is also valid in identifying Kennedy's administration as the initial, critical turning point. As detailed in the declassified memo in Source B, Kennedy approved transport helicopters, reconnaissance, and military and administrative advisers. Under Kennedy, advisers rose from under 1,000 to over 16,000 by 1963, and he approved the use of chemical defoliants like Agent Orange. This supports Interpretation 1's argument that Kennedy's 1961 decisions were a "key phase in the escalation" that "marked a key phase" in expanding the US role. Overall, while Kennedy made the crucial decisions that committed the US militarily (Interpretation 1), it was Johnson who took the decisive step of initiating a direct, large-scale American ground war (Interpretation 2). Until Johnson deployed combat troops and began bombing North Vietnam, the US role was theoretically advisory. Therefore, Interpretation 2 is the stronger explanation because Johnson's policies fundamentally changed the nature of the conflict into an American war.
