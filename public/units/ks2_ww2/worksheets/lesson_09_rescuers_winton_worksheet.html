Historical Reading • The Prague Rescue Operation

In December 1938, a 29-year-old London stockbroker named Nicholas Winton cancelled his Swiss ski holiday to visit Prague. He saw thousands of terrified refugees living in freezing conditions after Nazi Germany occupied the Sudetenland.

Winton decided he could not stand by as a passive bystander. He joined forces with Trevor Chadwick (a schoolteacher from Swanage, Dorset) and Doreen Warriner. While Chadwick risked his life dealing with the Gestapo in Prague to secure exit permits, Winton worked day and night in London finding British foster families, persuading the Home Office, and raising £50 per child. Together, they saved 669 children.

Task 3: The 50-Year Secret • The Scrapbook in the Attic Historical Empathy & Character

The Book That Stood Silent for Half a Century

For 50 years after the war, Nicholas Winton never told a single person about the 669 children he saved—not even his own wife, Grete. In 1988, Grete was tidying their attic when she discovered a dusty leather scrapbook containing lists of names, photographs, and letters from grateful parents. When asked why he kept silent for so long, Winton simply replied that the war was over and there was no reason to boast about doing what anyone should have done.

What does Winton's 50-year silence reveal about his true motivations and character?
