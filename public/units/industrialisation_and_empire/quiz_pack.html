25. ►
The People’s Charter (1838) — The Charter demanded six key political reforms to turn Britain from an aristocratic oligarchy into a true representative democracy.
[✓] [✗]
26. ►
Universal male suffrage and the Secret Ballot (or: Payment for MPs, Equal Constituencies, Abolition of Property Qualifications, Annual Parliaments) — These reforms aimed to ensure that ordinary working men could stand for Parliament and vote without intimidation from landlords.
[✓] [✗]
27. ►►

Wealthy ruling elites feared that giving working men the vote would lead to the confiscation of private property and wealth redistribution — MPs viewed Chartism as dangerous socialism, arresting leaders and deploying thousands of special constables to disperse the 1848 Kennington Common rally.

Unit Conceptual Foundations • 4 Core Anchors
Urbanisation: The massive population shift from rural countryside villages to industrial towns and cities, driven by factory employment and enclosure.
Laissez-Faire: The economic doctrine that governments should not interfere in the free market, wages, or factory working hours, resisting early safety reforms.
Chartism: The mass working-class political movement (1838–1848) demanding democratic parliamentary reform through the six points of the People’s Charter.
Miasma Theory: The incorrect medical belief that epidemic diseases like cholera were caused by inhaling poisonous airborne vapors from rotting filth.
