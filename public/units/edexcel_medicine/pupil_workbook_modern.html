Living Unit Timeline: Modern Medicine (Part I: 1900–1940)

Chronological Framework • Magic Bullets & Early Antibiotics
1901 Landsteiner Discovers Blood Groups
Karl Landsteiner identifies A, B, and O blood groups, transforming blood transfusions from fatal gambles into predictable surgical lifelines.
1909 Ehrlich & Hata Discover Salvarsan 606
Paul Ehrlich and Sahachiro Hata discover arsphenamine (compound 606), humanity’s first synthetic chemical ‘magic bullet’ curing syphilis.
1928 Alexander Fleming Discovers Penicillin
Fleming observes a clear zone of destroyed staphylococci around Penicillium notatum mold at St Mary’s Hospital, Paddington, London.
1932 Domagk Discovers Prontosil
Gerhard Domagk proves the red dye Prontosil cures streptococcal blood poisoning, establishing the sulfonamide class of antibacterial drugs.
• Living Timeline Sketchpad & Causal Synthesis (1900–1940) Sketch Ehrlich’s Salvarsan vial, Domagk’s Prontosil, or Fleming’s contaminated staphylococcus petri dish.
2/14 The 20th century transformed pharmacology from toxic general poisons into targeted magic bullets.

Living Unit Timeline: Modern Medicine (Part II: 1940–present)

Chronological Framework • Mass Antibiotics, The NHS, DNA & Oncology
1944 Mass Production of Penicillin for D-Day
Following Florey and Heatley’s US mission to Peoria, deep-tank fermentation yields 2.3 million doses of penicillin for the Normandy landings.
1948 Aneurin Bevan Launches the NHS
On 5 July 1948, the National Health Service opens at Park Hospital, Manchester, providing comprehensive healthcare free at the point of delivery.
1953 Watson & Crick Discover DNA Double Helix
Using Rosalind Franklin’s Photograph 51, James Watson and Francis Crick deduce complementary base pairing, unlocking the molecular secret of life.
1971 / 2003 CT Scanner (1971) & Human Genome (2003)
Hounsfield’s EMI CT scanner eliminates exploratory surgery; the Human Genome Project maps all 3 billion chemical base pairs in human DNA.
