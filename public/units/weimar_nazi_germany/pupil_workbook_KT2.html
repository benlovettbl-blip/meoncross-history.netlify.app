•Political intrigue: Elections of 1932 (230 seats), fall of Papen & Schleicher, Hitler appointed Chancellor (30 Jan 1933)

FEB 1920 • The 25-Point Programme & Foundation of NSDAP Key Topic 2.1

Hitler and Anton Drexler publish the 25-Point Programme in Munich. It demands the abolition of Versailles, creation of a Greater Germany, and denial of German citizenship to Jews.

8–9 NOV 1923 • The Munich Putsch (Beer Hall Putsch) Crushed Key Topic 2.1

Hitler and General Ludendorff attempt to seize power in Munich with 2,000 armed SA men. The Bavarian police fire on the marchers at the Odeonsplatz, killing 16 Nazis. Hitler is arrested and tried for high treason.

FEB 1926 • The Bamberg Conference Secures the Führerprinzip Key Topic 2.2

Hitler summons party leaders to Bamberg. He crushes the northern socialist wing led by Gregor Strasser, securing unchallenged authority as supreme Führer and focusing on rural, anti-communist voters.

2/20 "Anton Drexler founded the DAP; Hitler joined as member 555 and stole the entire show."

Living Timeline • Part 2: Chronological Anchors (Key Topic 2)

Instructions: Complete the dual-coding sketches to permanently anchor these chronological turning points in long-term memory.
29 OCT 1929 • Wall Street Crash Triggers the Great Depression Key Topic 2.3

The US stock market collapses; American banks immediately call in their short-term loans to Germany. German factories close, banks fail, and unemployment surges from 1.3 million to over 6 million.

JUL 1932 • NSDAP Becomes Largest Party in the Reichstag Key Topic 2.3

Amidst mass poverty and street clashes, the Nazis win 230 seats (37.3% of the vote) in the July 1932 election. Hitler demands the Chancellorship, but President von Hindenburg refuses to appoint the "Bohemian corporal".

30 JAN 1933 • Hitler Appointed Chancellor via Backstairs Intrigue Key Topic 2.4

Convinced by Franz von Papen that Hitler can be controlled in a coalition with only three Nazi ministers, President Hindenburg reluctantly appoints Adolf Hitler as Chancellor of Germany.

Question 3(a): Source Utility [8 marks • 12 mins]

SOURCE B
Presidential Emergency Decree under Article 48, Berlin, December 1931
From an official emergency decree signed by President Paul von Hindenburg and Chancellor Heinrich Brüning, published in the German legal gazette, December 1931.
“On the basis of Article 48 of the Reich Constitution, the following is decreed to secure the economy and finances: All public sector wages and salaries are reduced by ten percent. Unemployment relief payments are strictly curtailed. Prices of essential goods are reduced by decree.” By ruling through presidential decree rather than parliament, Chancellor Brüning became known as the ‘Hunger Chancellor’, driving millions of desperate Germans towards radical political parties.
SOURCE C
Contemporary photograph of Nazi SA Stormtroopers, Berlin, July 1932
From a press photograph taken during the July 1932 Reichstag election campaign in Berlin. It shows uniformed SA stormtroopers parading in formation with swastika flags.
By July 1932, the SA numbered over 400,000 men—four times the size of the regular German Army. Their disciplined parades, martial brass bands, and street brawls with the Communist Rotfrontkämpferbund presented an image of dynamic youth, strength, and order to middle-class voters terrified of Bolshevik revolution during the Great Depression.

HISTORICAL INTERPRETATIONS 1 AND 2 Target: How and why historians differ (AO4)
INTERPRETATION 1
From an account by historian William Carr, A History of Germany 1815–1990 (1991)
“The Nazi Party grew into a mass movement primarily because the Great Depression shattered the economic and social foundations of the Weimar Republic. With six million Germans unemployed by 1932, millions of desperate middle-class voters, shopkeepers, and farmers turned to the NSDAP as the only radical alternative to Communism. In a democracy that had collapsed into economic paralysis, it was the despair produced by mass unemployment and Brüning’s wage-cutting policies that drove voters in their millions to support Hitler.”
INTERPRETATION 2
From an account by historian Richard J. Evans, The Coming of the Third Reich (2003)
“The rapid growth of Nazi support between 1929 and 1932 cannot be explained solely by economic distress. Other nations suffered mass unemployment without turning to fascism. What set the Nazis apart was their brilliant modern electioneering and Hitler’s magnetic personal appeal. Goebbels orchestrated dynamic campaigns using aeroplanes, radio, and mass rallies, while the uniformed SA stormtroopers provided an intoxicating image of discipline and order on the streets. It was Nazi propaganda and paramilitary energy that captivated voters.”
