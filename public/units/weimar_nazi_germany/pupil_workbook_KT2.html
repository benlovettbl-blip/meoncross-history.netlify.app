FEB 1920 • The 25-Point Programme & Foundation of NSDAP Key Topic 2.1

Hitler and Anton Drexler publish the 25-Point Programme in Munich. It demands the abolition of Versailles, creation of a Greater Germany, and denial of German citizenship to Jews.

8–9 NOV 1923 • The Munich Putsch (Beer Hall Putsch) Crushed Key Topic 2.1

Hitler and General Ludendorff attempt to seize power in Munich with 2,000 armed SA men. The Bavarian police fire on the marchers at the Odeonsplatz, killing 16 Nazis. Hitler is arrested and tried for high treason.

FEB 1926 • The Bamberg Conference Secures the Führerprinzip Key Topic 2.2

Hitler summons party leaders to Bamberg. He crushes the northern socialist wing led by Gregor Strasser, securing unchallenged authority as supreme Führer and focusing on rural, anti-communist voters.

29 OCT 1929 • Wall Street Crash Triggers the Great Depression Key Topic 2.3

The US stock market collapses; American banks immediately call in their short-term loans to Germany. German factories close, banks fail, and unemployment surges from 1.3 million to over 6 million.

JUL 1932 • NSDAP Becomes Largest Party in the Reichstag Key Topic 2.3

Amidst mass poverty and street clashes, the Nazis win 230 seats (37.3% of the vote) in the July 1932 election. Hitler demands the Chancellorship, but President von Hindenburg refuses to appoint the "Bohemian corporal".

30 JAN 1933 • Hitler Appointed Chancellor via Backstairs Intrigue Key Topic 2.4

Convinced by Franz von Papen that Hitler can be controlled in a coalition with only three Nazi ministers, President Hindenburg reluctantly appoints Adolf Hitler as Chancellor of Germany.

Archival & Conceptual Blueprint • The Nazi Rise: Electoral Breakthrough & Political Intrigue

1. The Electoral Breakthrough: From Fringe Extremism to Largest Party (1928–1932)
📊 May 1928 Reichstag Election: 12 seats (2.6% vote). Stresemann’s economic prosperity meant fringe extremism failed to gain traction among working or middle classes.
📊 September 1930 Election (Post-Wall Street Crash): 107 seats (18.3% vote). Dramatic breakthrough: mass unemployment (3 million) and coalition collapse propelled Nazis into the second largest party.
📊 July 1932 Election (Depression Peak): 230 seats (37.3% vote). The absolute peak of Nazi electoral power: 13.7 million Germans voted NSDAP, making them the undisputed largest party.
📊 November 1932 Election (Decline & Crisis): 196 seats (33.1% vote). Nazis lost 2 million votes and 34 seats; party funds exhausted. Proved Hitler had to be installed via political intrigue before support slipped away.
2. The Backstairs Intrigue: The Fatal Conspirators of January 1933
PAUL VON HINDENBURG 84-year-old Junker Field Marshal and President. Despised Hitler as a "vulgar Bohemian corporal", but was exhausted by parliamentary paralysis and persuaded by his inner circle.
FRANZ VON PAPEN Aristocratic former Chancellor. Conspired against his rival Schleicher; convinced Hindenburg that Hitler could be tamed in a coalition with 8 conservative ministers: "We have framed him in!"
KURT VON SCHLEICHER Army general and Chancellor (Dec 1932 – Jan 1933). Attempted to split the Nazi party by offering Gregor Strasser the Vice-Chancellorship, alienating both Hitler and Hindenburg.
12/16 "July 1932 Election: Nazis won 230 seats and became the largest party in the German Reichstag."

Master Knowledge Organiser • Key Topic 2: Hitler’s Rise to Power, 1919–33

1. Core Disciplinary Vocabulary & Conceptual Definitions
• 25-Point Programme: The original 1920 NSDAP manifesto demanding Versailles abolition, Greater Germany, and denial of Jewish citizenship.
• The SA (Brownshirts): Paramilitary stormtroopers led by Ernst Röhm; used violence and intimidation to dominate German streets.
• Munich Putsch (1923): Hitler’s failed armed attempt to overthrow the Bavarian government; resulted in 16 deaths and Hitler’s imprisonment.
• Mein Kampf: Hitler’s ideological autobiography written in Landsberg Prison setting out Lebensraum, Aryan supremacy, and anti-Semitism.
• Bamberg Conference (1926): Meeting where Hitler crushed the socialist Strasser wing, establishing the absolute Führerprinzip over the party.
• Wall Street Crash (1929): US stock market collapse that triggered the Great Depression, throwing 6 million Germans into unemployment.
• Negative Cohesion: People voting for the Nazis not out of love for Hitler, but from shared hatred of Communism, Weimar chaos, and Versailles.
• Backstairs Intrigue: The secretive political scheming between Papen and Hindenburg in Jan 1933 that appointed Hitler Chancellor.
2. Essential Chronology
• Feb 1920: 25-Point Programme published; DAP becomes NSDAP
• 8–9 Nov 1923: Munich Putsch fails; 16 Nazis killed at Odeonsplatz
• Feb 1924: Hitler’s treason trial gives him national platform
• Feb 1926: Bamberg Conference establishes Führerprinzip
• May 1928: Nazis win only 12 Reichstag seats in "Golden Years"
• 29 Oct 1929: Wall Street Crash launches Great Depression
• Mar 1930: Brüning appointed Chancellor; begins rule by Article 48
• Jul 1932: Nazis win 230 seats, becoming largest party
• Nov 1932: Nazi vote slips to 196 seats; party near bankruptcy
• 30 Jan 1933: Hitler appointed Chancellor by President Hindenburg
3. Key Historical Figures Matrix
• Adolf Hitler: Leader of the NSDAP; brilliant orator who transformed a Munich fringe group into Germany’s largest party.
• Anton Drexler: Munich railway locksmith who founded the German Workers’ Party (DAP) in 1919; sidelined by Hitler.
• Ernst Röhm: Brutal ex-soldier who organised and commanded the 400,000-man paramilitary SA (Sturmabteilung).
• Paul von Hindenburg: Imperial Field Marshal and President of Germany (1925–34); reluctantly appointed Hitler Chancellor.
• Franz von Papen: Aristocratic politician who brokered the Jan 1933 deal with Hitler, falsely boasting he could control him.
• Dr Joseph Goebbels: Appointed Nazi propaganda chief in 1930; pioneered modern mass rallies, posters, and radio techniques.
"Backstairs Intrigue: Papen believed he could hire Hitler as Chancellor and 'push him into a corner'." 13/16

Grade 9 Exam Technique Masterclass • Pearson Edexcel Paper 3 Rubrics

1. The 4/4 Mark Formula: Inference from Source A [4 Marks • 5 Mins] TARGET: 4 / 4
Mark Scheme Law: Two separate inferences required. Each inference must state: (1) what you can deduce beyond surface description + (2) a verbatim quote or precise detail from Source A supporting your deduction. Never quote without explaining what it means!
2. The 12/12 Mark Formula: Explain Why [12 Marks • 18 Mins] TARGET: 11–12 / 12 (LEVEL 4)
Three Linked PEEL Paragraphs: Use both provided stimulus bullet points + bring in ONE distinct self-selected knowledge point. Every paragraph must feature causal links: "Consequently, As a direct consequence, This meant that..." Connect your factors: explain how economic crisis enabled political radicalism!
3. The 8/8 Mark Formula: How Useful are Sources B & C [8 Marks • 12 Mins] TARGET: 7–8 / 8 (LEVEL 3)
COP Formula (Content, Origin, Purpose): Evaluate Source B then Source C. Interrogate content using own knowledge; evaluate provenance (who wrote it, when, why, and who was the intended audience). Never dismiss a source as "biased"; explain how bias makes it useful as evidence of propaganda or fear!
4. The 16/16 Mark Formula: Evaluative Essay [16 Marks + 4 SPaG • 25 Mins] TARGET: 15–16 / 16 (LEVEL 4)
Criteria-Led Judgment Architecture:
• Opening: State your sustained thesis and establish your evaluative criteria (e.g. legal illusion vs daily social reality).
• Section 1: Support the targeted Interpretation using its quotes and your own historical evidence.
• Section 2: Evaluate the competing Interpretation using counter-evidence from your own knowledge.
• Conclusion: Deliver a justified verdict explaining why one historian’s emphasis is more persuasive.
14/16 "30 January 1933: Hindenburg reluctantly appointed Hitler; the Weimar democracy committed suicide."
