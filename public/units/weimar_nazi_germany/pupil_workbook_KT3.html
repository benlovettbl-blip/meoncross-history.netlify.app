27 FEB 1933 • The Reichstag Fire & Emergency Powers Decree Key Topic 3.1

The Reichstag building in Berlin is destroyed by arson; Dutch Communist Marinus van der Lubbe is arrested. Hitler uses the fire to persuade Hindenburg to sign the Reichstag Fire Decree, suspending freedom of speech, assembly, and press.

23 MAR 1933 • Passage of the Enabling Act (Dictatorship Established) Key Topic 3.1

Surrounded by armed SS and SA stormtroopers chanting intimidation at the Kroll Opera House, the Reichstag votes 444 to 94 to pass the Enabling Act. Hitler gains total legal power to enact laws without parliament or president for four years.

30 JUN 1934 • The Night of the Long Knives (SA Purge) Key Topic 3.1

Fearing the 2-million-strong SA under Ernst Röhm wanted a "second revolution", Hitler unleashes SS execution squads. Over 400 political rivals, including Röhm, Gregor Strasser, and former Chancellor Schleicher, are murdered without trial.

Archival & Conceptual Blueprint • The Totalitarian Police State & Propaganda Architecture

1. The Nazi Terror Apparatus: The Three Pillars of Police Control
🛡️ The SS (Schutzstaffel) — Led by Heinrich Himmler: Originally Hitler’s 200-man bodyguard; grew into a 250,000-man racial elite army. Controlled all German police, intelligence (SD), and the Concentration Camp system (Death’s Head Units / Totenkopfverbände).
🕵️ The Gestapo (Secret State Police): Plainclothes detectives with absolute power to arrest suspects without trial, tap telephones, and interrogate using torture. Depended on a vast network of civilian denunciations and block wardens.
⚖️ The Coordinated Legal System (The People’s Court): All judges forced to join the Nazi League. Juries abolished; judge Roland Freisler handed out thousands of death sentences for "treasonable grumbling" or anti-Nazi jokes.
2. The Ministry of Propaganda: Complete Media Domination (Joseph Goebbels)
RADIO (VOLKSEMPFÄNGER) 70% of homes owned a subsidized People’s Receiver by 1939. Radios were deliberately engineered to block British BBC broadcasts, broadcasting Goebbels’ speeches directly into living rooms.
MONUMENTAL RALLIES Annual Nuremberg Rallies gathered 500,000 marching stormtroopers under the "Cathedral of Light" searchlights, hypnotising crowds with theatrical grandeur and military power.
CENSORSHIP & ART Daily briefings told journalists what headlines to print. Over 20,000 Jewish and socialist books burned in May 1933; Degenerate Art (modernism) banned in favour of heroic Aryan realism.

Master Knowledge Organiser • Key Topic 3: Nazi Control and Dictatorship, 1933–39

1. Core Disciplinary Vocabulary & Conceptual Definitions
• Reichstag Fire Decree: Emergency decree of 28 Feb 1933 suspending civil liberties, personal freedom, and freedom of speech across Germany.
• The Enabling Act (1933): Law passed on 23 March 1933 granting Hitler dictatorial power to pass laws without the Reichstag or President.
• Gleichschaltung: The total coordination and Nazification of all German institutions, trade unions, and political parties under state control.
• Night of the Long Knives: Bloody purge on 30 June 1934 where the SS murdered Ernst Röhm and over 400 SA rivals, winning army loyalty.
• The SS (Schutzstaffel): Heinrich Himmler’s elite blackshirt organisation that controlled all police, intelligence, and concentration camps.
• The Gestapo: The secret state police operating without judicial oversight, using denunciations and torture to crush political dissent.
• The Reich Concordat (1933): Treaty with Pope Pius XI guaranteeing Catholic Church rights in exchange for priests staying out of German politics.
• Volksempfänger: Cheap mass-produced "People’s Receiver" radio designed to flood millions of German households with Nazi propaganda.
2. Essential Chronology
• 27 Feb 1933: Reichstag Fire blamed on Communist Van der Lubbe
• 28 Feb 1933: Decree for Protection of People & State suspends rights
• 23 Mar 1933: Enabling Act passed, ending Weimar democracy
• 2 May 1933: Trade unions abolished; replaced by Nazi DAF
• 14 Jul 1933: Law Against Formation of Parties outlaws all rivals
• 30 Jun 1934: Night of the Long Knives eliminates Röhm and SA leaders
• 2 Aug 1934: Hindenburg dies; Hitler combines offices as Führer
• Aug 1934: German Armed Forces swear personal oath of loyalty to Hitler
• Aug 1936: Berlin Olympic Games showcase Nazi regime to the world
• 1937: Pope Pius XI issues Mit brennender Sorge encyclical condemning Nazis
3. Key Historical Figures Matrix
• Adolf Hitler: Dictator and Führer of Germany; combined Chancellor and President roles to establish absolute totalitarian rule.
• Heinrich Himmler: Reichsführer-SS; built the SS, Gestapo, and concentration camp system into an all-powerful terror state.

Grade 9 Exam Technique Masterclass • Pearson Edexcel Paper 3 Rubrics

1. The 4/4 Mark Formula: Inference from Source A [4 Marks • 5 Mins] TARGET: 4 / 4
Mark Scheme Law: Two separate inferences required. Each inference must state: (1) what you can deduce beyond surface description + (2) a verbatim quote or precise detail from Source A supporting your deduction. Never quote without explaining what it means!
2. The 12/12 Mark Formula: Explain Why [12 Marks • 18 Mins] TARGET: 11–12 / 12 (LEVEL 4)
Three Linked PEEL Paragraphs: Use both provided stimulus bullet points + bring in ONE distinct self-selected knowledge point. Every paragraph must feature causal links: "Consequently, As a direct consequence, This meant that..." Connect your factors: explain how economic crisis enabled political radicalism!
3. The 8/8 Mark Formula: How Useful are Sources B & C [8 Marks • 12 Mins] TARGET: 7–8 / 8 (LEVEL 3)
COP Formula (Content, Origin, Purpose): Evaluate Source B then Source C. Interrogate content using own knowledge; evaluate provenance (who wrote it, when, why, and who was the intended audience). Never dismiss a source as "biased"; explain how bias makes it useful as evidence of propaganda or fear!
4. The 16/16 Mark Formula: Evaluative Essay [16 Marks + 4 SPaG • 25 Mins] TARGET: 15–16 / 16 (LEVEL 4)
Criteria-Led Judgment Architecture:
• Opening: State your sustained thesis and establish your evaluative criteria (e.g. legal illusion vs daily social reality).
• Section 1: Support the targeted Interpretation using its quotes and your own historical evidence.
• Section 2: Evaluate the competing Interpretation using counter-evidence from your own knowledge.
• Conclusion: Deliver a justified verdict explaining why one historian’s emphasis is more persuasive.
