JUN 1933 • Law for the Encouragement of Marriage Key Topic 4.1

The Nazi state offers marriage loans of up to 1,000 marks to newlywed Aryan couples, provided the bride gives up her job. For each child born, 25% of the loan is permanently cancelled, boosting the national birth rate.

15 SEP 1935 • Enactment of the Nuremberg Racial Laws Key Topic 4.4

Announced at the Nuremberg Rally, the Reich Citizenship Law strips German Jews of citizenship and voting rights. The Law for the Protection of German Blood and Honour makes marriage and sexual relations between Jews and Aryans illegal.

MAR 1935 • Introduction of Military Conscription & Rearmament Key Topic 4.3

Hitler openly renounces the Versailles disarmament clauses, announcing a peacetime army of 550,000 men and the creation of the Luftwaffe. Massive military rearmament absorbs hundreds of thousands of unemployed German workers.

2/20 "Kinder, Küche, Kirche: Women were pushed out of professions to be domestic child-bearers."

Living Timeline • Part 2: Chronological Anchors (Key Topic 4)

Instructions: Complete the dual-coding sketches to permanently anchor these chronological turning points in long-term memory.
DEC 1936 • First Hitler Youth Law Passed Key Topic 4.2

All non-Nazi youth organisations (Catholic and sporting clubs) are banned or absorbed. Membership of the Hitlerjugend becomes effectively expected for all children aged 10–18, and is made strictly compulsory in March 1939.

9–10 NOV 1938 • Kristallnacht (The Night of Broken Glass) Key Topic 4.4

Following the assassination of diplomat Ernst vom Rath in Paris, Goebbels unleashes nationwide anti-Jewish pogroms. SA and SS mobs burn over 1,000 synagogues, destroy 7,500 Jewish businesses, murder 91 Jews, and send 30,000 Jewish men to concentration camps.

1 SEP 1939 • Invasion of Poland & Outbreak of War Key Topic 4.3

Germany invades Poland, triggering World War Two in Europe. The four-year drive for economic autarky (self-sufficiency) shifts into total war mobilization, and persecution of minorities escalates toward mass deportation and murder.

"Law for Encouragement of Marriage: 1,000-mark loan; keep a quarter for every child you give Germany." 3/20

HISTORICAL INTERPRETATIONS 1 AND 2 Target: How and why historians differ (AO4)
INTERPRETATION 1
From an account by historian Richard Grunberger, A Social History of the Third Reich (1971)
“For the vast majority of German industrial workers, the Nazi era brought tangible improvements in everyday life. Unemployment was eliminated within four years, replacing the terror of the Great Depression with guaranteed employment and steady earnings. Through the ‘Strength Through Joy’ (KdF) organization, millions of ordinary workers enjoyed subsidised theatre tickets, sports facilities, and cruise holidays to Norway and Italy that had previously been the exclusive preserve of the rich. The ‘Beauty of Labour’ campaign renovated factory canteens, washrooms, and ventilation, creating genuinely improved working conditions.”
INTERPRETATION 2
From an account by historian Tim Mason, Social Policy in the Third Reich (1993)
“The claim that German workers experienced a higher standard of living under National Socialism is thoroughly misleading. In return for full employment, workers surrendered all basic rights and freedoms. Real wages remained below 1928 levels while compulsory deductions for the Labour Front, winter relief, and state insurance consumed up to thirty percent of pay packets. Furthermore, the average working week rose from forty-three hours in 1933 to forty-seven hours in 1939. Stripped of the right to strike and tied to their jobs by the Labour Book, workers suffered declining living standards.”
