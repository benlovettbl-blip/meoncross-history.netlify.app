Enquiries 1–4: Key Topic 1 Foundations • Qs 1–32 Official 2-Mark Standard: 1m Fact + 1m Explanation
Q1 The Kiel Naval Mutiny (Kiel & Wilhelmshaven)
Sailors refused orders for a suicidal final battle against the Royal Navy, triggering worker-soldier councils across Germany.
Q2 9 November 1918
Shattered the Hohenzollern autocracy, allowing SPD leader Friedrich Ebert to form a provisional republican government.
Q3 The Compiegne Armistice
Signed by Matthias Erzberger on behalf of the new civilian republic, later providing the pretext for the "stab in the back" myth.
Q4 The Ebert-Groener Pact (10 November 1918)
General Groener promised army loyalty in exchange for Ebert suppressing radical Bolshevik and communist revolutions.
Q5 Proportional Representation (PR)
Allocated 1 Reichstag seat per 60,000 votes, ensuring fair democratic representation but causing fractured coalition governments.
Q6 20 years of age
Established one of the most progressive universal franchises in the world, enfranchising women on equal terms with men.
Q7 Article 48
Intended only for national emergencies, it was later abused by Hindenburg and Bruning, effectively undermining parliamentary democracy.
Q8 Berlin was paralysed by violent political riots and the Spartacist Uprising
The peaceful town of Weimar provided safety for politicians to draft Germany's first democratic constitution.
Q9 £6.6 billion (132 billion gold marks)
Placed an intolerable annual financial burden on the German economy, crippling post-war reconstruction and government revenue.
Q10 Diktat
German delegates were locked out of Versailles negotiations and forced to sign under threat of immediate allied invasion.
Q11 The War Guilt Clause
Deeply humiliated German national pride and provided legal justification for all allied reparations and land confiscations.
Q12 The Dolchstosslegende (the "Stab in the Back" myth)
Promoted by Hindenburg and Ludendorff, it branded the Weimar politicians the "November Criminals" and undermined democratic legitimacy.
Q13 Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht
Crushed ruthlessly by right-wing Freikorps mercenaries hired by Ebert, permanently estranging communists from the SPD.
Q14 Launched a nationwide general strike that halted water, gas, electricity, and trains
Paralysed Berlin and forced Wolfgang Kapp to flee within four days, showing worker loyalty to the young Republic.
Q15 Germany defaulted on timber and coal reparation deliveries
Poincare seized raw materials directly, prompting the German government to order passive resistance and strike pay.
Q16 Printed uncontrolled quantities of unbacked paper banknotes
Devalued the mark to zero, wiping out middle-class savings and destroying faith in the currency and the Republic.
Q17 Gustav Stresemann
Architect of currency reform, international rehabilitation, and diplomatic reconciliation under the policy of fulfilment.
Q18 The Rentenmark
Strictly limited in supply by Hjalmar Schacht, it restored domestic financial confidence and paved the way for the Reichsmark.
Q19 800 million gold marks ($200 million)
Infused liquidity into German factories and railways, lowering annual reparation instalments to manageable levels.
Q20 The Young Plan (1929)
Negotiated by Owen Young, it lowered annual payments by 20% and committed France to evacuate the Rhineland five years early.
Q21 The Locarno Treaties (1925)
Stresemann broke diplomatic isolation, renounced war with France, and paved the way for Germany's entry into the League of Nations.
Q22 The Unemployment Insurance Act (1927)
Required workers and employers to contribute 3% of wages into a central fund, creating the world's most generous welfare net.
Q23 Over 2 million new homes (largely funded by the GeWoS)
Reduced urban overcrowding and improved sanitation, though government welfare budgets became heavily strained.
Q24 36 per cent of workers
Women worked in secretarial, banking, and retail roles, though they were paid on average 33% less than men for identical work.
Q25 The German Workers' Party (DAP)
Founded by Anton Drexler in Munich, it combined anti-Semitism, anti-capitalism, and vehement hatred of the Versailles Treaty.
Q26 The 25-Point Programme
Demanded scrapping Versailles, union of all Germans (Grossdeutschland), denial of Jewish citizenship, and nationalisation of trusts.
Q27 The National Socialist German Workers' Party (NSDAP / Nazi Party)
Added "National Socialist" to capture working-class voters while asserting aggressive imperial nationalism.
Q28 The black Swastika (Hakenkreuz) on a white circle and red field
Used imperial German colours (red, white, black) to project revolutionary vitality and Aryan racial supremacy.
Q29 The Fuhrer (Party Chairman with dictatorial powers)
Established the Fuhrerprinzip (leader principle), ending internal democratic voting and demanding unquestioning obedience.
Q30 8 November 1923
Sought to hold Bavarian leaders Kahr, Lossow, and Seisser at gunpoint and march on Berlin to depose the Weimar government.
Q31 General Erich Ludendorff
Marched alongside Hitler to the Feldherrnhalle, where Bavarian state police fired on the putschists, killing 16 Nazis.
Q32 Used the courtroom to make hours of unchallenged speeches attacking Weimar treason and Versailles
Sympathetic judges gave him front-page newspaper publicity, transforming him from a local Bavarian agitator into a national figure.

Enquiries 5–8: Key Topics 2 & 3 Dictatorship • Qs 33–64 Official 2-Mark Standard: 1m Fact + 1m Explanation
Q33 Power could never be seized by armed force; it had to be won legally through democratic elections
Determined to enter the Reichstag, dismantle the Weimar Constitution from within, and destroy democracy with its own weapons.
Q34 The Bamberg Conference (February 1926)
Asserted absolute Fuhrer authority, rejecting the anti-capitalist points of the programme to win over conservative industrialists.
Q35 The Wall Street Crash (Black Tuesday)
Collapsed the German banking system, caused industrial production to plunge 40%, and triggered mass corporate bankruptcies.
Q36 6.1 million workers (over 30% of the workforce)
Created widespread destitution, starvation, and desperation, destroying public faith in moderate democratic parties.
Q37 Heinrich Bruning
Pursued severe deflationary austerity: slashing civil service salaries, cutting unemployment benefits, and raising tax rates.
Q38 The Communist Party of Germany (KPD)
Won 100 seats in November 1932, terrifying middle-class business owners and wealthy landowners into backing the Nazis.
Q39 Used modern mass media, aeroplanes ("Hitler over Germany"), radio, records, and targeted demographic posters
Projected Hitler as a dynamic, youthful, omnipresent saviour who promised "Work and Bread" tailored to each social class.
Q40 Provided soup kitchens and order for members while engaging in deadly street battles with communist Red Front fighters
Created the impression of disciplined strength, youth, and anti-communist resolve against democratic chaos.
Q41 13.4 million votes (36.8% of the vote)
Though Hindenburg won re-election, the result proved Hitler commanded immense personal national popularity.
Q42 Franz von Papen
Lacked any majority in the Reichstag; lifted the ban on the SA to appease Hitler and governed entirely by presidential decree.
Q43 Fell by 2 million votes (lost 34 seats down to 196)
Showed Nazi momentum had peaked; exhausted party funds led Goebbels to write that "the future looks dark and gloomy".
Q44 General Kurt von Schleicher
Failed to split the Nazi Party by offering Gregor Strasser the Vice-Chancellorship, alienating both Hindenburg and big business.
Q45 Make Hitler Chancellor with Papen as Vice-Chancellor in a conservative coalition cabinet
Convinced the elderly Hindenburg that the non-Nazi majority in the cabinet would "box Hitler into a corner".
Q46 Only three ministers (Hitler, Frick, and Goering)
Papen boasted that "within two months we will have pushed Hitler so far into a corner he will squeak", disastrously underestimating him.
Q47 30 January 1933
Hitler attained supreme executive authority through legal, constitutional appointment, setting the stage for dictatorship.
Q48 A torchlight parade through the Brandenburg Gate
Projected triumphant paramilitary dominance, signalling to the German people that the Weimar Republic was dead.
Q49 The Reichstag (Parliament building in Berlin)
Hitler blamed an imminent Bolshevik revolution, using it to terrorise Hindenburg into signing emergency decrees.
Q50 Marinus van der Lubbe
Used by the Nazis as absolute proof that the KPD was orchestrating an armed communist uprising across Germany.
Q51 The Reichstag Fire Decree (Decree for the Protection of People and State)
Allowed the Gestapo to imprison 4,000 communists, shutter opposition newspapers, and hold citizens without trial.
Q52 The Enabling Act (Gesetz zur Behebung der Not von Volk und Reich)
Passed 444 votes to 94 (only SPD opposed), effectively transferring all legislative power to Hitler's cabinet and ending democracy.
Q53 Gleichschaltung ("Coordination")
Systematically brought state parliaments, trade unions, courts, and civil service under direct Nazi Party control.
Q54 All independent Trade Unions (replaced by the Nazi DAF)
Eliminated organised working-class resistance, banning all strikes and placing wages under state control.
Q55 The Night of the Long Knives (Operation Hummingbird)
Eliminated the radical socialist wing of the SA, winning the absolute loyalty and support of regular German army generals.
Q56 The Personal Oath of Loyalty directly to Adolf Hitler as Fuhrer
Bound the military by personal, sacred Christian honor to obey Hitler to the death, making subsequent military revolt treason.
Q57 Heinrich Himmler (Reichsfuhrer-SS)
Expanded the black-uniformed SS from a 280-man bodyguard into a vast racial, military, and police empire.
Q58 The Gestapo (Geheime Staatspolizei)
Operated above the law without judicial oversight; relied heavily on ordinary German citizens voluntarily denouncing neighbours.
Q59 Dachau (near Munich)
Established the brutal template of barbed wire, guard towers, and corporal terror for communists, trade unionists, and critics.
Q60 The People's Court (Volksgerichtshof)
Headed by Nazi judges who ensured that 90% of political treason defendants were found guilty and executed.
Q61 The Concordat (Reichskonkordat)
Hitler promised not to interfere with Catholic schools and youth clubs if the Catholic Centre Party dissolved itself.
Q62 The Reich Church (Reichskirche)
Sought to remove the Old Testament (as Jewish) and replace crosses with swastikas, outraging traditional Protestant clergy.
Q63 Protective custody (Schutzhaft)
Citizens could be imprisoned indefinitely in concentration camps without trial, warrants, or legal representation.
Q64 The National Socialist League for the Maintenance of the Law (BNSDJ)
Ensured judges prioritized Nazi ideology and racial purity above impartial legal justice in every court ruling.

Enquiries 9–12: Key Topics 3 & 4 Police State & Society • Qs 65–96 Official 2-Mark Standard: 1m Fact + 1m Explanation
Q65 Dr Joseph Goebbels
Master propagandist who controlled press, radio, cinema, literature, and art to align German minds with National Socialism.
Q66 The Public Book Burning (Bücherverbrennung)
Students burned works by Jewish, communist, and liberal authors like Einstein, Freud, Marx, and Remarque.
Q67 The Volksempfanger (People's Receiver - Model VE301)
Subsidised to cost just 35 marks; lacked shortwave bands so Germans could not listen to foreign broadcasts like the BBC.
Q68 Over 70 per cent of households
Enabled Goebbels to broadcast Hitler's voice directly into living rooms and factories with mandatory communal listening.
Q69 The Nuremberg Rallies (Reichsparteitag)
Designed by Albert Speer with "Cathedrals of Light" searchlights to project awe, monolithic unity, and total obedience.
Q70 Leni Riefenstahl
Pioneered dynamic camera angles and aerial shots to present Hitler as an all-powerful, messianic Aryan leader.
Q71 The Degenerate Art Exhibition (Entartete Kunst)
Denounced expressionist, cubist, and Jewish art as "filth", contrasting it with heroic, realistic Aryan neoclassical art.
Q72 The 1936 Berlin Olympic Games
Temporarily removed anti-Semitic signs to present Germany as modern, peaceful, and united, though Jesse Owens won 4 gold medals.
Q73 The Edelweiss Pirates (Edelweisspiraten)
Working-class teenagers who rejected regimented Nazi camps, camping in nature and singing banned parodied songs.
Q74 The Swing Youth (Swingjugend)
Wealthy youths in Hamburg and Berlin who welcomed Jewish friends and mocked Nazi militarism through Anglo-American style.
Q75 Cardinal Clemens August Graf von Galen
Exposed the secret murder of mentally disabled children; his immense public popularity prevented the Nazis from executing him.
Q76 Jehovah's Witnesses (Bibelforscher)
Over 1,200 were murdered in concentration camps wearing the purple triangle, demonstrating unyielding moral resistance.
Q77 The Red Orchestra (Rote Kapelle)
Resisted within the state apparatus, though most pre-war communist cells were rapidly penetrated by the Gestapo.
Q78 Telling political jokes (Flüsterwitze), complaining privately, and refusing the "Heil Hitler" greeting
Known as "inner emigration", it allowed citizens to preserve personal dignity without risking execution for public protest.
Q79 Total Gestapo surveillance, terror of Dachau, neighbourhood block wardens, and lack of independent civil organizations
The totalitarian destruction of trade unions, free press, and rival parties meant dissenters had no vehicle to organize.
Q80 Hitler eliminated unemployment, tore up the hated Versailles Treaty, and restored German national pride and public order
Economic recovery and foreign policy triumphs created immense genuine popularity that far outweighed opposition.
Q81 "Kinder, Küche, Kirche" (Children, Kitchen, Church)
Relegated women strictly to biological reproduction, domestic homemaking, and moral Christian-nationalist upbringing.
Q82 Marriage Loans (Ehedarlehen)
Worth nine months' wages, with 25% of the loan cancelled for each child born; having 4 children wiped out the debt entirely.
Q83 The Cross of Honour of the German Mother (Mutterkreuz)
Awarded in Bronze (4–5 children), Silver (6–7 children), and Gold (8+ children); members of the Hitler Youth had to salute them.
Q84 The Lebensborn ("Fount of Life") Program
Aimed to breed racially elite children in maternity homes, producing over 11,000 babies to expand the Aryan master race.
Q85 No makeup, plain hair in braids or buns, traditional dirndl dresses, no smoking, and no slimming diets
Celebrated natural peasant health and fertility over "degenerate, decadent Americanised" fashion and makeup.
Q86 The National Socialist Teachers' League (NSLB)
Over 97% of teachers joined, attending compulsory ideological training camps in racial science and Nazi ideology.
Q87 15 per cent of school time (at least 2 hours daily)
Hitler demanded that boys be hardened to endure pain and physical combat, prioritizing physical fitness over intellectual study.
Q88 Racial Studies (Rassenkunde)
Classified races hierarchically, indoctrinating children to view Jews as parasitic "sub-humans" (Untermenschen).
Q89 The Reich Labour Service (RAD - Reichsarbeitsdienst)
Young men lived in military-style camps, wearing uniforms and draining marshes or planting forests with spades at pocket-money pay.
Q90 The Autobahn Project
Created direct construction jobs, stimulated automobile production, and provided rapid military transport across Germany.
Q91 Autarky (Self-Sufficiency)
Promoted by Hermann Goering under the 1936 Four-Year Plan; developed ersatz (substitute) products like synthetic rubber (Buna) and oil from coal.
Q92 Strength Through Joy (KdF - Kraft durch Freude)
World's largest tourism operator; rewarded loyal workers to boost industrial productivity and minimize labour unrest.
Q93 The Volkswagen ("People's Car" / KdF-Wagen)
Designed by Ferdinand Porsche; all production was diverted to military Kubelwagen jeeps in 1939, swindling workers' savings.
Q94 The One-Day Boycott of Jewish Shops, Doctors, and Lawyers
Marked the regime's first official state anti-Semitic action, testing public compliance and normalizing discrimination.
Q95 The Nuremberg Laws (Reich Citizenship Law & Law for Protection of German Blood and Honour)
Banned marriages and sexual relations between Jews and Aryans, stripping Jews of civil rights and the right to vote.
Q96 Having three or four Jewish grandparents
Defined Jewish identity biologically by bloodline rather than religious practice, sealing the fate of secular assimilated citizens.
