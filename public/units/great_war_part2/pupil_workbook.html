Year 9 Enquiry • 1914–1919

The Great War: 1914–1919

Industrial Slaughter, Global Sacrifice, Total War & The Flawed Peace
Primary Visual Evidence • Archival Record Plate Imperial War Museum • London

Between August 1914 and November 1918, the First World War destroyed four European empires, cost over 20 million lives, and transformed Britain into a fully mobilised modern warfare state:

1. Voluntary Enlistment & Pals Battalions:
Lacking peacetime conscription, over 2.5 million British men volunteered in 1914–1915, forming civic Pals units like the Portsmouth and Hampshire Pals.
2. Industrial Firepower & The Somme Deadlock:
Rapid-fire artillery and Maxim machine guns forced armies underground into 400 miles of trenches, leading to 57,470 casualties on 1 July 1916.
3. Global Imperial Sacrifice & Erasure:
Over 4 million non-white colonial soldiers and labourers from India, Africa, and the Caribbean sustained the Empire’s war effort across multiple continents.
4. The Flawed Peace & Territorial Mandates:
The 1919 Treaty of Versailles and Sykes-Picot imperial partition dismantled old empires but sowed the seeds of 1939 and ongoing Middle East conflict.

Disciplinary Significance: The Great War shattered nineteenth-century European empires, brought women into industrial citizenship, and established the ideological conflicts that defined the twentieth century.
Disciplinary Hinge Question: Historical Evaluation
Did the Treaty of Versailles successfully resolve the underlying imperial rivalries of 1914, or did its punitive terms guarantee that European conflict would reignite twenty years later?

Question 3(b) & 3(c): Contrasting Interpretations [8 marks • 10 mins]

HISTORIOGRAPHICAL ANALYSIS
Interpretation 1 • Lord Curzon, British War Cabinet Minister, Speech at the Royal Albert Hall (1917)
“The Indian Army and the troops from our dominions have rushed forward with magnificent devotion to defend the King-Emperor. Side by side with their British brothers-in-arms, they have shed their blood on the plains of Flanders, Gallipoli, and Egypt. This imperial brotherhood proves that the British Empire is held together not by force, but by mutual love, loyalty, and common sacrifice.”
Interpretation 2 • David Olusoga, Modern British Historian, The World’s War (2014)
“Over four million non-white men were mobilised as combatants and labourers by Britain. Yet their treatment was defined by systemic racism. Black and Asian soldiers were barred from becoming officers, paid a fraction of British wages, and subjected to brutal floggings. After 1918, the Imperial War Graves Commission deliberately refused to give African dead individual headstones. Their sacrifice was whitewashed from British national memory.”
Question 3(b): Explain how Interpretations 1 and 2 differ about the experiences of colonial troops in the Great War. [4 marks]

August 1914 • The Rush to the Colours & Lord Kitchener’s Appeal Enquiry 1

Following the German invasion of Belgium, Lord Kitchener issues his iconic recruiting appeal; over 750,000 volunteers enlist in eight weeks, creating the civic Pals Battalions across Britain.

April 1915 • The Gallipoli Landings & ANZAC Mobilisation Enquiry 3

British, French, Indian, and Australian/New Zealand (ANZAC) forces land on the Gallipoli peninsula to knock the Ottoman Empire out of the war; eight months of bloody stalemate forge Australian national consciousness.

1 July 1916 • The First Day on the Somme & Destruction of the Pals Enquiry 2

Following a seven-day artillery bombardment, 100,000 British infantry assault intact German wire; 19,240 men are killed in hours, devastating entire towns and wiping out civic volunteer battalions.

September 1916 • First Tank Deployment at Flers-Courcelette Enquiry 2

Britain unveils the Mark I tank on the Somme, driving over trenches and crushing barbed wire; while mechanically unreliable, it inaugurates modern combined-arms armoured warfare.

1916–1917 • Conscription, "Canary Girls" & Total War Mobilisation Enquiry 4

Parliament enforces military conscription for all eligible men while 1 million women enter munitions factories ("Canary Girls") and transport; German U-boat attacks bring Britain within weeks of starvation.

November 1917 • The Balfour Declaration & Middle East Partition Enquiry 5

Foreign Secretary Arthur Balfour pledges British support for a Jewish national home in Palestine; conflicting with secret Sykes-Picot deals and pledges to Arab leaders, the stage is set for modern Middle Eastern partition.

28 June 1919 • The Signing of the Treaty of Versailles Enquiry 5

In the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, Allied powers force Germany to accept Article 231 (War Guilt), pay £6.6 billion in reparations, and surrender 13% of its European territory, leaving Weimar Germany humiliated.

1920–1923 • League of Nations Mandates & Parish Remembrance Enquiry 5

The League of Nations grants Britain and France Class A Mandates over Palestine, Iraq, and Syria; across Britain, parishes erect permanent stone and timber war memorials to mourn 888,000 fallen service personnel.

Between 1914 and 1923, the Great War destroyed four historic empires and fundamentally remade the political map of the modern world. 9/16
