Enquiries 1–4: Key Topic 1 Origins of the Cold War (1941–1958) • Qs 1–32 Official 2-Mark Standard: 1m Fact + 1m Explanation
Q1 USA (Roosevelt/Truman), USSR (Stalin), and Britain (Churchill/Attlee)
Formed as a marriage of convenience to defeat Nazi Germany despite profound ideological hostility.
Q2 To open a "Second Front" in Western Europe (Operation Overlord)
Relieved German military pressure on the Soviet Red Army fighting on the Eastern Front.
Q3 Four occupational zones (US, British, French, and Soviet)
Berlin, located deep inside the Soviet zone, was also subdivided into four corresponding sectors.
Q4 The United Nations (UN)
The USA, USSR, Britain, France, and China received permanent veto power on the Security Council.
Q5 To allow free, democratic elections across Eastern Europe (Declaration on Liberated Europe)
Stalin subsequently broke this pledge, installing puppet communist regimes across Eastern Europe.
Q6 Harry S. Truman
Adopted a far more aggressive, suspicious, and confrontational stance towards Stalin than Roosevelt.
Q7 The successful testing of the atomic bomb (Trinity test in New Mexico)
Intended to intimidate Stalin, but instead accelerated the Soviet crash programme to build their own bomb.
Q8 Each power took reparations primarily from its own occupation zone (USSR got 10% industrial equipment from West)
Prevented a unified German economic policy and deepened the divide between East and West.
Q9 Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August 1945)
Killed over 120,000 civilians immediately, forced Japan’s surrender, and initiated the nuclear arms race.
Q10 The Long Telegram
Warned that the USSR was fanatically hostile to the West and recommended a long-term policy of containment.
Q11 The Novikov Telegram (Nikolai Novikov)
Convinced Stalin that America was building up its military to launch an aggressive war against the USSR.
Q12 An "Iron Curtain" has descended across the continent
Stalin condemned the speech as warmongering and compared Churchill’s rhetoric to Adolf Hitler.
Q13 Satellite states (buffer zone)
Created by Stalin across Poland, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, and Czechoslovakia to protect Russia from Western invasion.
Q14 "Salami tactics" (cutting away opposition slice by slice)
Coined by Hungarian leader Rakosi to describe gradually arresting, discrediting, and executing rival politicians.
Q15 Czechoslovakia
Foreign Minister Jan Masaryk was defenestrated (thrown from a window), shocking the US Congress into passing Marshall Aid.
Q16 Poland had served as the historical military corridor for German invasions of Russia in 1914 and 1941
Cost the Soviet Union 27 million lives during WWII, convincing Stalin that a friendly Polish buffer was non-negotiable.
Q17 The Truman Doctrine
Pledged American economic and military assistance to any nation resisting armed subjugation by communist minorities.
Q18 Greece and Turkey
Britain could no longer afford to support the Greek monarchy against communist guerrillas, prompting US intervention.
Q19 Containment
Aimed to halt the outward geographic expansion of Soviet communism rather than rolling back existing territory.
Q20 The Marshall Plan (European Recovery Program)
Provided $13.7 billion in grants and goods over four years to reconstruct Western European economies and trade.
Q21 Creating prosperous export markets for American industrial and agricultural goods
Prevented the US economy from sliding into a post-war depression by financing European purchases of US products.
Q22 "Dollar Imperialism" (economic enslavement of Europe)
Stalin claimed the US was using financial aid to establish an economic empire and undermine European sovereignty.
Q23 Cominform (Communist Information Bureau)
Enforced ideological conformity to Moscow across Eastern Europe and ensured member states rejected Marshall Aid.
Q24 Comecon (Council for Mutual Economic Assistance)
Organised bilateral trade agreements and national specialisation within the Soviet bloc, excluding Western trade.
Q25 The British and American zones (later Trizonia with France)
Created a unified economic territory in Western Germany, provoking fierce Soviet protests at four-power talks.
Q26 The introduction of a new Western currency, the Deutschmark
Stalin feared the Deutschmark would undermine the worthless Soviet East German currency and unify the West.
Q27 Imposed a total ground blockade on all road, rail, and canal routes into West Berlin
Cut electricity, food, and fuel to 2 million West Berliners deep inside the Soviet occupation zone.
Q28 The Berlin Airlift (Operation Vittles)
US and British transport planes flew supplies into Tempelhof and Gatow airports every three minutes around the clock.
Q29 Eleven months (June 1948 to May 1949)
Delivered 2.3 million tonnes of supplies, proving Western determination to defend freedom in Berlin.
Q30 The Federal Republic of Germany (FRG / West Germany)
Led by democratic Chancellor Konrad Adenauer with its capital at Bonn.
Q31 The German Democratic Republic (GDR / East Germany)
A communist one-party state governed by the SED under Walter Ulbricht.
Q32 NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organisation)
Article 5 declared that an armed attack against one member in Europe or North America would be considered an attack on all.

Enquiries 5–8: Key Topic 2 Cold War Crises (1958–1970) • Qs 33–64 Official 2-Mark Standard: 1m Fact + 1m Explanation
Q33 The Soviet Union successfully tested its first atomic bomb (RDS-1)
Shocked Washington years ahead of CIA predictions and dramatically escalated the Cold War arms race.
Q34 The Hydrogen Bomb (thermonuclear bomb / Ivy Mike)
The Soviet Union matched the US by testing its own deployable hydrogen bomb in August 1953.
Q35 ICBMs (Intercontinental Ballistic Missiles)
Eliminated reliance on slow bombers; the USSR demonstrated leadership with the Sputnik launch in October 1957.
Q36 The "Secret Speech" to the 20th Party Congress
Denounced Stalin’s crimes, purges, and personality cult, raising false hopes of political reform in satellite states.
Q37 Matyas Rakosi ("the bald butcher")
His secret police (AVH) terrorised Hungary, creating simmering public fury that exploded into street protests in October.
Q38 Imre Nagy
A moderate communist reformer who introduced free elections, abolished the secret police, and freed political prisoners.
Q39 Hungary’s withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact and declaration of national neutrality
Stalin and Khrushchev could not tolerate a gaping hole in their military buffer zone bordering Austria.
Q40 1,000 Soviet tanks and 200,000 troops
Crushed armed Hungarian street resistance, killing over 20,000 Hungarians and forcing 200,000 to flee into exile.
Q41 Around 2.7 million to 3 million people (one-sixth of the GDR population)
Created an existential economic crisis for East Germany because the majority were young, skilled professionals ("brain drain").
Q42 The Berlin Ultimatum (demanding Western troops leave Berlin within 6 months to make it a free city)
Khrushchev threatened to hand control of access routes to East Germany if the West refused to withdraw.
Q43 The Camp David Summit
Khrushchev withdrew his six-month ultimatum, and the leaders agreed to hold a full superpower summit in Paris in 1960.
Q44 The shooting down of an American U-2 spy plane over Soviet airspace
Pilot Gary Powers was captured alive; Eisenhower refused to apologise, causing Khrushchev to storm out of Paris.
Q45 He bullied Kennedy by reissuing the Berlin Ultimatum, believing JFK was young and inexperienced
Kennedy refused to back down, increasing US defense spending by $3.2 billion and expanding military draft calls.
Q46 Sunday, 13 August 1961 (Operation Rose)
Closed all crossing points overnight, separating families and beginning construction of the concrete Berlin Wall.
Q47 The Standoff at Checkpoint Charlie
US diplomat E. Allan Lightner was stopped from entering East Berlin; after 16 hours Kennedy and Khrushchev agreed to pull tanks back.
Q48 "Ich bin ein Berliner" ("I am a Berliner")
Affirmed unwavering American commitment to West Berlin and condemned the Wall as the ultimate symbol of communist failure.
Q49 Fidel Castro
Nationalised American-owned sugar plantations, banks, and oil refineries, prompting the US to impose a trade embargo.
Q50 The Soviet Union (Nikita Khrushchev)
Turned Cuba into a communist ally barely 90 miles off the coast of Florida.
Q51 The Bay of Pigs Invasion (La Playa Giron)
Failed within 72 hours when Kennedy canceled US air cover; humiliated America and pushed Castro directly into Soviet arms.
Q52 To protect Cuba from another US invasion and balance US Jupiter missiles stationed in neighbouring Turkey
Also aimed to close the "missile gap" and gain immense leverage over Berlin negotiations.
Q53 Photographs of Soviet medium-range ballistic missile (MRBM) launch pads under construction in Cuba
Initiated the "Thirteen Days" (16–28 October 1962) where the world came closest to full thermonuclear war.
Q54 ExComm (Executive Committee of the National Security Council)
Debated military airstrikes and invasion against a naval quarantine, with Kennedy choosing the middle option.
Q55 Under international law, a "blockade" is a formal act of war; a "quarantine" was framed as a defensive measure
Gave Khrushchev time to turn his ships around without immediately triggering combat.
Q56 A US U-2 spy plane piloted by Major Rudolf Anderson was shot down over Cuba by a Soviet SAM missile
US military chiefs demanded immediate retaliatory airstrikes, but Kennedy refused to authorize them.
Q57 The Moscow-Washington Hotline (Teletype / wire link)
Allowed superpower leaders to communicate instantly during crises to prevent accidental nuclear escalation.
Q58 The Limited Test Ban Treaty (LTBT)
Permitted only underground testing; marked the first formal multilateral treaty limiting nuclear weaponry.
Q59 The Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT)
Nuclear states pledged not to transfer weapons, while non-nuclear signatories agreed to peaceful civilian nuclear power only.
Q60 Alexander Dubcek
Committed to reforming the communist system from within while remaining a loyal member of the Warsaw Pact.
Q61 "Socialism with a human face"
Aimed to combine communist planned economy with democratic freedoms, ending censorship and allowing rival parties.
Q62 Abolition of press censorship, freedom of speech, legalization of trade unions, and permission to travel to the West
Terrified Soviet leader Leonid Brezhnev and neighbouring communist rulers like Ulbricht in East Germany.
Q63 500,000 Warsaw Pact troops and 2,000 tanks invaded Czechoslovakia
Seized radio stations, arrested Dubcek, and took him in handcuffs to Moscow to sign away his reforms.
Q64 Non-violent civil disobedience (standing in front of tanks, painting over street signs, and student Jan Palach self-immolation)
Prevented the heavy bloodshed of Hungary 1956, but left the country demoralised under Soviet military occupation.

Enquiries 9–12: Key Topic 3 Détente to the Fall of the USSR (1970–1991) • Qs 65–96 Official 2-Mark Standard: 1m Fact + 1m Explanation
Q65 Détente (relaxation of tension)
Pursued by President Richard Nixon, Henry Kissinger, and Soviet Premier Leonid Brezhnev to reduce the cost of the arms race.
Q66 Crippling military expenditure and declining living standards (the Soviet economy was stagnant)
The USSR was spending up to 20% of its GDP on weapons, causing chronic food shortages and desperate need for US grain.
Q67 President Nixon’s historic visit to communist China (triangular diplomacy)
The USSR feared a hostile US-Chinese alliance against Moscow, compelling Brezhnev to invite Nixon to Moscow.
Q68 SALT 1 (Strategic Arms Limitation Talks)
Consisted of the Anti-Ballistic Missile (ABM) Treaty and an Interim Agreement freezing missile launcher numbers.
Q69 Two ABM sites (each with a maximum of 100 interceptor missiles; reduced to one in 1974)
Preserved "Mutual Assured Destruction" (MAD) by guaranteeing that neither side could build a shield to survive a counter-strike.
Q70 The Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (handshake in space)
US astronaut Thomas Stafford and Soviet cosmonaut Alexei Leonov docked spacecraft in orbit and shook hands.
Q71 The Helsinki Accords (Helsinki Declaration)
Represented the high-water mark of détente, dividing agreements into three thematic "Baskets".
Q72 Recognition of existing European borders (including the division of Germany and Soviet annexation of the Baltic states)
Formally legitimized post-WWII European borders and accepted the Soviet sphere of influence.
Q73 Afghanistan
Stalin and Brezhnev feared an Islamic revolution on their southern border following the 1979 Iranian Revolution.
Q74 Hafizullah Amin
Soviet intelligence believed Amin was secretly negotiating with the CIA; he was replaced by pro-Soviet puppet Babrak Karmal.
Q75 The Mujahideen (holy warriors)
Fought a fierce ten-year insurgency using mountain terrain, receiving covert weapons and cash from the CIA, Pakistan, and Saudi Arabia.
Q76 The Carter Doctrine
Declared that any attempt by an outside power to gain control of the Persian Gulf region would be repelled by US military force.
Q77 Suspension of grain sales to the USSR and restrictions on high-technology exports
Inflicted severe economic pain on the Soviet Union and ended US-Soviet trade cooperation.
Q78 The 1980 Moscow Summer Olympic Games
Organised by President Carter to protest Soviet aggression, leaving the games heavily depleted.
Q79 They boycotted the 1984 Los Angeles Olympic Games
Claimed their athletes would not be safe from anti-communist protesters in California.
Q80 The FIM-92 Stinger missile
Neutralised Soviet helicopter gunships (Mi-24 Hinds), robbing the Red Army of its decisive battlefield air superiority.
Q81 An "Evil Empire" (and the focus of evil in the modern world)
Framed the Cold War not as a conventional balance of power dispute, but as a moral crusade between freedom and totalitarianism.
Q82 US defense spending increased by over 40% (to over $300 billion annually)
Financed massive rearmament, including B-1 bombers, MX Peacekeeper missiles, and a 600-ship US Navy.
Q83 The Strategic Defence Initiative (SDI / nicknamed "Star Wars")
Proposed using laser and satellite technology to intercept and destroy Soviet nuclear missiles in flight before they reached America.
Q84 The 1972 ABM Treaty strictly banned nationwide anti-ballistic missile defenses and space-based weapons
Reagan argued SDI was a purely defensive shield that would render nuclear weapons obsolete.
Q85 If America could block Soviet retaliation, the US could launch a first nuclear strike with absolute impunity
Worse, Soviet scientists calculated that matching US computer and laser technology would bankrupt the USSR.
Q86 Korean Air Lines Flight 007 (KAL 007)
Killed all 269 passengers and crew, including a US Congressman; Reagan condemned it as a "massacre" and barbaric atrocity.
Q87 Exercise Able Archer 83
The USSR placed nuclear-capable air squadrons in East Germany on high alert, bringing superpowers terrifyingly close to war.
Q88 Economic stagnation, chronic shortages of food and basic goods, widespread corruption, and obsolete technology
Spending up to 25% of national wealth on the military left living standards lagging far behind the capitalist West.
Q89 Mikhail Gorbachev
The youngest member of the Politburo, dedicated to reforming Soviet communism through revolutionary "New Thinking".
Q90 Glasnost (openness)
Allowed public debate, free reporting of state corruption and disasters (like Chernobyl 1986), and freedom of speech.
Q91 Perestroika (restructuring)
Allowed small private businesses, foreign investment, and decentralized factory management to stimulate production.
Q92 The INF Treaty (Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty)
Eliminated an entire category of nuclear weapons (all ground-launched cruise and ballistic missiles with ranges 500–5,500km).
Q93 The USSR would no longer use military force to intervene in Eastern European internal affairs
Foreign ministry spokesman Gennadi Gerasimov nicknamed this the "Sinatra Doctrine" (they can do it "My Way").
Q94 Solidarity (Solidarnosc), led by Lech Walesa
Gorbachev refused to intervene, and Tadeusz Mazowiecki became Eastern Europe’s first non-communist prime minister since 1948.
Q95 Thursday, 9 November 1989
Spokesman Gunter Schabowski accidentally announced immediate travel freedom; millions celebrated as the Wall was dismantled.
Q96 The Malta Summit
Held on cruise ships off Malta; Gorbachev declared the world was leaving an epoch of Cold War and entering peace.
