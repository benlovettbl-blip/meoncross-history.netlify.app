Living Timeline • Part 1: Chronological Anchors (Key Topic 2)

Instructions: As you study each enquiry lesson, complete the timeline missions by sketching and annotating in the corresponding dual-coding sketchpads below.
NOV 1569 • Revolt of the Northern Earls Key Topic 2.1

Catholic Earls of Northumberland and Westmorland march on Durham Cathedral, tear up English bibles, and restore the Latin Mass with 4,600 men. Royal troops march north; the rebellion collapses and Elizabeth executes 450 rebels to crush northern disobedience.

FEB 1570 • Papal Bull: Regnans in Excelsis Key Topic 2.1

Pope Pius V issues the papal bull Regnans in Excelsis, excommunicating Elizabeth as a heretic and absolving all Catholic subjects of their oath of allegiance. This officially criminalizes Catholic loyalty and makes all active recusants potential traitors.

1577–1580 • Drake Circumnavigates the Globe Key Topic 2.2

Francis Drake completes the second circumnavigation in history in the Golden Hind, raiding Spanish Pacific colonies and capturing the treasure galleon Cacafuego (£140,000 in silver). Elizabeth knights Drake at Deptford, escalating Anglo-Spanish commercial rivalry.

2/16 "1569: The Northern Earls marched with Catholic banners; Elizabeth responded with 450 executions."

Living Timeline • Part 2: Chronological Anchors (Key Topic 2)

Instructions: Complete the dual-coding sketches to permanently anchor these chronological turning points in long-term memory.
AUG 1585 • Treaty of Nonsuch (War in Netherlands) Key Topic 2.3

Following the assassination of William of Orange and the French Treaty of Joinville, Elizabeth signs the Treaty of Nonsuch, sending Robert Dudley (Earl of Leicester) with 7,400 English soldiers to aid Dutch Protestant rebels against the Duke of Parma.

FEB 1587 • Execution of Mary, Queen of Scots Key Topic 2.1

Walsingham’s codebreaker Thomas Phelippes intercepts Babington’s ciphers explicitly approved by Mary Stuart. Tried under the 1584 Bond of Association, Mary is found guilty of high treason and beheaded at Fotheringhay Castle on 8 February 1587.

JUL–AUG 1588 • Defeat of the Spanish Armada Key Topic 2.4

Medina Sidonia sails 130 ships to ferry Parma’s invasion army across the Channel. English fireships scatter the crescent formation at Calais Roads; agile galleons batter Spanish ships at Gravelines, and violent storms wreck half the fleet on Scottish and Irish rocks.

"Regnans in Excelsis (1570): The Pope declared Elizabeth a heretic; Elizabeth declared plotters traitors." 3/16
