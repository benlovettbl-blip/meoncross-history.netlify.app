NOV 1569 • Revolt of the Northern Earls Key Topic 2.1

Catholic Earls of Northumberland and Westmorland march on Durham Cathedral, tear up English bibles, and restore the Latin Mass with 4,600 men. Royal troops march north; the rebellion collapses and Elizabeth executes 450 rebels to crush northern disobedience.

FEB 1570 • Papal Bull *Regnans in Excelsis* Key Topic 2.1

Pope Pius V issues a papal bull excommunicating Elizabeth, declaring her a 'pretended queen' and heretic. Crucially, the Pope releases all Catholic subjects from their oath of allegiance, making it a sacred Catholic duty to overthrow the Queen.

1571–1583 • The Ridolfi and Throckmorton Plots Key Topic 2.1

Italian banker Roberto Ridolfi plans a Spanish invasion by the Duke of Alba to marry Mary to the Duke of Norfolk. In 1583, French Catholic Francis Throckmorton coordinates a planned invasion backed by Spain and the Pope. Walsingham uncovers both.

Instructions: Complete the dual-coding sketches to permanently anchor these chronological turning points in long-term memory.
AUG 1585 • The Treaty of Nonsuch & Open War with Spain Key Topic 2.3

Following the assassination of Dutch Protestant leader William of Orange, Elizabeth signs the Treaty of Nonsuch with Dutch rebels, agreeing to send 7,400 troops under Robert Dudley. This ends decades of cold war and triggers open military conflict with Spain.

1586–FEB 1587 • The Babington Plot & Execution of Mary Stuart Key Topic 2.1

Anthony Babington sends coded letters in beer barrels to Mary Stuart agreeing to assassinate Elizabeth. Walsingham’s codebreaker Thomas Phelippes intercepts Mary’s endorsement. Mary is convicted of high treason and beheaded at Fotheringhay Castle on 8 Feb 1587.

APR 1587–AUG 1588 • Drake at Cadiz & Defeat of the Spanish Armada Key Topic 2.4

Drake raids Cadiz harbour, destroying 30 Spanish ships ('singeing the King's beard') and delaying the Armada by a year. In July 1588, 130 Spanish ships enter the Channel. English fireships break their formation at Calais; Gravelines artillery seals English victory.

"Regnans in Excelsis (1570): The Pope declared Elizabeth a heretic; Elizabeth declared plotters traitors." 3/24

3 8 AUG 1588
The Battle of Gravelines
• English race-built galleons engage scattered Spanish ships at close range (100 meters).

• Spanish heavy siege cannons mounted on two-wheeled land carriages reload slowly.
How did English ship design and gunnery tactics defeat Spanish warships at Gravelines?
4 AUG–SEPT 1588
The Winds & Atlantic Retreat
• Strong south-westerly gales drive battered Armada north past Scotland into Atlantic.
• Spanish ships lack anchors, fresh water, and food due to rotten unseasoned barrels.
• Over 40 Spanish ships wrecked along rocky coasts of Scotland and western Ireland.

5 1588–1589
Consequences of the English Victory
• Only 67 of 130 Spanish ships return to Spain; thousands of Spanish sailors perish.
• Elizabeth delivers Tilbury speech; commemorative Armada portrait and medal commissioned.
• War with Spain continues for another 16 years until Treaty of London (1604).

Naval Tactics & Strategic Map • The Spanish Armada (1588)

1. The Strategic Route of the Armada: Plymouth to Gravelines & The Atlantic Retreat
① The Channel (July): 130 Spanish ships in tight crescent formation sail past Plymouth and the Isle of Wight. English long-range fire inflicts minor damage.
② Calais Roads (7 Aug): Armada anchors waiting for Parma. Midnight English fireships scatter Spanish fleet, forcing captains to cut anchor cables.
③ Gravelines (8 Aug): Close-range artillery duel. English race-built galleons pummel drifting Spanish ships with rapid-fire culverins; 5 galleons sunk.
④ The Retreat (Aug–Sept): South-westerly gales drive Armada around Scotland and Ireland. Lacking anchors, over 40 ships wreck on Atlantic reefs.
2. Naval Architecture: Hawkins’ Race-Built Galleon vs The Spanish Imperial Carrack
🇬🇧 English Race-Built Galleon (*Revenge*, *Ark Royal*) • Designer: Sir John Hawkins (Treasurer of the Navy)
• Hull Design: Lower forecastles and aftercastles; streamlined hull for superior speed, maneuverability, and pointing into the wind
• Armament: Armed with long-range bronze culverins mounted on compact four-wheeled truck carriages
• Gunnery Doctrine: Trained gun crews could reload and fire broadsides rapidly from standoff range, avoiding boarding combat
• Tactical Goal: Batter enemy hulls and rigging from safe artillery distance
🇪🇸 Spanish Imperial Galleon / Carrack (*San Martín*) • Commander: Duke of Medina Sidonia
• Hull Design: High towering wooden forecastles and sterncastles designed as floating wooden fortresses for infantry
• Armament: Heavy short-range cannon and demi-cannon mounted on clumsy two-wheeled field carriages
• Gunnery Doctrine: Soldiers fired muskets from high castles while gunners reloaded outside ship hulls (extremely slow)
• Tactical Goal: Grapple enemy ships, board with veteran Spanish infantry, and capture vessels in hand-to-hand combat
20/24 "Armada Tactics: High Spanish castles for boarding vs low English race-built galleons for gunnery."

Master Knowledge Organiser • Key Topic 2: Challenges to Elizabeth at Home and Abroad, 1569–88

1. Core Disciplinary Vocabulary & Conceptual Definitions
• Papal Bull (1570): *Regnans in Excelsis*: Pope Pius V excommunicated Elizabeth, releasing subjects from loyalty.
• Privateering: State-licensed raiding of enemy merchant shipping by armed private vessels holding letters of marque.
• Bond of Association (1584): Pledge by Englishmen to execute anyone involved in an assassination attempt against Elizabeth.
• Treaty of Nonsuch (1585): Official military alliance committing 7,400 English soldiers under Robert Dudley to aid Dutch rebels.
• Singeing the King’s Beard: Drake’s April 1587 raid on Cadiz harbour destroying 30 ships and delaying the Armada by a year.
• Galleon: A large, multi-decked warship designed for ocean navigation and long-range cannon fire.
• Culverin: Long-range English bronze naval cannon mounted on truck carriages for rapid reloading.
• Battle of Gravelines (1588): Decisive naval clash off Flanders where English artillery battered scattered Spanish warships.
2. Essential Chronology
• 1569: Revolt of the Northern Earls
• 1570: Papal Bull excommunicates Queen
• 1571: Ridolfi Plot uncovered
• 1577–80: Drake circumnavigates world
• 1583: Throckmorton Plot uncovered
• 1584: Treaty of Joinville (Spain/France)
• 1585: Treaty of Nonsuch signed
• 1586: Babington Plot ciphers cracked
• Feb 1587: Mary Stuart executed
• July–Aug 1588: Spanish Armada defeated
3. Key Historical Figures Matrix
• Sir Francis Walsingham: Principal Secretary & Spymaster General; uncovered Ridolfi, Throckmorton, and Babington plots.
• Sir Francis Drake: Privateer, navigator, and vice-admiral; raided Cadiz in 1587 and commanded at Gravelines.
• Duke of Medina Sidonia: Spanish grandee appointed by Philip II to command the 130 ships of the Armada.
• Duke of Parma: Brilliant general commanding Spain’s 27,000-man veteran Army of Flanders.
• Anthony Babington: Catholic gentleman whose coded letters to Mary Stuart triggered her trial and execution.
• Sir John Hawkins: Treasurer of the Navy; designed English race-built galleons and developed naval gunnery.
