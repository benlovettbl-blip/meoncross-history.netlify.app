1572 • The Vagabonds Act Key Topic 3.2

Parliament passes the 1572 Vagabonds Act, creating a legal distinction between the 'deserving poor' (elderly and sick) and 'idle beggars'. Vagrants over 14 are whipped and burned through the ear; repeat offenders face execution.

1576 • Act for Relief of the Poor & Burbage Builds 'The Theatre' Key Topic 3.1

The 1576 Poor Act orders town councils to provide wool and raw materials to put the unemployed to work, creating 'Houses of Correction' (Bridewells). In the same year, actor James Burbage constructs 'The Theatre' in Shoreditch, launching Elizabethan public drama.

DEC 1577 • Drake Departs on Global Circumnavigation Key Topic 3.3

Sir Francis Drake departs Plymouth with five ships on the *Pelican* (*Golden Hind*). Backed secretly by Elizabeth and investors, his mission is to disrupt Spanish Pacific trade, discover trade routes to the Moluccas, and claim land for the English Crown.

SEPT 1580 • Drake Returns to Deptford; Knighted by Elizabeth Key Topic 3.3

Drake sails the *Golden Hind* into Plymouth Harbour after 33 months, becoming the first Englishman to circumnavigate the globe. His cargo of Spanish bullion yields a 4,700% return for royal investors, and Elizabeth knights him on board in April 1581.

1584–1585 • Raleigh’s Patent & The First Roanoke Colony Key Topic 3.4

Queen Elizabeth grants Sir Walter Raleigh a royal patent to explore and colonise Virginia. In 1585, 107 male colonists sail under Sir Richard Grenville and Ralph Lane to Roanoke Island, seeking gold, privateering bases, and fertile agricultural land.

1587–1590 • The Second Roanoke Colony ("The Lost Colony") Key Topic 3.4

John White leads 118 settlers, including women and children, to establish a permanent plantation. When White returns in 1590 after being delayed by the Armada, the entire colony has vanished without a trace, leaving only the word "CROATOAN" carved on a post.

KEY TOPIC 3.1 • ENQUIRY LESSON NOTEBOOK EDEXCEL PAPER 2 (1HI0/2B) • BRITISH DEPTH STUDY

Was Elizabethan society truly entering a "Golden Age" of culture and learning?

CHRONOLOGICAL INQUIRY SPINE • 5 CAUSAL MILESTONES PUPIL NOTE-TAKING ZONE • Answer each milestone prompt using evidence from teacher exposition
1 1558–1580s
Attitudes to Education & Literacy
• Education: fee-paying, private, and designed to reinforce the established social hierarchy.
• Literacy rates: approximately 15–20% of men and under 10% of women could read and write.
• Petty schools and dame schools taught basic reading, writing, and religion to younger children.
Why did Elizabethan attitudes towards social hierarchy limit educational opportunity?
2 1558–1580s
Grammar Schools
• Over 70 new grammar schools established for boys aged 7–14 from middling classes.
• Curriculum: intensive study of Latin grammar, Greek, classical literature, and rhetoric.
• 10-hour school days with strict discipline enforced by corporal punishment (birch rod).
How did grammar schools provide a route for sons of the gentry and merchants to rise in society?
3 1558–1580s
Universities & Noble Education
• Oxford and Cambridge: curriculum centered on philosophy, rhetoric, law, and theology.
• Noble boys: private home tutors teaching French, Latin, fencing, horsemanship, and dancing.
• Noble girls: private home education in music, needlework, French, and estate management.
How did the education of the nobility differ from schooling provided to the middling classes?
4 1558–1580s
Sports and Pastimes by Social Class
• Nobility and gentry: hunting, hawking, fencing, real tennis, and tournament jousting.
• Lower orders: folk football, archery, cudgel-fighting, wrestling, and tavern dice games.
• Blood sports enjoyed across all social classes: bear-baiting, bull-baiting, and cock-fighting.
How did leisure pursuits reflect both deep social divisions and shared national culture?
5 1576–1588
Development of the Theatre
• 1576: James Burbage builds ‘The Theatre’ outside the walls of the City of London.
• Purpose-built playhouses (The Curtain, The Rose, The Swan) built in Southwark and Shoreditch.
• Groundlings paid 1 penny to stand in open pit; wealthy patrons paid for roofed gallery seats.
Why did the theatre attract enormous popularity while provoking intense Puritan opposition?
4/24 "The Theatre (1576): The first permanent playhouse in London, built outside the city walls in Shoreditch."

KEY TOPIC 3.2 • ENQUIRY LESSON NOTEBOOK EDEXCEL PAPER 2 (1HI0/2B) • BRITISH DEPTH STUDY

Was Elizabethan poverty caused by personal idleness or unstoppable economic forces?

CHRONOLOGICAL INQUIRY SPINE • 5 CAUSAL MILESTONES PUPIL NOTE-TAKING ZONE • Answer each milestone prompt using evidence from teacher exposition
1 1558–1580s
Population Growth & Rising Prices
• England’s population grew by 35%, from under 3 million in 1558 to over 4 million by 1603.
• Successive harvest failures in the 1570s and 1590s led to food shortages and grain price spikes.
• Inflation: food prices increased rapidly while wages for agricultural laborers fell.
How did the relationship between population growth and food supply generate severe poverty?
2 1558–1580s
Changes in Farming: Enclosure & Sheep
• Enclosure: replacing open fields and common land with hedged fields for sheep farming.
• Cloth trade: raw wool and finished cloth accounted for over 80% of English export value.
• Sheep farming required significantly fewer farm hands than traditional arable crop farming.
Why did the expansion of sheep farming cause widespread rural unemployment and eviction?
3 1558–1570s
Changing Attitudes to the Poor
• Impotent poor: elderly, sick, disabled, and orphans physically unable to work.
• Idle poor (sturdy beggars): able-bodied individuals viewed as dishonest and dangerous.
• Pamphlets like Thomas Harman’s Caveat for Common Cursitors warning of beggar tricksters.
Why did Elizabethan authorities draw such a sharp distinction between the ‘deserving’ and ‘idle’ poor?
4 1572
The 1572 Vagabonds Act
• Compulsory poor rate: local property tax levied on parish households to fund poor relief.
• Overseers of the Poor: parish officials appointed to collect rates and distribute relief.
• Penalties for vagabonds: public whipping and a hole burned through right ear with a hot iron.
How did the 1572 Act combine harsh criminal punishment with the first compulsory tax for relief?
5 1576
The 1576 Act for Relief of the Poor
• Towns required to provide stocks of raw materials (wool, hemp, flax, iron) for unemployed.
• Houses of Correction (Bridewells) built in counties to compel persistent vagrants to hard labor.
• Laid legislative foundation for the comprehensive Elizabethan Poor Law of 1601.
Why did the 1576 Act represent a major shift in the state’s responsibility for unemployment?
8/24 "Poverty grew because population rose from 3m to 4m, while wool enclosure eliminated farm jobs."

Was English maritime exploration driven by scientific curiosity or ruthless economic greed?

CHRONOLOGICAL INQUIRY SPINE • 5 CAUSAL MILESTONES PUPIL NOTE-TAKING ZONE • Answer each milestone prompt using evidence from teacher exposition
1 1558–1575
New Navigational Technology
• Astrolabes and quadrants used by navigators to calculate latitude from sun and stars.
• Magnetic compasses, sea charts, and log-and-line used to estimate dead reckoning and speed.
• 1569: Gerardus Mercator publishes world map projection with parallel lines of longitude and latitude.
How did advances in navigational science make oceanic exploration feasible for English captains?
2 1558–1577
Improvements in Ship Design
• English ocean galleons: larger hull capacity, lower forecastles, and greater stability in rough seas.
• Sail design: combined traditional square sails for speed with triangular lateen sails for tacking.
• Naval armament: heavy culverin cannons mounted low down on compact four-wheeled carriages.
How did changes in galleon design and gun placement give English sailors an advantage at sea?
3 1550s–1570s
The Search for New Trade Routes
• Collapse of traditional wool trade through Antwerp in the 1550s.
• Joint-stock companies: Muscovy Company (1555), Eastland Company (1579), Levant Company (1581).
• Search for a Northwest Passage to China led by Martin Frobisher and John Davis.
Why did English merchants establish joint-stock companies to explore new global trade routes?
4 1577–1580
Drake’s Circumnavigation
• Dec 1577: Drake departs Plymouth aboard Pelican with 164 crew and five vessels.
• Navigates treacherous Strait of Magellan; lone surviving ship Golden Hind enters Pacific.
• Raids Spanish settlements along Peru and Chile; lands in California (Nova Albion); visits Ternate.
Why was Drake’s voyage through the Strait of Magellan and across the Pacific an extraordinary achievement?
5 1580–1588
Significance of Drake’s Voyage
• Sept 1580: Drake returns to Plymouth with £140,000 in silver, gold, and spice cargo.
• April 1581: Elizabeth knights Drake on board Golden Hind in presence of French ambassadors.
• English navigational charts and firsthand logs open up Atlantic and Asian trade horizons.
How did the success of Drake’s circumnavigation alter England’s position on the world stage?
12/24 "New navigation tech: The astrolabe measured stars, but Drake's daring navigated the globe."

Cultural & Global Blueprint • The Elizabethan Playhouse & Voyages of Discovery

1. Anatomy of an Elizabethan Playhouse: The Globe & The Swan (Bankside)
① The Heavens & Roof: Painted canopy ceiling over the stage supported by wooden pillars; housed winches, ropes, and cannons for special effects.
② The Yard / Pit: Unroofed earthen standing area surrounding the thrust stage; held up to 1,000 "groundlings" who paid 1 penny to watch in all weathers.
③ Tiered Galleries & Lords: Three tiers of covered wooden seating (2–3 pence); exclusive Lords’ Rooms above the stage cost 6 pence for aristocratic display.
2. Elizabethan Global Exploration: Drake’s Circumnavigation & The Virginia Colonies
🧭 Drake’s Global Track (1577–1580) • Route: Departed Plymouth → Cape Verde → Brazil → Strait of Magellan (Cape Horn) → Valparaíso → Lima → California (Nova Albion) → Pacific Crossing → Moluccas (Ternate) → Cape of Good Hope → Plymouth.
• Achievements: First English circumnavigation, claimed California for Elizabeth, broke Spanish Pacific exclusivity, secured clove treaty with Sultan of Ternate, returned with £140,000 in silver bullion.
🌲 Raleigh’s Virginia Colonisation (1585–1587) • 1585 First Colony: Ralph Lane and 107 soldiers settle Roanoke Island. Flagship *Tiger* floods seeds; settlers fail to farm, rely on Secotan handouts, alienate Chief Wingina, and evacuate with Drake in 1586.
• 1587 Second Colony ("Lost Colony"): John White leads 118 civilian men, women, and children. White returns in 1590 after Armada delays; colony has vanished, leaving only "CROATOAN".

Master Knowledge Organiser • Key Topic 3: Elizabethan Society in the Age of Exploration, 1558–88

1. Core Disciplinary Vocabulary & Conceptual Definitions
• Grammar School: Fee-paying secondary school for boys aged 7–14; over 70 established under Elizabeth.
• Petty School: Early elementary school for young boys run in a teacher’s home, teaching basic reading and writing.
• Enclosure: Fencing common arable land into private pastures for sheep farming, dispossessing farm labourers.
• Impotent Poor: The elderly, sick, and disabled unable to work; categorized as "deserving" of parish relief.
• Sturdy Beggars: Able-bodied vagrants categorized as "idle"; punished by whipping and forced labour.
• Houses of Correction: Bridewell workhouses created by the 1576 Poor Act to punish idle vagrants with hard labour.
• Astrolabe: Navigation tool measuring the altitude of sun and stars to calculate latitude at sea.
• Colony: A new overseas settlement established, populated, and governed by people from another country.
2. Essential Chronology
• 1572: Vagabonds Act passed
• 1576: Act for Relief of the Poor
• 1576: Burbage builds 'The Theatre'
• 1577: Drake sails on *Golden Hind*
• 1580: Drake returns to Deptford
• 1584: Raleigh receives Virginia patent
• 1585: First Roanoke colony founded
• 1586: Drake rescues Roanoke settlers
• 1587: Second Roanoke settlement
• 1590: White finds "CROATOAN" post
3. Key Historical Figures Matrix
• Sir Walter Raleigh: Courtier, poet, and explorer; organized and financed the Virginia colonization expeditions.
• James Burbage: Actor and builder; constructed 'The Theatre' in 1576, London’s first public playhouse.
• William Shakespeare: Playwright and actor; joined the Lord Chamberlain’s Men; shaped Elizabethan culture.
• Ralph Lane: Governor of the 1585 Roanoke colony; military officer whose brutality alienated native tribes.
• John White: Artist and governor of the 1587 "Lost Colony"; painted watercolors of Native American life.
• Chief Wingina: Secotan tribal chief on Roanoke Island; initially aided English, killed by Lane in 1586.
