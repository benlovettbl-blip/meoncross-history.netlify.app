Master Chronology Domino Flowchart

1871–1914 • 11 Anchor Flashpoints from German Unification to Continental War

Domino Flowchart
1871 German Empire Proclaimed: Unified in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles; Alsace-Lorraine annexed from France, sparking lifelong French Revanche.
1882 Triple Alliance Formed: Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy create a central defensive military pact.
1890 Bismarck Dismissed: Kaiser Wilhelm II rejects defensive diplomacy, cancels the Reinsurance Treaty, and launches aggressive Weltpolitik.
1894 Franco-Russian Alliance: France and Russia unite militarily, trapping Germany between two rival powers (the two-front encirclement nightmare).
1898–1900 Tirpitz Naval Laws: Germany begins building the High Seas Fleet to challenge the British Royal Navy's supremacy.
1904 Entente Cordiale Signed: Britain and France settle colonial disputes (Egypt & Morocco) and begin joint defensive military discussions.
1905–1906 First Moroccan Crisis: Wilhelm II lands at Tangier to test the Entente; the Algeciras Conference (1906) isolates Germany and binds Britain to France.
1906 HMS Dreadnought Launched: Revolutionary British all-big-gun battleship resets naval technology and intensifies the Anglo-German arms race.
1907 Triple Entente Completed: The Anglo-Russian Convention settles Asian colonial rivalries, uniting Britain, France, and Russia.
1908 The Bosnian Crisis: Austria-Hungary annexes Bosnia, permanently alienating Serbia and humiliating Tsarist Russia.
28 Jun – 4 Aug 1914 The July Crisis & War: Franz Ferdinand assassinated in Sarajevo → German Blank Cheque → Austrian Ultimatum → Russian mobilisation → Schlieffen Plan → World War.

Key Historical Figures & Academic Vocabulary

The 7 Protagonists • Pronunciation Guide • Core Terminology

People & Terms
The Seven Crucial Protagonists (1871–1914)
Otto von Bismarck • Imperial Chancellor of Germany (1871–1890)
Engineered German unification through "Blood and Iron"; constructed the Triple Alliance and Reinsurance Treaty to isolate France and prevent a two-front war.
Kaiser Wilhelm II • German Emperor (1888–1918)
Impulsive autocrat; dismissed Bismarck (1890), dropped the Russian treaty, championed aggressive Weltpolitik, and gave Austria the fatal Blank Cheque (1914).
Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz • State Secretary of the German Imperial Navy
Authored the German Naval Laws (1898/1900) and "Risk Theory"; directed High Seas Fleet construction that triggered the naval arms race with Great Britain.
Archduke Franz Ferdinand • Heir to the Austro-Hungarian Throne
Targeted by Serbian extremists for his reformist views; his assassination on 28 June 1914 in Sarajevo was the catalytic spark for the outbreak of war.
Gavrilo Princip • Serbian Nationalist Assassin (Young Bosnia / Black Hand)
19-year-old student operative who fired the fatal shots in Sarajevo to break the South Slav provinces away from Austria-Hungary into a Greater Serbia.
Sir Edward Grey • British Foreign Secretary (1905–1916)
Attempted international mediation during the July Crisis; delivered the British ultimatum to Germany to defend Belgian neutrality under the 1839 Treaty of London.
Helmuth von Moltke (the Younger) • Chief of the German General Staff
Executed the Schlieffen Plan; argued that Russian mobilisation meant Germany had to launch an immediate pre-emptive invasion of France via Belgium.
Academic Vocabulary with Spoken Pronunciation Guide
Weltpolitik [Velt-pol-ee-teek] (German)
"World Policy." Kaiser Wilhelm II's assertive foreign policy seeking global colonies, international prestige, and naval supremacy matching Britain.
Revanche [Ruh-vahnsh] (French)
"Revenge." The profound, enduring French national desire to avenge defeat in the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71) and reclaim Alsace-Lorraine.
Realpolitik [Ray-ahl-pol-ee-teek] (German)
Pragmatic, hard-nosed statecraft based on practical power and national self-interest rather than moral or ideological ideals.
HMS Dreadnought [Dred-nawt] (English)
Revolutionary 1906 British battleship with all-big-gun armament and steam turbines, rendering all previous battleships instantly obsolete.
Entente Cordiale [Ahn-tahnt Kor-dyahl] (French)
"Cordial Understanding." 1904 diplomatic accord resolving colonial disputes between Britain and France, paving the way for joint military talks.
Blank Cheque (Blankoscheck) [Blahnk-oh-sheck] (German)
Germany's unconditional pledge of military backing given to Austria-Hungary on 5–6 July 1914, emboldening Vienna to declare war on Serbia.
Mobilisation [Moh-bi-ly-zay-shun] (Military)
The complex, timetable-driven movement of reserves, troops, and supplies to frontiers via railways; once ordered, it made war unstoppable.
The Powder Keg of Europe (Metaphor)
The volatile Balkan peninsula where competing ethnic Slavic nationalisms and Austro-Russian imperial rivalries threatened continental peace.

Summative Assessment Preparation & Essay Architect

Key Stage 3 Unit Assessment • Extended Analytical Writing Framework

Essay Blueprint
Summative Assessment Enquiry Question:
“Why did the First World War break out in August 1914?”
1. The M.A.I.N. Structural Causes & The July Spark
Militarism: Anglo-German dreadnought naval race; massive army expansions; rigid railway mobilisation timetables (Schlieffen Plan).
Alliances: Triple Alliance (Ger, Aus, Ita) vs. Triple Entente (Bri, Fra, Rus); transformed a regional Balkan clash into a world war.
Imperialism: Scramble for Africa; Moroccan Crises (1905, 1911) cemented Anglo-French entente and isolated Germany.
Nationalism: French Revanche for Alsace-Lorraine; Pan-Slavism & Serbian nationalism in the Balkans (The Black Hand).
The Catalyst (The July Crisis): Sarajevo assassination (28 June) → German Blank Cheque (5 July) → Austrian Ultimatum (23 July) → Russian mobilisation (30 July) → German invasion of neutral Belgium (4 August).
2. Historiographical Verdict: Competing Interpretations
Fritz Fischer Thesis (German Aggression):
Germany's imperial leaders deliberately exploited the Sarajevo crisis, issuing the Blank Cheque to launch a pre-emptive war for European continental hegemony.
Christopher Clark Thesis ('The Sleepwalkers'):
No single power sought a world war. European leaders sleepwalked into catastrophe through mutual paranoia, miscalculation, and rigid alliance treaties.
3. Master Analytical Sentence Starters & Causal Connectives
• “On one hand, orthodox historians argue that German aggressive foreign policy...”
• “This directly escalated tensions because Wilhelm II's pursuit of Weltpolitik...”
• “Consequently, the Sarajevo assassination acted as the fatal catalyst because...”
• “However, revisionist historians like Christopher Clark emphasize that the Alliance System...”
• “In conclusion, while M.A.I.N. created the combustible structural conditions, the decisive spark was...”
