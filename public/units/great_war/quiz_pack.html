4. The Anglo-German Naval Arms Race & HMS Dreadnought
25.
HMS Dreadnought — Dreadnought featured ten 12-inch guns and turbine propulsion, making every existing battleship on the planet instantly obsolete.

[✓][✗]

The Two-Power Standard — Established by the Naval Defence Act 1889, Britain viewed naval supremacy as strictly vital for its food supply and empire.
[✓][✗]

To build a navy so strong that Britain would not risk a battle for fear of crippling itself — Tirpitz believed this would force Britain to make diplomatic concessions and grant Germany colonial territory.
[✓][✗]
29.
"We want eight and we won't wait!" — Public panic over German shipyard expansion forced the British government to double dreadnought production from 4 to 8 ships.
[✓][✗]

Because Britain was an island dependent on oceanic food imports, while Germany was already a land superpower — Winston Churchill described the German fleet as an aggressive 'luxury', while the Royal Navy was a defensive necessity.
[✓][✗]
31.
Britain (29 Dreadnoughts to Germany's 17) — Britain's overwhelming industrial capacity, financial wealth, and political consensus decisively outbuilt the German Empire.
[✓][✗]
32.
It permanently ended Britain's 'Splendid Isolation' and bound it to France and Russia — Realising it could not defend its empire alone against rising German power, Britain settled its historic rivalries.

33.
Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy — A mutual defensive pact promising assistance if attacked by France or other powers, though Italy remained a reluctant partner.
[✓][✗]
34.
Britain, France, and Russia — Formed through the 1894 Franco-Russian Alliance, 1904 Entente Cordiale, and 1907 Anglo-Russian Convention.
[✓][✗]
35.
Splendid Isolation — Britain focused on its worldwide empire and maintaining the naval balance of power without entangling continental treaties.
[✓][✗]
36.
The Entente Cordiale — It resolved colonial disputes (Britain in Egypt, France in Morocco) and paved the way for close diplomatic cooperation.
[✓][✗]
37.
The Anglo-Russian Convention — It ended the century-long 'Great Game' of imperial competition in Asia, clearing the path for joint European alignment.
[✓][✗]
38.
Italy held deep territorial rivalries with its alliance partner, Austria-Hungary — Italy coveted Italia Irredenta (Trentino and Trieste) ruled by Vienna, leading Italy to declare neutrality in 1914 and switch sides in 1915.
[✓][✗]
39.
A localized regional quarrel could instantly trigger a continent-wide chain reaction — Pledges of mutual defence meant empires felt compelled to mobilise armies to protect partners, converting a Balkan crisis into World War.
[✓][✗]
40.
The collapse of the Ottoman Empire created intense Slavic nationalism and imperial rivalries — Austria-Hungary and Russia both competed to dominate newly independent Balkan states like Serbia, creating constant crises.

41.

[✓][✗]

Gavrilo Princip — Princip was a member of Young Bosnia, a revolutionary student movement seeking to unite South Slavs into a Greater Serbia.
[✓][✗]
43.
The Black Hand (Union or Death) — Led by Colonel Dragutin Dimitrijević ('Apis'), head of Serbian military intelligence, who smuggled weapons and bombs across the border.
[✓][✗]
44.
Bosnia contained a large ethnic Serb population that Serbia wanted to incorporate into a Greater Serbian state — The 1908 Bosnian Crisis humiliated Serbia and its protector Russia, establishing a bitter blood-feud with Vienna.
[✓][✗]
45.
Germany's unconditional promise of total military support to Austria-Hungary against Serbia — Kaiser Wilhelm II and Chancellor Bethmann-Hollweg encouraged Vienna to strike quickly, gambling that Russia would back down.
[✓][✗]

[✓][✗]

The Schlieffen Plan — Conceived by Count von Schlieffen, it planned to conquer France in 6 weeks via neutral Belgium before turning east to confront Russia.
[✓][✗]

Master Chronology Domino Flowchart

1871–1914 • The 11 Anchor Flashpoints of the Outbreak of War

Timeline
1871 German Empire Proclaimed: Unified in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles; Alsace-Lorraine annexed from France, sparking Revanche.
1882 Triple Alliance Formed: Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy create a central defensive bloc.
1890 Bismarck Dismissed: Kaiser Wilhelm II rejects defensive diplomacy, cancels the Reinsurance Treaty, and launches Weltpolitik.
1894 Franco-Russian Alliance: France and Russia unite militarily, trapping Germany between two rival powers (Encirclement).
1904 Entente Cordiale Signed: Britain and France settle colonial disputes and begin joint defensive talks.
1905 First Moroccan Crisis: Wilhelm II challenges French control in Tangier; Algeciras Conference (1906) isolates Germany.
1906 HMS Dreadnought Launched: Britain resets naval technology; sparks rapid arms race with Tirpitz's High Seas Fleet.
1907 Triple Entente Formed: Anglo-Russian Convention unites Britain, France, and Russia.
1908 Bosnian Crisis: Austria-Hungary annexes Bosnia, permanently alienating Serbia and Russia.
28 June 1914 Sarajevo Assassination: Archduke Franz Ferdinand murdered by Gavrilo Princip (Black Hand); sparks July Crisis.
4 August 1914 Outbreak of World War: Germany enacts the Schlieffen Plan via neutral Belgium; Britain declares war.

Academic Vocabulary & Phonetic Guide

Essential Historical Terminology • Pronunciation & Core Definitions

Glossary
Weltpolitik [Velt-pol-ee-teek] (German)
"World Policy." Kaiser Wilhelm II's aggressive foreign policy to transform Germany into a global superpower with an overseas empire and massive battlefleet.
Revanche [Ruh-vahnsh] (French)
"Revenge." The profound, enduring French national desire to avenge their humiliating defeat in the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71) and reclaim Alsace-Lorraine.
Einkreisung (Encirclement) [Ine-kry-zoong] (German)
The persistent German strategic fear of being geographically surrounded and overwhelmed by a hostile Franco-Russian alliance on two fronts.
HMS Dreadnought [Dred-nawt] (English)
The revolutionary 1906 British battleship that rendered all older warships obsolete overnight and triggered a massive naval arms race with Germany.
Blank Cheque (Blankoscheck) [Blahnk-oh-sheck] (German)
Germany's unconditional pledge of military backing issued to Austria-Hungary on 5–6 July 1914, emboldening Vienna to issue its crushing ultimatum to Serbia.
Schlieffen Plan [Shlee-fen Plan] (German)
German war plan to rapidly invade France through neutral Belgium, capture Paris in 6 weeks, then transfer troops by rail to defeat Russia.
Mobilisation [Moh-bi-ly-zay-shun] (Military)
The complex, timetable-driven railway movement of millions of reservist troops and supplies to frontiers; once ordered, it made war virtually unstoppable.
The Powder Keg of Europe (Metaphor)
The volatile Balkan peninsula where competing ethnic Slavic nationalisms and Austro-Russian imperial ambitions threatened to explode at any moment.
