ACT I: ROMAN BRITAIN • 4th CENTURY YORK
The Ivory Bangle Lady: High Status in Roman Britain
Delivered by: Pupil 1
The Discovery (York, 1901)
Unearthed in a stone sarcophagus in York dating to c. 350 AD, containing the skeleton of a wealthy high-status woman.
Expensive Grave Goods
Buried with luxury goods: an elephant ivory armlet with rope pattern, jet bracelets, glass perfume flasks, and fine jewellery.
Forensic & Isotope Analysis
Forensic osteoarchaeology and dental isotope testing confirm she was of North African descent, proving Britain was multicultural long before it was called Great Britain.
HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
"She wasn’t an enslaved person or an outsider; she was a wealthy, high-status member of Roman British society."
York Museums Trust: Elephant ivory armlet recovered from the 4th-century York sarcophagus.
ACT II: TUDOR ENGLAND • THE ROYAL COURT
John Blanke: Musician at the Heart of the Royal Court
Delivered by: Pupil 2
Regular Royal Trumpeter
Served as a retained musician in the royal courts of both King Henry VII and King Henry VIII, performing at major state ceremonies.
Westminster Tournament Roll (1511)
Painted twice on the illuminated 60-foot royal vellum roll celebrating the birth of Prince Henry, riding on horseback in royal Tudor livery.
The Royal Pay Petition
Court records preserve his handwritten petition directly to King Henry VIII requesting a pay rise to eight pence a day—which the King approved.
HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
"John Blanke was not an onlooker—he was a respected, skilled professional at the very centre of royal court ceremony."
The College of Arms: John Blanke on the 1511 Westminster Tournament Roll.
ACT III: EARLY 20th CENTURY • CIVIL RIGHTS
Dr. Harold Moody: Medical Pioneer & Campaigner
Delivered by: Pupil 3
Academic Excellence & Hospital Bans
Born in Jamaica, studied medicine at King’s College London (1904), graduating top of his class. Hospital posts were denied due to colour bars.
Community Doctor in Peckham (1913)
Opened his own private GP surgery in Peckham, South London, in 1913. Beloved locally for treating impoverished families free of charge.
League of Coloured Peoples (1931)
Founded Britain's first major civil rights organization to challenge discriminatory housing bans, workplace exclusion, and armed forces color bars.
HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
"Dr. Moody campaigned tirelessly for decades, laying the direct groundwork for Britain’s modern race relations laws."
National Portrait Gallery: Dr Harold Moody (1882–1947), Bronze portrait by Ronald Moody.
ACT IV: POST-WAR BRITAIN • REBUILDING THE NATION
The Windrush Generation: Building the Modern Nation
Delivered by: Pupil 4
The Call to Rebuild (1948)
Facing post-war devastation and severe labour shortages, the British government invited Commonwealth citizens to rebuild cities, transport, and industry.
Backbone of the NHS
Arriving in the same month the NHS was founded (July 1948), Caribbean men and women became the vital nurses, doctors, and staff powering our hospitals.
Vibrant Cultural Contribution
Enriched British music, literature, cuisine, and founded enduring global cultural institutions such as London's Notting Hill Carnival.
HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
"They became the engine room of modern Britain, shaping the public services we all depend on every single day."
Imperial War Museum: HMT Empire Windrush arriving at Tilbury Docks, 22 June 1948.
