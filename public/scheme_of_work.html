Paper 1: Medicine Through Time with the Western Front

Year 11 | How has medicine changed from c1250 to the modern day?

Lesson Number & Title Learning Objectives Disciplinary Focus Core Assessment & Tasks
Lesson 1
KT1.1: What did Medieval people believe caused illness?
  • Explain how supernatural and religious factors influenced beliefs about the cause of illness.
  • Analyse the rational, natural theories of disease used in medieval England.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (12 marks): Explain why there was so little change in ideas about the cause of disease in the years c.1250-c.1500.
Lesson 2
KT1.2: How did Medieval people try to prevent and treat disease?
  • Explain the connection between medieval beliefs about disease and their approaches to treatment and prevention.
  • Evaluate the roles, training, and accessibility of different medical healers in medieval society.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (12 marks): Explain why there was little change in the care provided by hospitals in the period c.1250-c.1500.
Lesson 3
KT1.3: How did people respond to the Black Death?
  • Explain the religious and supernatural responses to the Black Death.
  • Analyse the rational and governmental methods used to prevent and treat the plague.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (16 marks): 'The main reason why medical care and treatment was ineffective during the medieval period (c1250-c1500) was because medical knowledge was based on Galen's ideas.' How far do you agree? Explain your answer. (16 marks + 4 marks for SPaG)
Lesson 4
KT2.1: Did the Renaissance change beliefs about the causes of illness?
  • Explain how Thomas Sydenham's scientific approach to observation changed the diagnosis of disease.
  • Assess the role of the printing press and the Royal Society in communicating and spreading scientific ideas.
 Causation
  • Exam Practice (12 marks): Explain why there were changes in the way ideas about the causes of disease and illness were communicated in the period c1500-c1700. (12 marks)
Lesson 5
KT2.2: Did treatments improve during the Renaissance?
  • Analyze the continuities and changes in Renaissance treatments and preventions, including the impact of exploration and alchemy.
  • Explain how institutional changes, specifically the Dissolution of the Monasteries, altered hospital care in England.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (12 marks): Explain why there was rapid progress in anatomical knowledge in the Renaissance period (c1500-c1700). (12 marks)
Lesson 6
KT2.3: How significant were William Harvey and the Great Plague?
  • Explain the significance of William Harvey's discovery and the reasons for its limited short-term impact.
  • Compare the government and public responses to the 1665 Great Plague with the 1348 Black Death.
 Significance
  • Exam Practice (16 marks): 'The most effective method of preventing the spread of the Great Plague (1665) was the use of quarantine.' How far do you agree? Explain your answer. (16 marks + 4 marks for SPaG)
Lesson 7
KT3.1: What breakthrough discoveries changed our understanding of disease causes?
  • Explain the difference between Spontaneous Generation and Germ Theory.
  • Analyze the significance of Robert Koch's scientific methodology in microbiology.
 Causation
  • Exam Practice (16 marks): 'Louis Pasteur's publication of the Germ Theory was the biggest turning point in understanding the causes of disease.' How far do you agree? Explain your answer.
Lesson 8
KT3.2: How did the Industrial Revolution transform prevention and treatment?
  • Explain how hospital care and nursing standards were transformed c1700-c1900 under the influence of Florence Nightingale.
  • Analyse how the breakthroughs of anaesthetics and antiseptics solved the critical surgical problems of pain and infection.
  • Evaluate the role of government intervention in disease prevention through the 1875 Public Health Act.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (16 marks): 'The discovery of Carbolic Acid was the most significant turning point in surgery in the years c1700-c1900.' How far do you agree? Explain your answer. (16 marks + 4 marks for SPaG)
Lesson 9
KT3.3: How significant were Edward Jenner and John Snow?
  • Explain the significance and limitations of Edward Jenner's development of the smallpox vaccine.
  • Analyze how John Snow investigated the 1854 cholera epidemic and assess the significance of his findings.
 Significance
  • Exam Practice (12 marks): Explain why there was rapid progress in public health in the second half of the nineteenth century (c1850-c1900). (12 marks)
Lesson 10
KT4.1: How have modern discoveries changed our understanding of illness?
  • Explain how the discovery of DNA and the Human Genome Project transformed the understanding of genetic and hereditary illnesses.
  • Analyze the influence of lifestyle choices on modern health and the impact of technology on medical diagnosis.
 Change and Continuity
  • Exam Practice (16 marks): 'The discovery of DNA was the most significant breakthrough in understanding the causes of illness in the period c.1900-present.' How far do you agree? Explain your answer.
Lesson 11
KT4.2: How have prevention and treatment advanced in the modern era?
  • Explain how scientific and technological developments led to the creation of magic bullets and antibiotics.
  • Analyze the impact of the National Health Service (NHS) on the provision of and access to medical care.
 Change and Continuity
  • Exam Practice (16 marks): 'Alexander Fleming was the most important individual in the development of penicillin.' How far do you agree? Explain your answer. (16 marks + 4 marks for SPaG)
Lesson 12
KT4.3: How was Penicillin discovered and mass-produced?
  • Contrast the roles of Alexander Fleming with those of Howard Florey and Ernst Chain in the penicillin breakthrough.
  • Analyze how the factors of government intervention, science and technology, and war collaborated to enable the mass production of penicillin.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (12 marks): Explain why there was rapid progress in disease prevention in the period c1900-present. (12 marks)
Lesson 13
KT4.4: How has the government tackled the modern epidemic of Lung Cancer?
  • Explain how modern science and technology have transformed the diagnosis and treatment of lung cancer since c1900.
  • Analyze the effectiveness and changing focus of government legislation to prevent smoking-related lung cancer.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (12 marks): Explain why the government took action to improve public health in the period c1900-present. (12 marks)
Lesson 14
KT5.1: What was the historical and medical context of the Western Front?
  • Explain the state of surgical asepsis, radiology, and transfusion science on the eve of World War I.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of the support trench system on the Western Front. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of the problems involved in transporting wounded soldiers away from the battleground. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (8 marks): How useful are Sources A and B for an enquiry into the difficulties faced by soldiers in the trench system of the Western Front? Explain your answer, using Sources A and B and your knowledge of the historical context.
  • Exam Practice (4 marks): How could you follow up Source A to find out more about the work of the stretcher bearers on the Western Front?
Lesson 15
KT5.2: How did the trench environment create new medical challenges?
  • Explain the causes, symptoms, and preventive strategies developed to combat trench foot and trench fever.
  • Analyze the impact of weaponry on injuries, specifically focusing on shrapnel, head injuries, and gas gangrene from soil.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of poison gas attacks on the Western Front. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of the fighting on the Western Front that led to a high number of injuries. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (8 marks): How useful are Sources A and B for an enquiry into the problem of ill health arising from the trench environment? Explain your answer, using Sources A and B and your knowledge of the historical context.
  • Exam Practice (4 marks): How could you follow up Source A to find out more about the problem of trench foot?
Lesson 16
KT5.3: What new illnesses and wounds did soldiers face?
  • Explain the connection between artillery weaponry, shrapnel, and the introduction of the steel Brodie helmet in 1915.
  • Analyse how the muddy, manured environment of Flanders led to severe wound infections like gas gangrene and tetanus.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of aseptic surgery in the early 20th century. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of early x-ray machines. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (4 marks): How could you follow up Source A to find out more about the treatment and prevention of the effects of gas attacks?
Lesson 17
KT5.4: How did the RAMC and FANY operate the chain of evacuation?
  • Explain the four main stages of the Western Front chain of evacuation and the mode of transport between them.
  • Evaluate the roles, significance, and contributions of the RAMC and FANY on the Western Front.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of the underground hospital at Arras. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of the work of the FANY on the Western Front. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (8 marks): How useful are Sources A and B for an enquiry into the work of medical staff in the Casualty Clearing Stations (CCS) on the Western Front? Explain your answer, using Sources A and B and your knowledge of the historical context.
  • Exam Practice (4 marks): How could you follow up Source A to find out more about the work of medical staff in the Casualty Clearing Stations?
Lesson 18
KT5.5: What incredible medical advances were forged on the Western Front?
  • Explain how the Thomas splint and mobile X-ray units solved critical evacuation and surgical challenges.
  • Analyze the development of blood transfusion and the significance of the first blood bank at the Battle of Cambrai.
 Historical Enquiry
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of the Thomas Splint. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (2 marks): Describe one feature of the medical advances in blood transfusions on the Western Front. (2 marks)
  • Exam Practice (8 marks): How useful are Sources A and B for an enquiry into the effectiveness of new medical developments used to treat wounded soldiers on the Western Front? Explain your answer, using Sources A and B and your knowledge of the historical context.
  • Exam Practice (4 marks): How could you follow up Source A to find out more about new techniques being used on the Western Front to deal with injuries?
